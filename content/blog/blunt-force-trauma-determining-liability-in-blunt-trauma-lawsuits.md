A California injury claim must connect the defendant’s conduct to the injury and the resulting losses. The medical cause of an injury and legal fault are separate questions. Understanding that difference helps explain what evidence matters, who may be responsible, and why compensation is not automatic.

## Track the impact, symptoms, and treatment on one timeline

Record where the impact occurred and when symptoms first appeared. Keep later changes separate from what you noticed immediately. For example, a witness can describe the impact, while a clinician can document exam findings. Keep both records and let the medical team evaluate any connection between them.

## What blunt force trauma means

Blunt force trauma is an injury caused by impact or force without an object necessarily penetrating the body. It can involve compression, crushing, or sudden acceleration and deceleration. Injuries may include bruises, broken bones, brain injuries, or damage to internal organs.

The skin can remain intact despite serious underlying injuries. Visible bruising does not reliably show the extent of internal damage. California’s Emergency Medical Services Authority provides information about the state’s trauma care system and its role in treating serious injuries. [California EMSA trauma information](https://emsa.ca.gov/trauma/)

### How the circumstances affect a claim

In a vehicle collision, an occupant may strike the steering wheel, dashboard, or another interior surface. Pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists can suffer injuries from contact with a vehicle or the ground. Investigators must determine what caused the collision and how the impacts occurred.

Falls can cause head, hip, spinal, and other injuries. A claim may turn on a damaged stair, spill, or maintenance problem rather than the fall itself.

Assaults involving punches, kicks, or objects may support intentional-injury claims instead of ordinary negligence claims. Sports injuries raise different questions about inherent risks, equipment, supervision, and participation agreements. An injury during a game does not automatically mean someone acted wrongfully.

## Get medical care before gathering evidence

Suspected internal bleeding requires emergency care. Warning signs may include fainting, confusion, severe abdominal pain, breathing difficulty, or signs of shock. Serious internal injuries can occur without visible bleeding. Call 911 if you suspect an emergency rather than waiting to collect evidence or speak with a lawyer. [MedlinePlus guidance on bleeding](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000045.htm)

Clinicians consider the force involved, symptoms, examination findings, and vital signs. They may order X-rays, CT scans, ultrasound, MRI, or blood tests when appropriate. Not every patient needs every test. The treating medical team decides which examinations and treatments are necessary.

Treatment may involve observation, clinician-directed pain management, immobilization, surgery, or rehabilitation. Medication decisions belong with the treating clinician, especially when bleeding or another complication is possible. This discussion provides general information, not a diagnosis or home-treatment plan.

Medical records can later document when symptoms began, what clinicians found, and how the injury affected daily life. Their first purpose, however, is to support appropriate care.

## What a negligence claim must establish

Most negligence claims require proof of duty, breach, causation, and damages. Each element addresses a different issue. A defendant may acknowledge that an injury occurred while disputing responsibility for it. California Courts recommends identifying the proper defendants and organizing supporting evidence before pursuing a lawsuit. [California Courts personal injury guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury)

### Duty and breach

Duty concerns the defendant’s legal obligations under the circumstances. Drivers generally must use reasonable care when operating vehicles. Property owners and occupiers may have duties concerning unsafe conditions, although their responsibilities depend on the facts.

Breach concerns what the defendant did or failed to do. Evidence might show distracted driving, an unreasonable failure to address a hazard, or inadequate precautions. An accident alone does not necessarily prove a breach.

### Causation and damages

Causation links the wrongful conduct to the injury. A doctor may explain that an impact caused a fracture. Other evidence must establish who was legally responsible for the impact.

Damages are the resulting losses. Medical expenses, lost earnings, pain, and physical limitations need evidentiary support. A diagnosis alone does not establish how much compensation is recoverable.

**Hypothetical example.** A customer slips in a store and fractures a wrist. The medical records establish the fracture. Liability may depend on what caused the fall, how long the hazard existed, and whether the store reasonably should have discovered and addressed it. Medical records cannot resolve those questions on their own.

## Identifying the responsible parties

The person directly involved in an incident may not be the only potential defendant. Depending on the evidence, an investigation could involve a driver, employer, property owner, maintenance contractor, product manufacturer, or public entity.

Each proposed defendant needs a factual and legal connection to the claim. In a multivehicle collision, reconstructing the sequence of impacts may help determine whose conduct contributed to a particular injury. Naming every party connected to an incident is not a substitute for investigating responsibility.

### Property conditions, assaults, and sports

A [premises injury claim](/practice-areas/slip-and-fall) often depends on who controlled the premises, the hazard involved, inspection practices, and notice of the condition. A property owner is not automatically responsible for every injury on the property.

An assault may support a civil claim separate from any criminal case. A claim against another party, such as a business operator, requires an independent assessment of that party’s conduct and legal duties.

Sports claims may involve assumption-of-risk principles or releases. Their effect depends on the circumstances. Neither participation in a sport nor a signed agreement should be treated as an automatic answer without reviewing the activity, conduct, and relevant document.

## How comparative fault can affect recovery

[California’s comparative fault system](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) can reduce compensation when an injured person’s negligence contributed to the harm. Partial fault does not necessarily prevent a claim. It also does not guarantee recovery. The injured person must still prove the defendant’s responsibility.

**Hypothetical example.** A factfinder determines that compensable losses total $100,000 and assigns the injured person 20 percent of the fault. The award would generally fall to $80,000 before other applicable issues are considered. This example illustrates a calculation, not a prediction of any claim’s value.

When several defendants are involved, economic and non-economic damages may be allocated differently. In covered cases, California generally limits a defendant’s liability for non-economic damages to that defendant’s percentage of fault. It is therefore important not to divide every category of loss in the same way. [California Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV)

## Preserve evidence that addresses the disputed issues

Evidence should help explain both the incident and its consequences. A photograph of a bruise documents something different from a photograph of the broken step that allegedly caused the fall.

When safe and available, preserve scene photographs showing conditions, vehicles, equipment, and surrounding areas. Keep witness names and contact details, police or incident reports, workplace reports, relevant messages, insurance information, and correspondence.

Medical records, discharge instructions, bills, and receipts can help document treatment and expenses. Employment records may establish missed work or changed duties. Notes about symptoms and daily limitations can provide useful context when they accurately reflect what happened.

Surveillance footage may be overwritten, and physical conditions can change. Prompt preservation can matter, but do not trespass, enter unsafe areas, or delay medical care to gather evidence. A lawyer can assess whether formal preservation requests are appropriate.

### Pre-existing conditions and medical evidence

A pre-existing condition does not resolve whether an incident caused additional harm. The question may be whether the event caused a new injury, aggravated an existing condition, or was unrelated to later symptoms.

Accurate medical histories help distinguish those possibilities. Earlier records, diagnostic findings, and the course of treatment may all matter. Neither assuming that every later symptom came from the incident nor treating an earlier diagnosis as defeating the claim provides a sound analysis.

Experts may address injury mechanisms, accident reconstruction, future treatment, or work limitations. Their opinions must answer specific questions based on evidence, not supply certainty where facts are missing.

## What compensation may cover

Available damages depend on the claim, supporting evidence, and applicable law. [Economic and non-economic damages](/blogs/how-much-is-my-personal-injury-case-worth-california) compensate for losses. Punitive damages serve a different purpose and require separate proof.

### Economic losses

Economic damages may include reasonable medical expenses, rehabilitation costs, lost earnings, and reduced earning capacity. Future losses need support, such as a medical prognosis or evidence of lasting work restrictions.

A medical bill does not settle every question about recovery. The connection between treatment and the incident, the amount legally recoverable, and any reimbursement obligations may require further review.

### Non-economic losses

Non-economic damages may address pain, emotional distress, physical impairment, and loss of enjoyment of life. Specific, credible examples help explain these losses. Difficulty sleeping, being unable to lift a child, or needing help with everyday tasks can show how an injury changed someone’s life.

A spouse may also have a separate loss-of-consortium claim in appropriate circumstances. This is not compensation automatically available to every family member.

### Punitive damages and fatal injuries

Punitive damages are not routine, even when an injury is severe. California generally requires clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud, or malice under the applicable statute. Ordinary negligence alone is insufficient. [California Civil Code section 3294](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3294.&lawCode=CIV)

Fatal injuries require a distinction between wrongful-death and survival actions. Wrongful-death claims address qualifying survivors’ losses. Survival actions concern claims belonging to the deceased person that legally survive death. Eligible claimants and available damages differ. Neither action is simply an ordinary injury claim under another name. [California Code of Civil Procedure section 377.30](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.30.&lawCode=CCP) [Section 377.60](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.60.&lawCode=CCP)

## Filing deadlines and claims with special rules

California personal injury and wrongful-death claims often start with a general two-year filing rule. That deadline is not universal. Delayed discovery, minors, medical malpractice, and other circumstances can change the analysis. Claims involving public entities may require an administrative claim well before the ordinary lawsuit deadline. [California Courts deadline guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations)

Workplace injuries may involve workers’ compensation rules as well as possible claims against third parties. If medical treatment allegedly caused additional harm, that allegation may involve requirements different from those governing the original accident.

Do not assume insurance negotiations extend a filing deadline. Our [California filing-deadline guide](/blogs/california-personal-injury-deadlines) explains why court, notice, and evidence timelines need separate attention. Early review should identify the relevant parties, claim types, and deadlines rather than rely on a general timeline.

## From investigation to settlement or litigation

A claim usually begins with investigation, records collection, and an assessment of fault and losses. Insurance coverage is a separate issue. A person may be legally responsible even if coverage is limited or disputed.

Settlement discussions may resolve the claim. Filing a lawsuit may still be necessary to preserve rights or address contested issues. Litigation can include document exchanges, depositions, expert analysis, motions, mediation, and trial. Filing suit does not mean a trial is inevitable.

Before accepting a settlement, review which claims the release covers and whether future treatment remains uncertain. Liens or reimbursement claims may also affect the amount ultimately received. A lawyer can explain these issues and assess the evidence without guaranteeing an outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can blunt trauma be serious without bruising?

Yes. Internal injuries can occur without obvious bruising or external bleeding. Seek emergency care for suspected internal bleeding, fainting, confusion, breathing difficulty, or other severe symptoms. Appearance alone cannot establish that an injury is minor.

### Does a diagnosis prove that someone else was negligent?

No. A diagnosis helps establish the injury. Liability requires evidence connecting the defendant’s legally wrongful conduct to that harm. A medical explanation of how an injury occurred does not, by itself, establish who is legally responsible.

### Can I pursue a claim if I was partly at fault?

Potentially. California comparative fault can reduce compensation according to your share of responsibility. You must still prove the claim. Questions about causation, damages, and insurance coverage may remain even after fault percentages are determined.

### What should I bring to an initial legal consultation?

Bring available reports, photographs, witness information, medical records, bills, insurance correspondence, and wage-loss documents. A short chronology can help explain the incident, treatment, and ongoing limitations. Do not delay seeking advice because some records are missing.

### Will a settlement cover future medical care?

That depends on the agreement. A settlement release may resolve future claims arising from the incident even if treatment continues. Before signing, review the prognosis, anticipated expenses, release language, and any reimbursement obligations.

### How long do I have to file a blunt trauma lawsuit?

A two-year period often applies to California personal injury claims, but exceptions and earlier claim requirements can apply. Public-entity involvement, delayed discovery, age, and the type of claim can affect the deadline. Obtain an individualized assessment promptly.

### Does every blunt trauma claim go to trial?

No. Some claims settle before a lawsuit is filed. Others settle during litigation. Whether trial becomes necessary depends on disputed facts, legal issues, and settlement positions. Neither a settlement nor a favorable verdict can be guaranteed.
