A misspelled name, a missing witness and a disagreement about fault are different problems. They may require different information and different responses. Before writing that a report is wrong, identify which statement you challenge, who supplied it and what would help the agency evaluate it.

This guide addresses a report that already exists. If you are still organizing the aftermath of the collision, use the [California crash guide](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california) for the broader safety, evidence and reporting checklist.

## Obtain the report from the agency that prepared it

Save the incident number, collision date and location, and confirm the investigating agency. A city-street collision may involve municipal police; a freeway investigation may involve CHP. The city name in an address does not establish who holds the report. Check the paperwork or responding officer’s information rather than sending the same request to several agencies.

CHP’s current instructions allow eligible parties to request a CHP crash report through its Crash Portal or by using CHP 190 in person or by mail. Follow its identification, eligibility and fee instructions. That process requests a copy; it is not a general form for ordering an officer to change a conclusion. [Official CHP crash-report requests](https://www.chp.ca.gov/traffic/request-a-crash-report/)

If Buena Park Police or another city department investigated, use that department’s records process. Ask whether the available copy includes diagrams, continuation pages and any supplements that can be released to you. A missing page and an entry you disagree with should not be treated as the same issue.

## Identify what kind of problem you found

Read the report as a complete document before drafting a response. The narrative, diagram and party identifiers may explain a reference that seems wrong in isolation. Make a working copy for notes and retain the original file unchanged.

| Type of issue | Example | Useful supporting material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identifying detail | Incorrect plate, vehicle description or spelling | Registration, identification or original scene photograph |
| Date or location | A cross street differs from the scene record | Original images, trip records or other contemporaneous records |
| Missing information | A witness or recording is not identified | Contact details, original file and explanation of its source |
| Disputed statement | A sentence attributes words to the wrong person | A precise explanation of what you said and available corroboration |
| Disputed conclusion | The cause analysis conflicts with your account | Evidence addressing the sequence, visibility or conduct in dispute |

The right-hand column is a way to organize your materials, not a promise that an agency must accept them. Explain any uncertainty. A photograph’s displayed time may need context, and a witness may have seen only part of the event.

## Separate a factual correction from a disagreement about fault

If the report gives a plate number incorrectly, point to the entry and the record showing the plate. If you disagree with the officer’s cause analysis, identify the underlying facts you believe were missed or misunderstood. Repeating that you were not at fault does not explain what the reviewer should investigate.

Read attributed statements carefully. An officer may be recording another driver’s allegation rather than adopting it as an established fact. Your response should distinguish an inaccurate transcription of your statement from a competing account given by someone else. Keep the name or party number associated with each account clear.

A disputed police conclusion is also different from an insurer’s liability decision or a court’s determination. The [California comparative-fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) explains how responsibility may be disputed in the injury claim. Seek advice about the actual evidence rather than assuming a report either guarantees or defeats recovery.

## Ask about the agency’s review process

Contact the investigating agency and ask where to send a request concerning the existing report. Give the incident number and explain whether you are reporting an identifying error, offering new evidence or challenging an interpretation. Ask what method and file format it accepts and whether a particular officer or unit handles the review.

CHP’s reporting manual describes supplemental reports as a way to document additions or changes to an earlier investigation. It also explains that a supplement does not necessarily change the officer’s opinions or conclusions. That is an agency reporting procedure, not a guarantee that a member of the public can demand a particular revision. [CHP narrative and supplemental-report instructions](https://www.chp.ca.gov/siteassets/policy/hpm/hpm-110.5/hpm-110.5-ch-7.pdf)

City departments can have different procedures. Do not present CHP’s internal form as a statewide public correction application. Keep the name of the person you contacted, the date and the instructions received. If the first contact handles only copies, ask where requests about the content of an investigation should go.

## Build a correction packet another person can follow

Use a short cover note and an indexed set of supporting records. Put each disputed item on its own line with the page, section and party identifier. Explain what you believe is inaccurate and identify the document that addresses that point. Separate a request for factual correction from a request that new material be considered.

Preserve original recordings and photographs. If you create a cropped image or a marked diagram for explanation, keep it separate from the original and label it. A still frame can help someone locate an event in a longer video; it should not replace the complete file when the surrounding sequence matters.

Avoid sending an entire medical history to resolve a plate-number error. Ask what information is needed and use an appropriate delivery method for sensitive records. If a lawyer represents you, coordinate the request so that the agency does not receive inconsistent versions from different people.

## A focused request you can adapt

Use the following structure as an organizing aid. It is not an official form, a sworn declaration or a filing that satisfies every reporting requirement. Follow the agency’s own instructions and include only accurate information you are comfortable substantiating.

- **Report reference:** Incident number, collision date, location and investigating agency.
- **Your connection:** Your role in the collision and an appropriate way to contact you.
- **Specific entry:** Page, section, party identifier and the statement at issue.
- **Requested review:** The factual correction or additional information you want considered.
- **Supporting record:** File name or document, its source and the point it addresses.
- **Remaining uncertainty:** Facts you cannot verify or questions needing investigation.
- **Response requested:** Confirmation of receipt and instructions for obtaining any revised report or supplement.

For example, you could write, “Please review the plate listed for Party 2 on page 1. The attached scene photograph shows the plate on that vehicle.” Include the photograph’s filename and when it was taken so the reviewer can match it to your request. Keep a dated copy of what you sent. If there’s a receipt or submission number, save it with the original report so you can ask about the same request when you follow up.

## A missing-witness example

**Hypothetical example.** A report describes a lane-change collision but does not mention a pedestrian who spoke with the driver afterward. The driver has the pedestrian’s contact details and a message explaining where the pedestrian stood. The driver should identify what the person says they observed, preserve the message and ask how to submit that information.

That witness may have seen the vehicles only after the impact. If so, say that. If the witness saw the approach but their view was partly blocked, preserve that qualification too. An account can still be useful without establishing every part of the collision.

Now suppose a business later locates video. Retain the original file, the source and the explanation of the camera’s displayed time. Send a follow-up identifying the earlier request and the new record. Do not describe the video as confirming your entire account before it has actually been reviewed.

## Keep the insurer and other records updated separately

If an insurer relies on the disputed entry, explain the specific disagreement and provide relevant supporting material through its claim process. State accurately whether you requested an agency review, received a response or obtained a supplement. Those are different stages. A request that is still pending should not be described as an accepted correction.

Keep every version of the report and record when you received it. When a supplement arrives, ask whether the insurer has received it and which part of the investigation remains unresolved. The [insurance-adjuster guide](/blogs/what-is-an-insurance-adjuster-car-accident) explains how to keep requests and coverage communications organized.

A correction to a police report does not establish that the DMV amended an SR-1 or that an insurer updated its claim system. Our [DMV accident-reporting guide](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident) explains the separate report. Address any error with the organization responsible for that record.

## Continue preserving evidence and checking deadlines

Do not stop gathering lawful evidence while waiting for a response. A camera recording can become unavailable, a vehicle can be repaired and a witness can become harder to reach. Keep the preservation question separate from whether an agency will release a copy or change its narrative.

A pending review also should not be treated as an extension of a claim or lawsuit deadline. Keep insurer notices, citation paperwork and legal filing issues in separate entries. The [California filing-deadline guide](/blogs/california-personal-injury-deadlines) explains why individual facts and possible public-entity involvement require attention.

If the disputed account affects a significant injury claim, bring the report, your correction packet, insurer correspondence and any approaching deadline to a [consultation with the office](/contact). Ask which missing record would make the greatest difference to the next decision. The [car accident claim overview](/practice-areas/car-accidents) explains where the report fits alongside vehicle evidence, witness accounts and insurance records. Keep original documents and arrange secure delivery of sensitive material.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I edit the PDF and send the corrected version to the insurer?

Do not present your own edited file as an agency-issued report. Keep the official version and explain disputed entries in a separate note. Label any annotated working copy so its origin is clear.

### Does the investigating officer have to change the fault conclusion?

A request for review does not guarantee that result. Provide the evidence supporting your position and ask what response or additional record is available under the agency’s process. A supplement can add information without changing the conclusion.

### What if the report omits a witness?

Preserve the contact information and the source of the account, then ask the investigating agency how to provide it. Explain what the witness personally observed and any limits on their view. Do not write a statement for the witness to adopt.

### Can CHP correct a report written by city police?

Begin with the agency that prepared the investigation. CHP’s report-request process concerns CHP reports. A local department’s investigation should be addressed through that department’s own process.

### What if no report was prepared at all?

That is a different issue from correcting an existing record. Ask the appropriate agency whether a report or incident record exists and what reporting steps remain available or required. Do not invent a report number or assume a correction request can create an investigation retroactively.

If there is no written report, our [guide to opening a claim without a police report](/blogs/car-insurance-claim-without-police-report-california) explains which other records you can use.

### Should I wait for the correction before contacting my insurer or a lawyer?

Do not assume waiting is harmless. Notify the relevant insurer as required, preserve evidence and check deadlines while the report issue is addressed. Explain which facts remain disputed instead of delaying every part of the claim until the agency responds.
