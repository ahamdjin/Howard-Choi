## What California requires and what the minimums cover

California DMV lists standard motor vehicle liability limits of $30,000 for injury or death to one person, $60,000 for bodily injury per accident, and $15,000 for property damage. The first two figures work together when more than one person is injured. Property damage has its own limit. Ask for the declarations and effective dates of the motorcycle policy when confirming the amounts that apply to a crash. [California DMV insurance requirements](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/insurance-requirements/)

Liability coverage is for covered losses the insured is legally responsible for causing to others. If a rider loses control and damages a parked car, the other owner may have a property damage claim. If the rider’s motorcycle also needs repairs, look to the bike’s physical damage coverage and the circumstances of the crash. The fact that the rider complied with minimum insurance requirements does not establish coverage for the motorcycle itself.

Check that the insurance card lists the correct vehicle. Match the VIN and policy dates to the declarations. If you replaced a bike or changed how it is used, confirm when the insurer recorded the change and whether the policy has a provision for a newly acquired vehicle. A quote and an active policy are different documents. Keep proof of the effective date and any updates.

## Which coverage might pay each bill?

| Expense or claim | Coverage to check | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Injury to someone the insured rider allegedly harmed | Bodily injury liability | Responsibility, who is insured, and applicable limits |
| Damage to the rider’s motorcycle | Collision, comprehensive, or the other driver’s property damage liability | Cause of loss, inspection requirements, deductible or limits |
| Injuries to the rider or a passenger | Medical payments if purchased, health insurance, or applicable liability or motorist coverage | Eligibility, bills, limits and coordination with other payers |
| A crash caused by a driver with missing or inadequate coverage | Uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage if applicable | Insured status, facts about the other driver and policy conditions |
| Loss of custom parts or safety gear | Any equipment or accessory coverage | Scheduled equipment, receipts, limits and exclusions |

Ask about passenger coverage specifically. A passenger may have different rights and policy questions from the operator. If a policy covers one rider and another person was operating the bike, confirm who was authorized to use it and whether an exclusion applies. Avoid assuming a policy written for another motorcycle or for the driver’s car will respond in the same way.

If the bike was financed, the lender may require physical damage coverage. That requirement addresses the lender’s interest in the motorcycle. It does not replace a review of medical coverage or liability limits. Keep the loan documents, title and any equipment receipts separate from your medical bills and work records.

## A California intersection example

Suppose a car turns left across a motorcycle’s path and the two collide. The rider’s bike is damaged, the rider and passenger need treatment, and the car driver says the motorcycle was traveling too fast. Preserve video, impact photographs, helmet condition and witness information. A dispute about speed should be evaluated with evidence, alongside the driver’s turn and visibility. The [motorcycle accident guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-accident-guide) explains how different evidence can matter in a motorcycle claim.

The rider can ask their own insurer about applicable collision and medical payments coverage while the car driver’s insurer evaluates fault. The passenger may need a separate injury claim number. If the car has a low liability limit and more than one person was hurt, request the applicable coverage information and review other potential sources before agreeing to a release. See the [uninsured and underinsured driver guide](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims).

Now suppose the motorcycle had aftermarket luggage and protective equipment damaged in the crash. Gather purchase records and photographs showing what was installed before impact. Ask whether the carrier treats the items as standard parts, scheduled equipment, or excluded property. Provide those details to the adjuster handling the motorcycle, separate from the rider’s injury claim.

## What affects the cost of insuring a motorcycle?

Quotes can differ by bike, rider, use, location, limits and deductibles. Compare the same motorcycle and coverage amounts across policies. If one quote costs less, identify whether it also limits passengers, reduces uninsured motorist coverage, or excludes equipment. Ask which discounts are actually included and what documentation the insurer needs to keep them. A price comparison is useful only when you know what changed.

Before borrowing or lending a bike, ask how the policy treats an occasional rider. If you ride for a business or use the motorcycle for delivery work, give the agent that detail in writing. Ask whether the proposed policy covers that use and keep the reply. The same facts may matter later if an adjuster investigates the circumstances of a crash.

## What to bring to an injury claim review

- The policy and declarations that applied on the date of the collision.
- The bike’s VIN, registration, photos, repair estimate, and receipts for equipment.
- The identity of each rider and passenger, and information about who owned or authorized use of the motorcycle.
- Statements, witness contacts, scene photos and the relevant police or incident number.
- Medical records, bills, health plan explanations, and documents showing time missed from work.

Keep the helmet and other equipment in their post-crash condition if they may be evidence. Document where they are stored. Discuss the scope of any proposed release before settling vehicle damage if injuries are still unresolved. The firm’s [motorcycle accident practice](/practice-areas/motorcycle-accidents) describes how it reviews those claims.

## Check how borrowing a motorcycle affects the claim

If the rider was using someone else’s bike, start with the policy listing that motorcycle and identify the rider’s own policies. Document permission and what the ride was for. A friend borrowing a bike for a short trip, a household member who uses it every weekend, and a rider doing deliveries can raise different questions. Ask the insurer whether it is reviewing the driver, the use, or the vehicle. Provide the endorsement or rental agreement when it helps establish that fact.

The passenger’s situation can differ from the driver’s. Get the passenger’s name and contact details and ask which claim number belongs to them. If an adjuster opens an injury file under the operator’s name, confirm that it also has a record of the passenger’s expenses. Keep the passenger’s medical paperwork out of the repair file and confirm what authorizations are needed.

A rider may also be asked about licensing or a helmet. Obtain the records and report them accurately. Those facts can be relevant to the investigation, while video and witness accounts may still be needed to explain the movements of the vehicles. The DMV’s rider guide explains the separate licensing and safety requirements. [California DMV motorcyclist guide](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/special-interest-driver-guides/motorcyclists-guide/)

If several policies respond, ask how they coordinate and which payment each carrier is considering. One company may handle damage to the bike while another investigates injuries. Confirm whether a proposed payment covers equipment as well as the motorcycle and whether the release reaches any injury claims. For the insurer reviewing the bike, send photos showing its condition before the crash alongside the post-crash inspection. For the injury adjuster, keep treatment, wage and recovery records under a separate claim reference.

Keep any statement about a coverage limit with the policy period it refers to. If an adjuster quotes a renewal limit by mistake, send the collision date and request confirmation of the limit that was in effect that day.

If the incident involved a motorcycle and a separate vehicle owned by the same household, check both declarations. The owner name can be the same while the insured vehicles, policy periods and available benefits differ.

## Before a damaged motorcycle is repaired or collected for salvage

Ask where the motorcycle will be kept, who can access it and what the insurer plans to inspect. Record the VIN, odometer, model and equipment as far as you can do so safely. Keep the repair estimate and identify any part the shop believes needs further inspection. If a repair would change evidence relevant to a disputed collision, discuss the timing with the insurer and the lawyer reviewing the injury before authorizing the work.

If the carrier proposes a total loss, request the written valuation. Check that its description matches the bike you owned, including the year, model, mileage and any equipment the policy covers. Submit receipts or earlier photographs for a specific omission. The [California total loss guide](/blogs/what-happens-when-car-is-totaled-california) explains the difference between a vehicle valuation and a loan balance. Ask the motorcycle adjuster to explain the policy terms and figures that apply to your bike.

Confirm what a pickup authorization permits and whether any storage charges are continuing. Ask who is arranging collection, the destination and how you can obtain the inspection or valuation records. Tell the relevant people if an inspection still needs to be arranged. Keep the response in writing so a change in the motorcycle's location does not leave you relying on an unanswered request.

Keep your treatment and benefit questions moving in their own file. If the motorcycle policy includes medical payments coverage, the [MedPay guide](/blogs/medical-payments-coverage-medpay-california) explains how to match approved payments to provider balances. Verify eligibility and terms under the motorcycle policy. A vehicle inspection or property payment does not answer those medical coverage questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does motorcycle liability insurance pay my own medical bills?

Generally, liability coverage addresses covered injuries the insured causes to others. Ask about medical payments, health insurance or another driver’s liability coverage. Which benefits apply depends on the policy and crash.

### Does the state minimum pay to repair my motorcycle?

The liability minimum applies to covered claims against the insured. For damage to your own motorcycle, check your physical damage coverage and whether another party may be responsible. Ask which deductible and limits apply.

### What if the driver who hit me has no insurance?

Ask your carrier to confirm any uninsured motorist and collision coverage, and identify what it needs to verify the other driver’s status. Keep evidence of fault, injury and damage. Coverage for the bike may differ from coverage for the rider.

### Do I have to accept the first repair or total-loss estimate?

Ask how the carrier calculated the amount and which equipment it included. If an estimate omits work or uses the wrong model, submit the specific records showing the difference. Keep any release separate from the estimate so you can see which claims a proposed payment would close.

### What should I do if someone knocks over my parked motorcycle?

Photograph the bike's position and the surrounding parking area before moving it when safe. Record when it was last undamaged, preserve any note and identify witnesses or cameras. The [parked-vehicle accident guide](/blogs/what-to-do-if-someone-hits-your-parked-car-california) explains that evidence process. Ask your motorcycle insurer which coverage applies to the actual cause of damage and whether it needs an inspection before repairs.
