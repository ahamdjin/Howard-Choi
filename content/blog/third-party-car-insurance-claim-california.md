## Decide what needs attention first

Start with the bill or decision you need addressed. If your car is in a storage yard and you need transportation, get the location, daily charges, and inspection schedule in writing. If you were injured, keep your medical and work records separately from the vehicle estimate. A third-party property damage claim may move quickly even while an injury claim is still being evaluated.

You can notify your own insurer while you gather information for a third-party claim. Check your policy for notice and cooperation requirements. Reporting the crash and requesting payment under a specific coverage are separate steps. Ask which coverage your insurer is opening, who is assigned, and whether any request you make will affect how it coordinates with another carrier. [California Department of Insurance’s accident guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/hadaccident.cfm)

California’s [fault-based system](/blogs/is-california-a-no-fault-state-or-an-at-fault-state) makes the other driver’s legal responsibility central to a liability claim. That carrier may wait to evaluate the driver’s account, verify coverage, and review evidence. An insurance card and an incident number help open the file. They do not establish that liability has been accepted.

## Compare the two ways to handle vehicle damage

| Question | Your own carrier | The other driver’s carrier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What could pay? | Applicable collision or another benefit under your policy | Property damage liability if coverage and responsibility are established |
| What can delay payment? | Inspection, proof of loss, and a coverage or repair dispute | Coverage verification, a fault dispute, or several claims sharing a limit |
| What might you owe up front? | The applicable deductible and charges the policy excludes | Expenses you incur before approval or amounts the carrier disputes |
| What should you ask for? | The relevant policy provision and written estimate | The written coverage and liability position and what is still being investigated |

An insurer may direct you to a preferred repair facility. Ask whether you can use a shop you choose and how the insurer will review additional damage found during repairs. If your own carrier pays, it may seek recovery from the responsible party. Tell both carriers about the payment so they can coordinate. Keep [your collision deductible](/blogs/car-insurance-deductible-after-accident-california), the repair payment, and any reimbursement in separate entries.

If the insurer declares a total loss, use the [totaled-car guide](/blogs/what-happens-when-car-is-totaled-california) to check the valuation before comparing offers. For some drivers, using their own collision coverage gets the vehicle inspected sooner. For others, the other carrier has already accepted responsibility and authorized repairs. The right comparison uses the timing and costs of the actual options available to you. Ask for the next concrete action from each company instead of relying on an estimate of how quickly the whole case will close.

## Open the claim with the facts you have

Verify the carrier’s contact information and provide the crash date, time and location. Give the driver and vehicle information you collected. If the other driver was [using a borrowed vehicle](/blogs/does-car-insurance-follow-the-car-or-driver-california) or working, say what you know and how you know it. Ask what proof the insurer needs to confirm coverage. Our [auto liability insurance guide](/blogs/what-is-auto-liability-insurance-california) explains how injury and property damage limits are applied. Avoid guessing about who owned the car or what the driver was doing.

Provide photographs of the scene and damage, witness contact information, and the incident report number if available. Send originals or copies that preserve useful details such as date and location. If the adjuster is waiting for a police report, ask whether it can start reviewing photos, inspection findings, and witness accounts in the meantime. Our [California post-crash guide](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california) covers immediate steps and what to document.

Ask [the insurance adjuster](/blogs/what-is-an-insurance-adjuster-car-accident) to confirm the claim number, the assigned person for property damage, and the assigned person for bodily injury. A request to record a statement, inspect the car, or obtain medical records can come from a different team. Keep a copy of what you provide and note the date. If the insurer says it still needs information, ask for a specific list.

## An example when fault is disputed

Two cars collide while one is turning into a driveway. The drivers disagree about whether the turn signal was on. The other insurer says it needs to speak to its driver before accepting responsibility. Meanwhile, one car is at a tow yard and the daily storage bill keeps increasing.

The owner can ask their carrier whether collision coverage is available and get instructions about where the car should be moved. They can also tell the other adjuster that a business camera may have captured the turn and identify who controls it. Those actions address different problems. Moving the car can control storage costs. Preserving the video can help resolve fault. Neither action requires waiting for the other insurer to finish its review.

If the owner uses collision, ask whether the insurer expects to pursue reimbursement and how it will handle the deductible. If the other carrier later accepts fault, notify it of any collision payment already made. Do the same for rental payments. The goal is to identify the balance actually owed and the party with the right to seek it.

## When insurance is missing or the limits are low

If the other carrier says the policy was inactive or did not cover the driver, get its position in writing. Check your own collision and uninsured motorist provisions. Whether a person, vehicle, or loss qualifies can depend on the policy and the facts. The [uninsured driver claims guide](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims) explains how those questions differ for vehicle damage and injury.

Even when the other driver has coverage, several injured people or vehicles may share a limit. Ask which limits are involved and which claims the insurer is evaluating. A release can affect your ability to pursue another insurer or responsible party, so read the entire proposal before agreeing. A fast property payment should be reviewed on its own terms if the crash also caused injuries.

### Keep the DMV filing on its own schedule

Opening a claim does not complete your DMV obligations. Review the [California DMV accident reporting guide](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident) and retain confirmation if an SR-1 is submitted. When you send the report number to an insurer, tell it whether the number refers to a police report, a DMV submission, or an insurance claim. They are different records.

If you are weighing a settlement with unresolved injuries, the firm’s [car accident practice page](/practice-areas/car-accidents) describes the types of coverage and evidence questions it can review.

## What to do when one insurer accepts only part of the claim

Ask which part has been accepted and which remains disputed. A company may authorize bodywork while declining a rental extension. It may agree to the amount of a repair estimate but still be investigating whether an injury came from the collision. A written response should identify the expense, the reason it is disputed, and whether the carrier wants more documentation.

For example, suppose the insurer approves five rental days, but the shop documents that the car was unsafe to release for eight. Send the shop’s dates, reason for the delay, and itemized rental invoice. Ask the insurer to review the three disputed days. If your policy already reimbursed two of those days, say so. The remaining amount is easier to resolve when the insurer can see which days were covered and which ones still cost you money.

The same approach works for a disputed injury item. If the adjuster says it never received the employer’s confirmation of missed shifts, send that confirmation with the specific dates and ask for acknowledgment. If the company instead disputes that the missed time resulted from the crash, obtain its reason in writing so you can identify the evidence that addresses that question.

If the carrier requests proof that a bill remains unpaid, send the current statement and keep the date it was issued. An older invoice may include charges that another insurer has since paid.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I contact the other driver’s insurer directly?

Yes. Ask to open a claim and provide the facts and documents you have. A claim number is a reference for tracking the file. Ask separately whether the insurer has verified coverage and accepted liability.

### Can I report the same crash to both companies?

Yes. Each company may be investigating a different coverage. Disclose payments and any offers involving the same loss so the insurers can coordinate. Keep each claim number beside the documents that relate to it.

### Do I have to wait for the other insurer before repairing my car?

Check your policy and any inspection requirements first. If you have applicable collision coverage, ask your carrier about proceeding while liability is disputed. If repairs have to start for safety or to avoid accumulating costs, document the damage and tell the relevant insurer what is happening.

### What if the other adjuster stops responding?

Send a dated follow-up that identifies the claim, the documents you supplied, and the decision or update you need. Ask who is handling the file and whether anything remains outstanding. California’s [fair claims regulations](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/130-laws-regs-hearings/05-CCR/fair-claims-regs.cfm) describe claims handling standards and the department can provide consumer assistance.
