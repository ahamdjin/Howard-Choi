But not everyone knows exactly what the law says. Does a back seat passenger need to wear a seat belt? Can the police stop you for not wearing one? What happens if your child is not properly secured?

California seat belt laws require drivers and passengers ages 16 and older to wear a properly fastened seat belt when traveling in a covered motor vehicle on a highway. Children younger than 16 must also be properly restrained under California's child passenger safety laws.

The main adult seat belt requirement is found in California Vehicle Code Section 27315. [California Vehicle Code section 27315](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=27315.) Separate rules cover infants, children, and younger teenagers.

Here is what drivers and passengers need to know about California's seat belt laws in 2026, including fines, exceptions, and upcoming changes in 2027.

## What Are the Seat Belt Laws in California?

California's Motor Vehicle Safety Act requires most people traveling in passenger vehicles to wear a seat belt.

Under Vehicle Code Section 27315, drivers cannot operate a covered motor vehicle on a highway unless they and their passengers ages 16 and older are properly restrained. Passengers in this age group also have an independent duty to wear a seat belt.

The law applies to both front and rear seats.

California also has separate requirements for children. Younger children generally need an appropriate car seat or booster seat, while older children must use a suitable restraint or properly fitting seat belt.

## California Seat Belt Requirements by Age

| Passenger age | California requirement in 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Under 2 years | Rear facing car seat unless the child weighs at least 40 pounds or is at least 40 inches tall |
| Ages 2 through 7 | Appropriate car seat or booster seat in the rear seat, unless a statutory exception applies |
| Ages 8 through 15 | Appropriate child restraint or properly worn seat belt |
| Ages 16 and older | Properly fastened seat belt in covered vehicles, subject to applicable exemptions |

A child under eight who is at least 4 feet 9 inches tall may qualify to use a regular seat belt under current law.

However, meeting the minimum legal requirement does not always mean an adult seat belt offers the best fit. A child should remain in an appropriate booster seat until the belt fits correctly.

## Do Back Seat Passengers Have to Wear Seat Belts in California?

Yes. California's general seat belt law applies to passengers in both the front and back seats of a covered motor vehicle.

Some passengers assume the back seat is safe enough without a seat belt. That is not true.

During a crash, an unrestrained person can move forward or sideways with considerable force. This can cause serious injuries to that person and others inside the vehicle.

The California Department of Motor Vehicles advises every driver and passenger to buckle up before traveling.

There are limited exceptions for certain vehicles and situations, including specific taxi and emergency vehicle rules. However, most passengers traveling in ordinary cars should expect to wear seat belts regardless of where they sit.

## Who Is Responsible If a Passenger Is Not Wearing a Seat Belt?

Responsibility depends partly on the passenger's age.

### Passengers Ages 16 and Older

Under California Vehicle Code Section 27315, passengers ages 16 and older have their own legal responsibility to wear a seat belt.

Drivers must also comply with the rules that apply to operating a vehicle with passengers.

That means an adult passenger can receive a citation for failing to buckle up. A driver may also face legal consequences under the circumstances covered by the statute.

### Passengers Younger Than 16

Children and teenagers younger than 16 are covered by California's child passenger restraint laws.

Under Vehicle Code Sections 27360 and 27360.5, responsibility generally falls on the parent, legal guardian, or driver, depending on who is present in the vehicle.

For example, if a parent is riding in the passenger seat and someone else is driving, the law may place responsibility on the parent rather than the driver.

The safest practice is simple. Before moving the vehicle, check that every passenger is using the appropriate restraint.

## California Child Car Seat and Booster Seat Laws

California has specific child passenger safety requirements because adult seat belts do not always fit younger children properly.

The appropriate restraint depends on the child's age, size, and the manufacturer's instructions.

### Children Under 2 Years Old

Children younger than two must generally ride in a rear facing car seat.

California law provides an exception once the child reaches either 40 pounds or 40 inches in height.

However, this does not mean parents should automatically switch to a forward facing seat as soon as the child meets either threshold.

The California Highway Patrol recommends keeping children rear facing as long as possible within the car seat manufacturer's permitted height and weight limits.

### Children Ages 2 Through 7

Children under eight must generally ride in an appropriate child passenger restraint system in the back seat.

Depending on their size and development, this may be a rear facing seat, forward facing seat, or booster seat.

California law provides certain exceptions, including an allowance for a child who is at least 4 feet 9 inches tall to use a properly fitting seat belt.

A regular vehicle seat belt should cross the upper thighs and the center of the chest rather than the stomach or neck.

### Children Ages 8 Through 15

Children ages eight through fifteen must use an appropriate child restraint system or seat belt.

Although the general legal age threshold changes at eight, many children still need booster seats after their eighth birthday.

The California Office of Traffic Safety recommends booster seats until children are tall enough for an adult belt to fit correctly.

Important 2027 update: California is introducing a new five step test for proper seat belt use by children in this age group. The change becomes operative January 1, 2027.

## What Changes to California Seat Belt Laws Take Effect in 2027?

California Assembly Bill 435 introduced updated child passenger restraint requirements. [California Senate summary of enacted 2025 legislation](https://sfa.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-12/digest_2025.pdf)

One important change is a five step seat belt fit test for children ages eight through fifteen.

Starting January 1, 2027, the law defines proper seat belt restraint for this age group using five conditions.

### California's New Five Step Seat Belt Test

Back against the seat: The child must be able to sit all the way back against the vehicle seat.

Knees at the edge: The child's knees must bend over the edge of the seat.

Shoulder belt position: The belt must fit snugly across the center of the chest and shoulder, not the neck.

Lap belt position: The lap belt must sit as low as possible and touch the child's thighs.

Proper fit throughout the trip: The child must be able to maintain that position for the entire journey.

If a child cannot meet all five requirements, an appropriate child restraint system may still be needed.

For example, a nine year old child may be legally old enough to use a regular seat belt under the 2026 rules. However, beginning in 2027, that child must meet the five step test to qualify as properly restrained by the vehicle belt alone.

This upcoming change makes seat belt fit especially important for parents of children ages eight through fifteen.

The five step test is not yet the operative statutory test as of November 2026.

## What Is the Fine for Not Wearing a Seat Belt in California?

Failing to wear a seat belt can result in a traffic citation.

Under Vehicle Code Section 27315, the statutory base fine for an adult seat belt violation is:

| Violation | Statutory fine |
| --- | --- |
| First adult seat belt offense | Up to $20 |
| Each subsequent adult seat belt offense | Up to $50 |

These amounts are base fines, not necessarily the total amount someone pays.

California adds various assessments and court charges to many traffic violations.

As a result, a ticket may cost considerably more than $20 or $50.

For the current amount, check the citation and the applicable court's traffic bail schedule.

## Are Child Seat Belt Violations More Expensive?

Yes.

California Vehicle Code Section 27360.6 sets higher statutory penalties for violations involving child passenger restraints.

| Violation | Statutory fine |
| --- | --- |
| First child restraint offense | $100 |
| Second or subsequent child restraint offense | $250 |

Additional assessments may apply.

The statute also provides limited options for reducing or waiving fines in certain circumstances, including qualifying financial hardship and completion of an approved education program.

These provisions are different from the penalties for adult seat belt violations.

## Can You Get Pulled Over for Not Wearing a Seat Belt in California?

Yes,California has a primary enforcement seat belt law.

This means a police officer can stop a driver for an observed seat belt violation without first identifying another traffic offense.

Officers do not have to wait until someone is speeding or running a red light before enforcing the seat belt requirement.

California also participates in the Click It or Ticket campaign, which encourages drivers and passengers to buckle up.

The campaign includes enforcement aimed at unrestrained passengers in both the front and back seats.

## Does a Seat Belt Ticket Add Points to Your Driving Record?

An ordinary adult seat belt violation under Vehicle Code Section 27315 generally does not add a negligent operator point to a California driver's record.

However, this does not mean you can ignore the citation.

A ticket still requires a response by the deadline shown on the notice.

Child restraint violations are treated differently and may carry a driving record point.

If you receive a ticket, confirm the exact Vehicle Code section listed on the citation. That will help you understand the applicable fine and other possible consequences.

## Can You Attend Traffic School for a Seat Belt Ticket?

For a first adult seat belt offense, Vehicle Code Section 27315 allows a court to order attendance at a traffic violator school or another approved safety program instead of imposing the usual fine and associated charges.

This is subject to the court's decision.

It is not an automatic right, and the availability of an approved program depends on the circumstances.

## Are There Exceptions to California Seat Belt Laws?

Yes. California recognizes several narrow exceptions.

However, a person cannot simply choose not to wear a seat belt because it feels uncomfortable or inconvenient.

### Medical Conditions and Disabilities

Vehicle Code Section 27315 provides an exemption for people with certain medical conditions or physical disabilities that prevent proper seat belt use.

The condition must be certified by an appropriate licensed medical professional, as specified by the statute.

A person claiming the exemption should have the necessary documentation.

### Certain Emergency Vehicles

Specific exemptions apply to authorized emergency vehicles and public employees under conditions set out in the law.

Agency rules may still require seat belt use even where the general statute provides an exception.

### Certain Delivery and Collection Workers

California law includes limited exceptions for people performing specific work duties, such as newspaper delivery, rural postal delivery, and waste or recycling collection.

These exceptions apply under defined working conditions.

They do not create a general exemption for all delivery drivers.

### Taxis and Limousines

California has special seat belt provisions for certain taxi and limousine operations.

For example, the statute contains a limited exception for a taxi driver transporting a paying passenger on a city street.

Different requirements can apply to front seat passengers and vehicle operators.

Passengers should not assume that traveling in a taxi, limousine, or other hired vehicle automatically removes the need to wear a seat belt.

### Older Vehicles

California law does not generally require a vehicle owner to install or maintain seat belts that were not required under applicable federal law when the vehicle was originally sold.

This is a narrow vehicle equipment provision.

It should not be treated as permission to remove existing seat belts or ignore other applicable vehicle safety laws.

## How Should You Wear a Seat Belt Properly?

Simply clicking the seat belt into place is not enough if it is positioned incorrectly.

California law defines proper restraint by the position of the lap and shoulder portions of the belt.

A correctly worn seat belt should:

Fit snugly across the body without excessive slack.

Place the lap belt low across the hips or upper thighs.

Position the shoulder belt across the chest and shoulder.

Avoid crossing the stomach or neck.

Remain properly positioned for the entire trip.

Do not place the shoulder belt behind your back or under your arm.

For a pregnant passenger, the lap belt should sit low across the hips, below the abdomen. The shoulder belt should cross the chest and remain away from the neck.

These adjustments help the seat belt protect the stronger parts of the body during a crash.

## Why Are Seat Belts Important in a Car Accident?

The [vehicle safety guide](/blogs/the-safest-cars-to-drive-in-the-us) explains how restraint fit, installed equipment, and crash-test results answer different safety questions.

Seat belts reduce the risk of serious injuries by helping people remain in their seats during a collision.

When a vehicle stops suddenly, its occupants continue moving until something stops them.

Without a seat belt, a person may strike parts of the vehicle or be thrown from it.

A correctly worn seat belt helps slow the person's movement and spread crash forces across stronger parts of the body.

According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, lap and shoulder seat belts reduce the risk of fatal injury to front seat passenger car occupants by approximately 45%.

They also reduce the risk of moderate to critical injuries by around 50%.

These figures demonstrate why proper seat belt use is so important, even during short journeys.

Airbags provide additional protection, but they are designed to work with seat belts rather than replace them.

## Can You File an Injury Claim If You Were Not Wearing a Seat Belt?

Yes. Not wearing a seat belt does not automatically prevent an injured person from pursuing compensation after a California car accident.

However, it may affect the amount the person can recover.

California uses a comparative fault system. This means responsibility for an injury can be shared between different parties when the evidence supports it.

For example, another driver may have caused the collision by running a red light. If you were not wearing a seat belt, the defense might argue that your injuries became more serious because you were unrestrained.

That argument is known as the seat belt defense.

The defense must be supported by evidence. It is not enough to point out that someone was not wearing a seat belt.

## How Does California's Seat Belt Defense Work?

California Civil Jury Instruction 712 explains what a defendant generally must establish when claiming that an injured person was negligent for failing to wear a seat belt.

The defense must prove:

A working seat belt was available.

A reasonably careful person in the same circumstances would have worn it.

The injured person did not wear the available seat belt.

Wearing the belt would have prevented the injuries or made them less severe.

The fourth requirement can be especially important.

There may be a difference between an injury caused by the collision itself and an injury made worse by not wearing a seat belt.

Medical records, collision evidence, and expert opinions may help explain that difference.

California Vehicle Code Section 27315 also states that violating the seat belt statute does not automatically establish negligence in a civil case.

### Example of Comparative Fault

Imagine that a driver runs a red light and crashes into another vehicle.

The injured person was not wearing a seat belt.

After reviewing the evidence, a jury determines that the other driver caused the crash. However, it also finds that the injured person's failure to wear a seat belt contributed to the severity of the injuries.

If the recoverable damages were $100,000 and the injured person were assigned 20% [comparative fault](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury), the remaining recovery would be $80,000.

This is only an example. The actual outcome depends on the evidence, the injuries involved, and the applicable law.

Not wearing a seat belt does not automatically make someone responsible for causing a crash.

## Frequently Asked Questions About California Seat Belt Laws

### Is it illegal to drive without a seat belt in California?

Yes. Most drivers must wear a properly fastened seat belt when operating a covered motor vehicle on a highway under California Vehicle Code Section 27315.

### Do adults have to wear seat belts in the back seat?

Yes. The general law applies to adult passengers in both the front and rear seats. Limited statutory exceptions may apply in certain vehicles or circumstances.

### How much is a seat belt ticket in California?

The statutory base fine is up to $20 for a first adult offense and up to $50 for subsequent offenses. Court charges and assessments can make the actual amount higher.

### Can a passenger get a seat belt ticket?

Yes. Passengers ages 16 and older have their own legal duty to wear seat belts under California's general seat belt statute.

### Can police stop you just for not wearing a seat belt?

Yes. California allows primary enforcement of its seat belt requirements.

### Is a seat belt required in an Uber or Lyft?

Passengers should wear seat belts in rideshare vehicles. A rideshare trip does not create a general exemption from California's seat belt requirements.

### Do children need booster seats after turning eight?

They may. A child who cannot use an adult seat belt correctly should remain in an appropriate booster or other child restraint. New statutory belt fit requirements become operative on January 1, 2027.

### What is the new California seat belt law for 2027?

Assembly Bill 435 introduces a five step test to determine whether children ages eight through fifteen are properly restrained by a seat belt. The requirements become operative January 1, 2027.

### Can you get a seat belt exemption for medical reasons?

Certain medical conditions or disabilities may qualify for an exemption with the certification required by California law.

### Can you sue after a car accident if you were not wearing a seat belt?

Yes. Failure to wear a seat belt does not automatically prevent an injury claim. However, evidence that it contributed to the severity of injuries may affect recoverable damages.
