## What to do before leaving the parking spot

First make sure no one is hurt and the area is safe. If the damaged car creates a hazard or needs to be moved, document its position when you can do so safely. Take wide photos that show the parking stall, curb, nearby vehicles and street signs, then take close-ups of the damage. Photograph any note and keep the original. The original placement may help establish how it was left.

Write down the last time you saw the car undamaged and the time you discovered the damage. Narrow the window with a receipt, appointment record, parking ticket or earlier photo. If you were parked at an Orange County shopping center, note the entrance, store, or stall number and ask management who controls nearby cameras. A street address alone can be too broad for a useful footage request.

Look for witnesses and ask what they actually observed. Someone may have seen a vehicle pull away without seeing the impact. Record both details. If another driver is present, exchange the required information and keep their contact, plate, registration and insurance details. Avoid relying on a verbal promise to pay later. An estimate may be higher once the shop inspects the damage.

## The next step depends on what the other driver did

**The driver stayed.** Get their information and tell the carrier whether you have a witness, video, or an account from the driver. Ask which adjuster will inspect the car and whether coverage and responsibility have been accepted. Take care of any reporting duties separately.

**The driver left a note.** Save it and verify the contact information. A note with a phone number may lead to the driver, but it might omit the plate, insurer, or other identifying details. Ask the carrier how it will verify coverage. If you cannot reach the person or suspect the information is wrong, tell the appropriate law enforcement agency what you found.

**The driver left no information.** Ask the responsible agency how to report the discovery and notify your insurer. Make a list of camera locations and owners so the insurer or investigator can follow up. When a witness gives you a plate or vehicle description, preserve the source and timing. A plate identifies a vehicle. Further investigation may be needed to identify the driver.

A shopping center’s incident form can document the discovery. Keep a copy, but check whether there is also a police or CHP report and whether a separate DMV filing is required. Our [California DMV reporting guide](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident) explains how the records differ.

## Which insurance might cover the damage?

If the other driver is identified and their insurer accepts responsibility, their property damage liability coverage may pay for covered losses, subject to its limits. Confirm what it has approved for repairs, towing and transportation. Get written instructions about the inspection and who will pay the shop.

Your own collision coverage may pay for a covered impact, subject to the deductible. Ask whether you can use it while the other driver or policy is being investigated. Uninsured motorist property damage coverage and deductible waivers have separate conditions. The California Department of Insurance notes an identification requirement for uninsured motorist property damage coverage. Check the policy before expecting that benefit in an unknown-driver incident. [California Department of Insurance’s auto coverage guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

Sometimes a driver finds a broken mirror or a scrape and cannot tell whether it was a vehicle impact, vandalism or something else. Describe the facts and the evidence you have. Let the insurer explain which coverage it is evaluating and why. If it denies a benefit, request the policy language and the factual basis in writing.

### Why it helps to document damage early

Imagine the rear quarter panel is dented and you need the car to get to work. You have one photo from the parking lot and a store receipt. A repair shop discovers a damaged sensor several days later. The initial photo and repair supplement together show the progression of the investigation. Ask the shop to document when it found the hidden damage and why it believes the damage belongs to this incident.

If you need to drive the car before an inspection, check whether it is safe first and follow any insurer instructions. Save receipts for towing or transportation and ask which charges have been authorized. A photograph after the car is moved can still be useful when you explain where the car was originally parked and why it was moved.

## What if you hit someone else’s parked car?

California DMV tells drivers to try to locate the owner. If you cannot find them, leave the required identifying and contact information in a conspicuous place and report the collision to the appropriate law enforcement agency. Save a photo of the note and record when and where you left it. If you are driving a borrowed vehicle, tell the owner and give the insurer the owner and vehicle information it needs. [California DMV collision guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/financial-responsibility-insurance-requirements-and-collisions/)

Check the DMV reporting threshold and deadlines as well. A note, a call to the police, and an insurance report serve different purposes. If you only saw a small scuff, avoid assuming that repairs will cost less than the reporting threshold. Ask how to comply if the extent of the damage is unclear.

If anyone was injured, keep the vehicle claim and injury documents separate. ## How to request and preserve video from a parking lot

Give the property manager the narrowest time window you can support and describe where the vehicle sat. “Row four near the pharmacy entrance, facing the street” can be more useful than “somewhere by the store.” Note whether a camera appears to face the approach, the exit or the parking space. A recording of a vehicle leaving may help identify it even if the impact occurred out of view.

Ask who controls the footage and whether they can preserve the relevant period. A tenant may control one camera and the property owner may control another. If the manager says a request must come from police, an insurer, or another authorized person, record the contact details and pass them along promptly. Keep the time of the request and any reply. A preservation request and a copy of the recording are different outcomes.

If you receive a clip, save it in its original format. Note the time displayed, where the camera was mounted and whether you can verify the clock against a receipt or other record. Avoid sending only an edited still if the motion or timing matters. A short note can point the adjuster to the relevant part while preserving the full recording.

### When the car is part of a shared household

If someone else parked the car, ask when they arrived, which route they used, and whether they noticed damage earlier. Keep their account separate from your own discovery. Give the insurer the owner’s name and the names of the people who can provide information. If a lender or leasing company has an interest in the car, check whether it requires separate notice or approval before repairs. Keep the records together so that the person dealing with the shop can answer the owner’s and insurer’s questions consistently.

If damage to lights, tires or steering makes the car unsafe, ask about towing before driving it. Keep the tow destination and receipt so the carrier can locate the car and confirm which charges were authorized.

When a witness offers a photo, ask when and where it was taken and whether there is an original file. Keep that information with the photo so its timing can be checked later.

## Check the repair payment and the costs you still need to cover

Consider a hypothetical covered collision repair of $2,800 with a $1,000 deductible. If those are the applicable figures and there are no other adjustments, your insurer's repair payment would be $1,800. Ask the shop how it will collect your portion and whether any charges remain outside the approved estimate. The [car insurance deductible guide](/blogs/car-insurance-deductible-after-accident-california) explains how to reconcile the carrier's payment with the amount you actually pay.

If the responsible driver is later identified, ask whether your carrier will pursue recovery and include your deductible. Keep that update separate from the original repair payment. The [subrogation guide](/blogs/what-is-subrogation-car-insurance-california) explains which figures to request if a recovery produces only part of the amount you expected. Finding a driver and collecting money from that driver or an insurer are separate steps.

If you need transportation while the car is inspected or repaired, confirm the approval before reserving a rental. Ask for the covered daily amount, the total allowance and the last authorized day. A shop's revised completion estimate does not establish approval for more rental days. Keep that conversation with the [rental reimbursement checklist](/blogs/rental-car-reimbursement-after-accident-california), especially if you rely on the car for trips between Buena Park, other Orange County communities and Los Angeles County.

If a renewal later shows the wrong accident description, keep the parked-car evidence with the notice. The [insurance rate guide](/blogs/how-accidents-affect-car-insurance-rates-california) explains how to distinguish an accident rating entry from a price change for other reasons. Ask which record the insurer used instead of assuming the repair payment tells you how it rated the event.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I find the damage the next day?

Record when you found it and when you last saw the car undamaged. Give the insurer and reporting agency the best time window you can support. Save parking receipts, camera leads and any earlier photographs.

### Will I have to pay a deductible if my car was parked?

You may owe the applicable deductible if your own collision coverage pays. If the other carrier pays under liability coverage, the deductible on your collision policy generally does not apply to that payment. Ask your insurer about possible recovery if it handles the repair.

### Can a parking lot hit-and-run be covered without knowing the driver?

Check your collision coverage and the facts of the loss. Other benefits may have different requirements, including identifying an uninsured driver. Ask the insurer which policy conditions apply and what documentation it needs.

### Do I need a police report before opening a claim?

You can usually notify your insurer with the information you have. Ask about separate legal reporting duties and any requirements for the particular coverage. Keep the agency and incident number if you file a report.

### What if the parked vehicle was a motorcycle?

Preserve the parking position, damage, time window and witness information in the same way. Tell the insurer that the damaged vehicle is a motorcycle and identify the policy listing that bike. The [California motorcycle insurance guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-insurance) explains how to check physical damage, equipment and rider-related benefits before assuming a car policy provides the same protection.
