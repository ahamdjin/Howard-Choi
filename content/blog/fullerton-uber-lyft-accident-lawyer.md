## Build a trip timeline before comparing insurance limits

Save the ride receipt, the pickup and drop-off times, and any messages in the app. Our [California rideshare insurance guide](/blogs/uber-lyft-accident-insurance-california) explains how to organize those records for a coverage review. Mark the time of the crash separately. If the impact happened near pickup, the difference between an accepted request and a passenger already in the car may matter. Give the insurer or attorney the underlying records and ask which policy provision applies to that phase.

## What to do after a rideshare accident in Fullerton

Start with safety and medical attention. Call emergency services when someone is injured or there is an immediate hazard. Tell medical professionals what happened and describe your symptoms accurately, including symptoms that develop after you leave the scene.

If you can do so safely, preserve information that connects the collision to the rideshare trip.

- Save screenshots of the trip, driver profile, receipt, pickup location, and messages.
- Photograph vehicle damage, road conditions, traffic controls, and visible injuries.
- Exchange contact and insurance information with the drivers involved.
- Obtain witness contact details and the collision report number, if available.
- Keep medical bills, treatment records, wage-loss documentation, and transportation receipts.

Report the collision through the appropriate app and notify relevant insurers as required by applicable policies. An app report does not replace a police report, insurance claim, or lawsuit. Keep copies of submissions and correspondence. Evidence and the correct identification of defendants are important parts of preparing a personal injury case. ## How Uber and Lyft insurance phases work

Insurance depends on what the driver was doing when the collision occurred. California regulators distinguish between waiting for a request, traveling to pick up a passenger after accepting a request, and transporting that passenger. The California Public Utilities Commission’s page explains those phases, but its legacy uninsured motorist figures should not be relied on as a statement of current 2026 coverage. [CPUC rideshare insurance guidance](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/tncinsurance)

### App off

When a driver is offline and making a personal trip, the driver’s personal auto policy generally becomes the starting point. Rideshare platform coverage ordinarily does not apply simply because the person sometimes drives for Uber or Lyft.

Other coverage may still matter, including insurance held by another at-fault driver or an injured person’s own policy. “App off” does not necessarily mean that only one insurance policy could respond.

### App on and waiting for a request

When the driver is logged in but has not accepted a ride, a different coverage period applies. Do not assume that ordinary personal insurance automatically provides primary coverage. Personal policies may exclude rideshare activity, while a rideshare endorsement or platform-provided coverage may address particular exposures.

The relevant policies need to be reviewed together. Whether a passenger was physically present is not enough to determine which coverage applies.

### Ride accepted and traveling to the pickup

Accepting a ride request changes the insurance analysis before the passenger enters the vehicle. California’s rideshare framework provides $1 million in third-party liability coverage during the accepted-trip period, subject to applicable requirements and policy terms.

That limit is not an automatic settlement amount. Liability coverage addresses covered claims against an insured party. Establishing fault, injury, and damages remains necessary.

### Passenger onboard

Coverage continues during the passenger trip, but liability insurance and uninsured or underinsured motorist insurance serve different purposes.

Liability coverage responds to covered harm caused by an insured party. Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, often called UM/UIM, may apply when a responsible motorist has no insurance or insufficient insurance, subject to policy conditions. Collision coverage concerns vehicle damage and may involve eligibility requirements and a deductible. [California Department of Insurance auto insurance guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

## What changed for California rideshare insurance in 2026?

California’s SB 371 reduced rideshare UM/UIM requirements. Older articles describing a blanket $1 million UM/UIM requirement should not be used to evaluate a 2026 crash without checking the current law and policy. [California Senate transportation legislation summary](https://stran.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-11/2025-legislative-bill-summary-transportation.pdf)

Uber states that, beginning January 1, 2026, its California coverage includes $1 million in third-party liability coverage and UM/UIM limits of $60,000 per person and $300,000 per accident. These are separate coverages, not interchangeable limits. Uber’s announcement should not automatically be treated as proof of the terms of every Lyft policy. [Uber’s California insurance reform announcement](https://www.uber.com/us/en/newsroom/california-insurance-reform/)

**Hypothetical example.** An uninsured driver strikes an Uber carrying a passenger. Uber’s advertised $1 million third-party liability coverage does not automatically provide $1 million for that passenger’s uninsured motorist claim. The applicable UM/UIM policy, crash date, covered-person definition, and claim requirements need separate review.

For either platform, obtain the policy and endorsements in effect on the collision date rather than relying solely on a current website summary.

## Who may be responsible for the crash?

Potentially responsible parties may include the rideshare driver, another motorist, or another person or entity whose conduct contributed to the collision. A dangerous roadway or defective vehicle component may raise additional issues, but those possibilities require supporting evidence.

Uber or Lyft is not automatically vicariously liable for every act of a driver using its app. A claim against a platform requires an applicable legal basis and supporting facts. Access to platform-provided insurance is also a different question from whether the platform itself is legally liable.

[California comparative fault](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) can reduce damages when an injured person shares responsibility. It does not guarantee recovery. For example, evidence that both drivers contributed to a collision may affect fault allocation and the claims available to an injured passenger. Identifying defendants requires more than identifying the company named in the app.

## Compensation that may be available

### Economic damages

[Economic damages](/blogs/how-much-is-my-personal-injury-case-worth-california) address proven financial losses. Depending on the claim, they may include medical treatment, rehabilitation, lost earnings, reduced earning capacity, property damage, and reasonably necessary future care.

Useful documentation can include invoices, wage records, employer statements, and medical opinions. A future expense needs support showing why it is reasonably expected and how it relates to the collision.

### Non-economic damages

Non-economic damages address harms such as physical pain, emotional distress, and loss of enjoyment of life. Records describing sleep disruption, mobility limitations, or difficulty performing ordinary activities can help explain an injury’s practical effects.

Persistent anxiety should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional rather than treated only as a claim item. A family member’s separate claim for relationship-related harm, if available, has its own legal requirements. [National Institute of Mental Health information on anxiety disorders](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders)

### Punitive damages

Punitive damages are not routine compensation for a serious crash. California Civil Code section 3294 requires clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud, or malice. Additional requirements apply when seeking punitive damages against an employer for an employee’s conduct.

An allegation of careless driving does not, by itself, establish entitlement to punitive damages. [California Civil Code section 3294](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3294.&lawCode=CIV)

### Fatal collisions

A fatal rideshare collision may involve a [wrongful-death claim](/blogs/buena-park-wrongful-death-attorney), a survival action, or both. These are distinct claims with different rules concerning who may bring them and which damages are available. A family should not assume that every loss recoverable in an injury case carries over unchanged after a death.

## Filing deadlines require individual review

California personal injury and wrongful-death claims often begin with a two-year general filing rule, but that is not a universal deadline. Claims involving government entities may require a much earlier administrative claim. Discovery rules, minors, medical malpractice, and other circumstances can change the analysis.

Insurance notice obligations may also require prompt action. Reporting a crash in the app or negotiating with an insurer generally does not substitute for timely legal filings. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I bring a claim if I was a rideshare passenger?

Potentially. A claim may involve the rideshare driver, another driver, or multiple parties. Passenger status does not determine fault or guarantee that a particular policy will pay. Preserve the trip receipt and information about every vehicle involved.

### Does Uber or Lyft always provide $1 million in coverage?

No. Coverage depends on the driver’s activity, the type of claim, the crash date, and the policy. The $1 million third-party liability limit should not be confused with UM/UIM coverage or treated as a promised payment.

### What if the driver was waiting for a ride request?

That is a separate insurance period from an accepted trip. The driver’s personal policy may contain rideshare exclusions. Platform coverage and any rideshare endorsement should be reviewed rather than assuming personal insurance is primary.

### What if an uninsured driver caused the collision?

UM/UIM coverage may be relevant, but coverage eligibility and limits require review. Our [uninsured-driver and hit-and-run claims guide](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims) explains the separate coverage and evidence questions. California’s 2026 changes mean older descriptions may be inaccurate. Obtain the policy covering the trip and check any other potentially applicable insurance.

### Can I recover damages if I was partly at fault?

Possibly. California comparative fault can reduce damages according to the injured person’s share of responsibility. It does not establish liability or guarantee payment. Evidence about how the collision occurred remains essential.

### Should I accept the insurer’s first settlement offer?

Evaluate it against documented losses, unresolved medical needs, disputed liability, and available coverage. A settlement commonly requires a release of claims. Understand what rights you are giving up before signing, particularly if your prognosis is uncertain.

### How soon should I speak with a lawyer?

Promptly, especially if injuries are significant, coverage is disputed, or a government entity may be involved. Early advice can help preserve evidence and identify deadlines. Do not assume that ongoing insurance discussions extend the time to file a lawsuit.
