But there is one problem.

When you try to sell or trade in your car, buyers may offer less because it has an accident history.

Even a properly repaired vehicle can be worth less than a similar car that has never been in a crash.

This loss is known as diminished value.

In California, you may be able to recover diminished value from the driver responsible for the accident. However, payment is not automatic. You need to show that your vehicle lost market value because of the crash. Your right to compensation depends on the evidence, the circumstances of the accident, and applicable law.

This guide explains how diminished value claims work in California, how to estimate your vehicle's lost value, what evidence you need, and how to handle an insurance claim.

## What Is a Diminished Value Claim in California?

A diminished value claim is a request for compensation when a vehicle loses market value because of an accident.

The difference is not always visible.

A vehicle may have new parts, matching paint, and properly repaired mechanical components. But its accident history can still affect how much someone is willing to pay for it.

For example, imagine two identical used cars.

Both are the same model, year, and mileage. Both are in good working condition.

One has never been involved in an accident. The other was damaged in a collision and professionally repaired.

A buyer may prefer the car with no accident history or offer less for the repaired vehicle.

That difference in market value is what a diminished value claim seeks to address.

In California, these claims are generally considered part of the broader rules governing compensation for property damage.

## Is Diminished Value Recoverable in California?

Yes, California law recognizes that damage to personal property may reduce its value even after repairs.

California Civil Jury Instruction 3903J explains how damages to personal property can be calculated. [CACI 3903J, reproduced by Justia](https://www.justia.com/trials-litigation/docs/caci/3900/3903j/)

In appropriate circumstances, a person may recover reasonable repair costs and the remaining reduction in value after repairs.

However, there are important limits.

The amount must be supported by evidence, and the legal rules are different depending on whether you are making a claim against another driver or your own insurance company.

### Third Party Diminished Value Claims

A third party claim is made against the person responsible for damaging your vehicle, often through that person's liability insurance company.

Suppose another driver runs a red light and damages your car.

Their insurance company pays for repairs, but the vehicle remains worth less than it was before the collision.

You may be able to seek compensation for the remaining loss in value.

California law allows property damage claims based on proven losses. However, insurers may challenge claims based only on a vehicle's accident history, particularly when repairs fully restored its condition.

That is why a clear, independent valuation can be important.

### First Party Diminished Value Claims

A first party claim is made under your own insurance policy.

For example, you might use your collision coverage to repair your car after an accident.

In California, standard collision coverage generally does not require insurers to pay for a vehicle's remaining market depreciation after proper repairs.

Courts have upheld policy terms limiting this type of recovery.

Your policy language matters. If you believe additional coverage applies, review the contract and ask your insurer for a written explanation.

### First Party vs. Third Party Claims

| Feature | First party claim | Third party claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who receives the claim? | Your insurance company | Responsible driver or their liability insurer |
| Coverage basis | Your insurance contract | Legal responsibility for property damage |
| Diminished value recovery | Generally excluded under standard collision coverage | May be recoverable when supported by law and evidence |
| Main issue | Policy language | Fault, causation, and proven loss |
| Supporting documents | Policy, repairs, valuation | Liability evidence, repairs, market valuation |

Understanding this difference before filing can save time and prevent unnecessary disputes.

## What Are the Three Types of Diminished Value?

Not every vehicle loses value for the same reason.

There are three commonly discussed types of diminished value in the automotive appraisal industry.

### 1. Inherent Diminished Value

Inherent diminished value is the reduction in a vehicle's resale value simply because it has been involved in an accident.

This can happen even when repairs are completed correctly.

For example, a buyer might offer less for a repaired luxury sedan after discovering that it previously sustained significant collision damage.

The vehicle may function normally, but the accident history affects its market appeal.

Inherent diminished value is frequently discussed in insurance disputes. Whether a particular loss is legally recoverable depends on the facts and applicable rules.

### 2. Immediate Diminished Value

Immediate diminished value is the difference between a vehicle's market value just before an accident and its value immediately afterward, before repairs.

For example, a car worth $30,000 before a collision may be worth only $18,000 in its damaged condition.

The immediate reduction is $12,000.

However, this does not mean the owner is automatically owed $12,000 in addition to repair costs.

California's property damage rules are intended to compensate proven losses without duplicating the same damage.

### 3. Repair Related Diminished Value

Repair related diminished value occurs when completed repairs leave a vehicle with problems that reduce its market value.

Examples include:

Paint that does not match the rest of the vehicle.

Poorly aligned body panels.

Remaining mechanical problems.

Unresolved structural issues.

Visible signs of earlier damage.

Some problems may need additional repairs rather than a separate diminished value payment.

If a body shop has not restored the vehicle correctly, it is important to document the problem before accepting the repair as complete.

## How Much Is a Diminished Value Claim Worth in California?

There is no fixed California percentage that determines every diminished value claim.

The amount depends on the vehicle and the circumstances of the accident.

Important factors include:

The vehicle's market value before the crash.

Its age, mileage, and general condition.

The type and severity of collision damage.

Whether repairs were completed properly.

Its previous accident and repair history.

The resale market for comparable vehicles.

Whether the accident caused a measurable reduction in value.

A newer vehicle with low mileage may suffer a larger dollar loss than an older vehicle with extensive previous damage.

But no single factor determines the result.

### Example of a Diminished Value Calculation

Imagine you owned a car with the following values:

| Valuation factor | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Market value before the accident | $30,000 |
| Reasonable repair cost | $6,000 |
| Market value after repairs | $27,500 |
| Remaining diminished value | $2,500 |

The calculation is straightforward:

$30,000 minus $27,500 = $2,500

In this example, the vehicle has a potential remaining loss in market value of $2,500 after repairs.

The repair cost is separate from that remaining depreciation.

However, this is an illustration, not an automatic insurance payout. The valuations must be supported by credible evidence, and California's damages rules limit the total amount that can be awarded.

A professional appraisal can help determine whether the estimated loss reflects actual market conditions.

## How to Calculate Diminished Value in California

Several methods are used to estimate how much value a vehicle has lost.

Some are based on market evidence. Others use formulas.

### Method 1: Compare Market Values Before and After Repairs

One approach is to estimate what the car was worth immediately before the collision and what it would reasonably sell for after repairs.

The difference gives an estimated remaining loss.

For example:

Pre accident value: $25,000

Post repair value: $22,000

Estimated diminished value: $3,000

This approach works best when both valuations use reliable market evidence.

The estimates should account for the vehicle's mileage, features, condition, location, and accident history.

### Method 2: Use a Professional Vehicle Appraisal

An independent automobile appraiser may provide a more detailed evaluation.

The appraiser can examine:

Repair invoices and damage photographs.

Vehicle history reports.

The type of damage sustained.

Current vehicle condition.

Local market listings and comparable sales.

Market adjustments supported by available data.

An appraisal should explain how the final amount was reached.

A report that simply provides a dollar figure without showing the supporting evidence may be difficult to defend.

### Method 3: Understand the 17c Formula

The 17c formula is a diminished value calculation method associated with an insurance settlement in Georgia.

It generally starts with a percentage of a vehicle's value and applies adjustments for damage severity and mileage.

Some insurers and valuation services use versions of this approach.

However, California law does not establish the 17c formula as a mandatory method for calculating diminished value.

A formula may produce a useful preliminary estimate, but it does not replace evidence of what a vehicle is actually worth in the relevant market.

If an insurer uses a formula, ask how the calculation was performed and what information supports the result.

### California Diminished Value Calculator: Example

The following example shows how a simple market value calculation works.

| Vehicle detail | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle | 2022 midsize sedan |
| Mileage | 35,000 miles |
| Pre accident market value | $26,000 |
| Accident damage | Rear collision |
| Repair cost | $4,500 |
| Estimated post repair market value | $23,800 |
| Estimated diminished value | $2,200 |

Calculation: $26,000 − $23,800 = $2,200

These figures are hypothetical.

They do not represent the actual resale value of a particular make or model.

Before using an estimate in an insurance claim, compare it against reliable local market data.

## How to File a Diminished Value Claim in California

If you are still organizing the crash records, the [California after-crash checklist](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california) explains which scene, vehicle, and insurer documents to preserve.

Filing a diminished value claim requires more than telling an insurance company that your car is worth less.

You need a clear explanation and documents supporting the amount requested.

### Step 1: Confirm Who Was Responsible for the Accident

Start by determining who caused the crash.

For a third party claim, evidence that another driver was legally responsible is important.

Useful information may include:

Police reports.

Photographs of the accident scene.

Witness statements.

Insurance investigation findings.

Available video footage.

A police report can provide useful information, but it does not always make a final legal determination of fault.

California follows comparative fault principles. If more than one driver was responsible, the amount recoverable may be affected.

### Step 2: Complete the Necessary Vehicle Repairs

For a claim based on remaining loss in value after repairs, it is often helpful to finish repairs first.

That allows you to document the vehicle's actual condition.

Keep every repair invoice, estimate, and related document.

If repairs are incomplete or defective, address those issues separately so the valuation does not confuse repair problems with remaining market depreciation.

### Step 3: Determine the Vehicle's Pre Accident Value

Find out what your vehicle was reasonably worth immediately before the collision.

You can begin by researching comparable vehicles with similar:

Make and model.

Model year.

Mileage.

Trim level.

Features.

Overall condition.

Geographic market.

Resources such as Kelley Blue Book and vehicle listings can help establish a starting point.

However, asking prices are not always the same as completed sale prices.

For a larger claim, an appraiser may provide stronger evidence.

### Step 4: Estimate the Value After Repairs

Next, determine what the vehicle is worth with its repaired accident history.

Use comparable sales or professional valuation evidence where available.

You may also request a written trade in estimate from a dealership.

A single dealership quote may not be enough by itself. Several independent sources can provide a more balanced estimate.

### Step 5: Organize Your Evidence

Before contacting the insurance company, organize the records supporting your claim.

| Document | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Accident report | Helps document how the collision happened |
| Repair invoices | Shows the damage repaired and the cost |
| Damage photographs | Supports the description of the original loss |
| Vehicle history report | Documents reported accident history |
| Independent appraisal | Provides a reasoned market value estimate |
| Comparable vehicle data | Supports the requested valuation |
| Insurance correspondence | Records previous offers, decisions, and explanations |

A strong claim connects the accident, the repairs, and the remaining market loss.

### Step 6: Submit a Written Diminished Value Claim

Once the evidence is ready, submit the claim to the appropriate insurer.

Include the claim number, vehicle information, accident date, requested amount, and supporting documents.

Explain how the loss was calculated.

Keep a copy of everything you submit.

A written submission makes it easier to track what the insurer received and how the claim was handled.

### Step 7: Review the Insurance Company's Response

The insurance company may:

Accept the requested amount.

Request additional documents.

Offer a lower payment.

Dispute the valuation.

Reject the claim.

If you receive a lower offer, ask for a written explanation.

Compare the insurer's reasoning with the evidence supporting your own estimate.

### Step 8: Negotiate or Consider Further Action

If the insurer disputes the amount, additional market evidence or a professional appraisal may help.

You can also consider whether legal assistance is appropriate.

For smaller disputes, California small claims court may be an option.

The best approach depends on the amount at issue, the cost of obtaining more evidence, and the strength of the claim.

## What If the Insurance Company Denies Your Diminished Value Claim?

A denial does not necessarily mean that the insurer's valuation is correct.

However, it also does not mean every denied claim is valid.

First, identify why the insurer refused payment.

Common reasons include:

### The Insurance Policy Excludes Diminished Value

If the claim is against your own collision policy, the contract may exclude post repair depreciation.

California courts have generally enforced clear limitations of this kind.

### The Insurer Disputes the Amount

The insurer may believe the market value difference is smaller than you claimed.

Request the valuation method and the evidence supporting its calculation.

### The Insurer Says the Repairs Restored the Vehicle

This is a particularly important issue for claims involving inherent diminished value.

An insurer may argue that properly completed repairs restored the vehicle and that any remaining loss is based only on buyer perceptions.

California law does not guarantee payment for every claimed loss associated with an accident record.

You may need credible evidence of a remaining loss that is legally compensable.

### There Is Insufficient Evidence

A general statement that repaired vehicles sell for less may not establish how much value your particular vehicle lost.

A detailed appraisal and comparable vehicle information can help address this issue.

You can request a review of the insurer's valuation and submit additional evidence.

Start by asking the adjuster for a written explanation.

If there are mistakes in the vehicle's mileage, model, prior condition, or damage history, explain them and provide supporting documents.

If your dispute concerns an insurer's handling of the claim, the California Department of Insurance provides consumer assistance and a complaint process.

Some disputes must ultimately be addressed through negotiation or court proceedings.

## How Long Do You Have to File a Diminished Value Claim in California?

Our [California filing-deadline guide](/blogs/california-personal-injury-deadlines) explains why property damage, injuries, insurance notice, and government claims may require separate deadline reviews.

California Code of Civil Procedure Section 338(c)(1) generally provides a three year limitation period for lawsuits involving damage to personal property.

For an ordinary vehicle damage claim arising from a collision, this period will commonly run from the accident date.

For example, if an accident occurred on March 15, 2026, an ordinary three year period would generally reach March 15, 2029.

But this example should not be treated as a guaranteed deadline in every case.

Claims involving government entities, contractual insurance rights, or unusual circumstances may have different notice requirements or time limits.

An insurance claim and a lawsuit are also different things.

Reporting a loss to an insurer does not necessarily stop the legal filing deadline.

For that reason, it is best to report the claim promptly and confirm any applicable deadlines before they expire.

## How Long Does a Diminished Value Insurance Claim Take?

There is no fixed processing period for every diminished value dispute.

A straightforward claim with clear evidence may be resolved more quickly than one involving disputed liability or valuation.

California's Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations establish general requirements for insurance claim handling.

For example, insurers generally must acknowledge a claim and begin investigating within 15 calendar days after receiving notice.

They generally must accept or deny a claim within 40 calendar days after receiving proof of claim, subject to regulatory provisions for continuing investigations.

These requirements should not be confused with a promise that every diminished value claim will be paid within 40 days.

If additional investigation is necessary, an insurer may require more time while following applicable notice requirements.

## Can You File a Diminished Value Claim If You Were Partly at Fault?

Possibly.

California uses a [comparative fault system for negligence claims](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury).

If another person contributed to the accident, your percentage of responsibility can affect the amount of damages you recover.

Imagine that a vehicle has a proven diminished value loss of $3,000.

If the owner is found 25% responsible for the collision, the recovery attributable to that loss might be reduced to $2,250.

The actual result depends on the legal issues, available evidence, insurance coverage, and any applicable limits.

If you were entirely responsible for the accident, you generally cannot recover damages from another driver who bears no legal responsibility.

Your own collision policy may also exclude diminished value.

## Can You File a Diminished Value Claim After a Total Loss?

A remaining loan balance raises a separate issue; the [GAP insurance guide](/blogs/insurance-how-does-gap-insurance-work) explains the difference between a loan shortfall and a repaired vehicle’s remaining loss of value.

Diminished value claims usually concern vehicles that have been repaired but remain worth less because of the accident.

When a vehicle is declared a total loss, the valuation question is different.

The insurer typically determines the vehicle's actual cash value immediately before the loss, subject to the applicable coverage and settlement rules.

In a total loss situation, there is generally no repaired vehicle with a separate remaining reduction in value to claim.

If you believe the total loss settlement undervalues your vehicle, the more relevant issue may be challenging the insurer's pre accident valuation.

## Can You File a Claim for a Leased or Financed Vehicle?

It depends on who owns the vehicle and who has the legal right to recover the loss.

A financed car is commonly owned by the borrower, subject to the lender's security interest.

A leased vehicle is generally owned by the leasing company.

The lease or financing agreement may affect who is entitled to payment and how an insurance settlement is handled.

Before submitting a diminished value claim involving a leased vehicle, review the agreement and confirm who has the right to pursue the loss.

## Is It Worth Filing a Diminished Value Claim for an Older Car?

An older vehicle can sometimes lose additional market value after an accident.

However, the amount may be relatively small, particularly when the car already had high mileage, previous damage, or a low resale value.

Consider the cost of proving the claim.

For example, paying for an expensive appraisal may not make financial sense if the potential remaining loss is small.

A useful first step is to compare the vehicle's value before the accident with supported estimates of its value after repairs.

If the evidence suggests a meaningful difference, a more detailed appraisal may be worthwhile.

California small claims court may be an option for certain disputed vehicle property damage claims.

It is important to identify the correct defendant, prepare supporting evidence, and meet the filing and service requirements.

The small claims process is not necessarily the right option for every insurance dispute.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Filing a Diminished Value Claim

Several mistakes can make a claim harder to evaluate.

### Relying Only on Online Estimates

Online vehicle pricing tools can provide a starting point, but they may not fully account for the effect of your particular accident.

Use multiple sources and explain the assumptions behind the valuation.

### Assuming Every Accident Creates the Same Loss

Two vehicles with similar repair bills may have very different changes in market value.

Age, model, previous damage, and local demand all matter.

### Filing Against the Wrong Insurance Coverage

Your own collision policy may not cover diminished value after proper repairs.

Confirm whether you are making a first party insurance claim or pursuing a legally responsible driver.

### Accepting a Settlement Without Reviewing Its Terms

An insurance settlement may include a release of certain claims.

Before signing, check whether the document applies only to repairs or also includes remaining property damage and other potential claims.

### Waiting Too Long

Evidence can become harder to collect as time passes.

Vehicle listings change, witnesses may be difficult to contact, and legal deadlines continue to run.

Prompt documentation makes it easier to evaluate a claim.

## Final Thoughts

A repaired car is not always worth as much as it was before an accident.

That difference can matter when you decide to sell, trade in, or keep the vehicle.

California law provides a way to seek compensation for certain proven losses in market value, but the result depends on the facts of the accident, the available evidence, and the legal basis for the claim.

Start by understanding which insurer is involved, documenting the repairs, and obtaining a reasonable valuation.

If the amount is disputed or the legal issues are complicated, a California attorney can help you understand your options and evaluate whether pursuing the claim is worthwhile.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Diminished Value Claims in California

### Can you claim diminished value in California?

Yes. California law allows compensation for certain proven losses in vehicle value caused by another person's wrongful conduct. Whether a specific claim is recoverable depends on the evidence and applicable law.

### Does California require insurance companies to pay diminished value?

California does not require every insurer to automatically pay diminished value. Recovery depends on the nature of the claim, liability, policy provisions, and proof of loss.

### Can I claim diminished value from my own insurance company?

Generally, standard collision insurance policies in California do not cover remaining market depreciation after proper repairs. Review your policy for any applicable coverage.

### How is diminished value calculated in California?

One common estimate compares the car's market value immediately before the accident with its market value after repairs. A professional appraisal can help support the figures.

### Is the 17c formula required in California?

No. California law does not require the 17c formula. Market evidence and applicable property damage law are important when determining a recoverable amount.

### Do I have to sell my car to prove diminished value?

Not necessarily. A professional appraisal and credible market evidence can help establish an estimated loss even if you keep the vehicle.

### What if my car was already damaged before the accident?

Previous accidents and existing damage can affect the valuation. The claim should focus on the additional loss caused by the accident in question.

### How long do I have to sue for diminished value in California?

The general statute of limitations for damage to personal property is three years. Different deadlines or notice rules may apply in some situations.

### Can I make a diminished value claim on a leased car?

Possibly, but the leasing company may own the vehicle and hold the relevant property rights. Check the lease agreement before pursuing payment.

### What if the insurance company refuses to pay?

Ask for a written explanation, review the evidence, and consider an independent appraisal. Depending on the circumstances, you may also seek legal advice or pursue an appropriate court claim.
