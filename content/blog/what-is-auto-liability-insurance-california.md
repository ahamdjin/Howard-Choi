## What bodily injury and property damage coverage each do

Bodily injury liability is the coverage an insurer looks at when someone says an insured driver caused their injuries. Medical costs and lost income are examples of losses that can be part of an injury claim. What the insurer pays depends on legal responsibility, the facts and documentation, and the available coverage. An insurer may defend an insured against a covered lawsuit. If you receive one, send the papers to the insurer immediately and record when you were served.

Property damage liability addresses a different loss. It may pay for another person’s damaged vehicle or a fence, storefront, or other property struck in a crash. The property damage limit applies separately from the injury limit. Ask what happens when several vehicles are damaged. A policy limit that would easily cover one bumper repair may be insufficient for a pileup with towing, repairs, and more than one total loss.

Coverage for the insured driver’s own damaged car usually requires a separate provision, such as collision. Injuries to the driver or passengers may raise questions about medical payments, health insurance, or uninsured motorist benefits. Look up the appropriate coverage instead of looking for a single “full coverage” line. The [California at-fault insurance guide](/blogs/is-california-a-no-fault-state-or-an-at-fault-state) explains how coverage and responsibility interact.

## How the numbers work in a multi-person crash

Consider a minimum-limits policy and a crash with three injured people. Each has documented losses that might exceed $30,000. Even though each person is subject to a $30,000 individual limit, the combined bodily injury limit is $60,000 for the crash. The insurer does not have $90,000 available by multiplying the individual cap by three. How that $60,000 might be distributed depends on the claims, the facts and any settlement arrangements.

Suppose the same crash damages two parked cars. Those claims are evaluated under the separate property damage limit. Paying one injury claim does not automatically reduce what is available in the property damage column. Likewise, an unused portion of the property limit does not ordinarily increase the bodily injury limit.

Now imagine a fourth person says they were hurt but has submitted no records yet. Ask whether the insurer is evaluating additional claims under the same limit. This can affect the timing and structure of negotiations. It does not establish anyone’s injury value or mean that accepting the first offer is the only option. The [California settlement timeline guide](/blogs/how-long-car-accident-settlement-takes-california) describes why multi-party investigations take time.

## Why the minimum is only a starting point

California DMV lists the standard minimums. It also recognizes other ways to meet financial responsibility requirements. The California Department of Insurance describes a separate Low Cost Automobile Insurance program for eligible drivers. Ask what policy was in force on the collision date and read its declarations. An insurance card can identify a carrier and policy number, but it rarely answers all questions about the limits that apply to this crash. A limit caps what a particular coverage can pay. It does not set the value of the injury or erase any potentially recoverable loss above that amount. If bills and other losses exceed an apparent limit, identify which other sources of recovery might apply before making a decision. The crash might involve another responsible driver, a vehicle owner, an employer, or applicable underinsured motorist coverage. Each requires its own facts and coverage analysis.

Drivers reviewing their own policy can ask an agent to price several limits while keeping the other coverages comparable. The amount shown on a quote means little if the second quote also changed the driver list, deductible, use of the car, or uninsured motorist limits. Compare declarations line by line. Ask how occasional borrowers, household drivers, and vehicles used for work are treated. The [borrowed-car coverage guide](/blogs/does-car-insurance-follow-the-car-or-driver-california) explains which owner, driver and trip records help answer those questions.

## What to check before accepting a policy-limits offer

If you are seeking payment from another driver’s insurer, the [third-party claim guide](/blogs/third-party-car-insurance-claim-california) explains how the investigation proceeds. Request a clear written description of the offer. Find out which policy and coverage it comes from, who would be released, and which claims would be closed. If the offer involves bodily injury, check whether it leaves a separate vehicle claim open. If the claim involves several injured people, ask whether the offer depends on everyone agreeing to a distribution.

Put your own coverages beside the offer. Confirm whether you purchased uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, who qualifies as an insured, and what conditions apply before a settlement with another driver. A settlement can affect rights under your own policy, so review notice and consent requirements before signing. The [uninsured and underinsured driver guide](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims) covers the documents that help with this review.

Keep a separate running list of medical bills, payments already made, and any reimbursement claims. A settlement’s face amount does not necessarily tell you the amount you will receive after obligations are resolved. Ask which bills remain outstanding and whether any provider or plan has asserted a claim against the proceeds. Avoid counting the same loss twice when assembling a demand.

## When there is disagreement about fault or coverage

If the insurer denies coverage, ask whether it disputes that the driver was insured, that the vehicle or use was covered, or that the policy was active. A denial based on an excluded driver calls for a different response from a denial based on a lapse in coverage. Get the relevant document and the insurer’s reasoning in writing. Keep the crash evidence available even if coverage is currently the main question.

If the insurer accepts that there was coverage but assigns you part of the fault, ask what facts support its position. A damage photograph, camera recording or witness account may help clarify the sequence. The [comparative fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) explains how shared responsibility affects a California injury claim. You can bring the insurer’s letters, coverage information and evidence to the firm’s [car accident practice](/practice-areas/car-accidents) for an injury claim review.

### What to keep in a coverage file

Record the date of the crash, vehicles involved, people injured, and the claim numbers for each carrier. Save the actual declarations and endorsements whenever you can get them. Put offers and releases in date order, and write down any stated deadline. Track property and injury payments separately. If you are asked to sign a release, keep the proposed version and any later changes so you can compare which people and losses are included.

## How to read an insurance declaration after a crash

Find the policy dates and vehicle description before you look at dollar figures. If there is a revised declarations page, check its effective date. A policy renewed after the collision may have different limits. Verify the insured driver and vehicle identifiers, then find the bodily injury and property damage entries. Ask for the policy wording if the insurer says a limit applies only in a particular situation.

Some policies express liability as a combined single limit. Others split it between per-person injury, per-crash injury and property damage. Ask which format you have and how the company reads it. Comparing two numbers without knowing which limits they represent can make a smaller policy look bigger than it is.

If the driver was making a delivery or using an employer’s vehicle, ask what other policies may apply. A company vehicle might be listed on a commercial policy and its use may need to be confirmed. A delivery platform might maintain separate insurance that depends on the work status at the time. Keep the trip, assignment, or app records so that the claim is evaluated for the correct moment. An insurer’s initial statement about a personal policy may leave those other questions unanswered.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does liability insurance pay to fix my own car?

Liability coverage generally addresses covered losses to other people. To evaluate damage to your own vehicle, check collision coverage and whether another party’s liability coverage may respond. Ask which deductible or coverage limit applies to each route.

### Are the minimum limits guaranteed payouts?

No. The limits are maximums under the relevant coverage. The amount paid depends on documented losses, legal responsibility and the policy. The same limit can also be shared among multiple claimants.

### Is the amount on the insurance card the full policy information?

Use the card to identify the insurer and policy. Ask for declarations and any applicable endorsements. A separate endorsement may affect who is covered or how the insurer treats a vehicle’s use.

### What if the other driver’s limit does not cover my medical bills?

Get the coverage information and review potential claims against other responsible parties. Check your own insurance for underinsured motorist benefits and any applicable settlement requirements. An injury claim review can help determine which sources are available and what evidence is needed.
