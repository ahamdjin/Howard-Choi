## What Is a Digital License Plate?

A digital license plate is an electronic display that replaces the traditional metal tag on your car. Pioneered by Sacramento-based company Reviver, these plates use e-ink screens (similar to Kindle displays) and connect to your vehicle via a built-in cellular network.

You can manage your registration, customize the display, and even track your car’s location through a mobile app.

There are two main types:

- Battery-powered plates (RPLATE): Designed for easy installation, typically on the rear of the vehicle.
- Hardwired plates (RFLEET): Used on commercial or fleet vehicles and connected to the car’s electrical system.

**Important**

Digital license plates are only available to mount on the rear of the vehicle.

## Where Are They Available?

Reviver currently advertises registration availability in California and Arizona. Eligibility to register a product is different from permission to travel with an appropriately registered plate in another jurisdiction. Verify the vehicle, registration state, and applicable rules before ordering. [Manufacturer availability information](https://www.reviver.com/geographic-expansion)

Possible expansion into another state is not the same as current registration approval. Confirm availability through the manufacturer and the relevant motor-vehicle agency.

California was the first state to approve digital plates for all vehicles through AB-984, setting the framework for registration, use, and security standards. Other states are currently evaluating the technology, weighing the potential convenience benefits against privacy and data security concerns.

## Benefits of Digital License Plates

Digital plates offer several advantages for drivers and businesses, including:

### 1. In-App Registration Renewal

Skip the long DMV lines and sticker mailings. Digital plates can update registration automatically through the Reviver app. This makes it easier to stay compliant and reduces the risk of expired registration tickets.

### 2. Enhanced Security

Some models include GPS tracking, helping locate a stolen vehicle more quickly. Additionally, the plates can send real-time alerts if tampered with, adding an extra layer of protection for your vehicle.

### 3. Customization

Digital plates allow for personalized display options, including dark or light mode and custom banner messages. Owners can display messages such as “STOLEN” or “EXPIRED” when the car is reported stolen or registration lapses, making communication with law enforcement or the public more efficient.

### 4. Fleet Management

Businesses with multiple vehicles can track mileage, status, and location in real time. Additionally, business owners should confirm which display messages and uses are permitted; customization is not unrestricted advertising space.

### 5. Sleek, Modern Design

Digital plates have a clean, high-tech appearance that appeals to tech-savvy drivers and early adopters. They also allow for dynamic updates, which can make a vehicle stand out while maintaining compliance with state regulations.

## Disadvantages of Digital License Plates

Despite the innovation, digital license plates aren’t for everyone.

### 1. High Cost

At the time of review, the manufacturer’s product page lists a $899 purchase price and states that an active service plan is required for continued access to certain features. Do not combine older annual-plan figures with a different monthly product and present them as one current offer. Check the actual checkout, plan, taxes, installation, and renewal terms. [Current product listing](https://www.reviver.com/rplate)

### 2. Privacy and Tracking Concerns

Digital plates connect to cellular networks and can transmit location and registration data. Critics raise concerns about potential misuse of this data by third parties, advertisers, or hackers. Drivers who value privacy may feel uncomfortable with the idea of a continuously connected license plate.

### 3. Security Risks

Like any connected device, digital plates are vulnerable to software bugs, technical glitches, or hacking attempts. While manufacturers implement security measures, no system is completely foolproof, and a compromised plate could allow unauthorized access to vehicle information or location tracking.

### 4. Maintenance and Durability

E-ink screens are designed to last, but extreme weather conditions such as high heat, heavy rain, or snow could damage the display or reduce visibility. Unlike traditional plates, digital plates may require software updates or battery repairs, which can be inconvenient and costly.

## Legal and Privacy Considerations

As with digital driver’s licenses, digital license plates collect and transmit certain data, which raises important questions about ownership, privacy, and data security.

- Data ownership and consent: In California and other participating states, the DMV requires manufacturers to comply with strict data protection standards. However, drivers should carefully review the terms of service to understand who owns the data and how it may be shared with insurers, law enforcement, or third-party vendors.
- Law enforcement access: Law enforcement agencies may use digital license plate data to identify stolen vehicles or confirm registration. While this can improve public safety, it also increases concerns about real-time surveillance and location tracking without a warrant.
- Compliance and liability: If a digital plate malfunctions—such as failing to display the correct number or registration status—the driver is still responsible for ensuring compliance with state vehicle laws. Drivers may face fines if their registration is not properly displayed, even if the error stems from a software issue.
- Consumer protections: California’s DMV and consumer protection agencies continue to evaluate digital plate technology. As the market grows, additional regulations and privacy safeguards are expected to ensure drivers’ data remains secure and use is transparent.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### How much does a digital license plate cost?

Compare the current hardware price with the service plan offered for your registration and product. Include taxes, installation where needed, and the cost of continued service. A dated price in an article is not a guaranteed quote.

### What if I am in an accident and my RPLATE gets damaged?

If your digital license plate is damaged in a [car accident](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california), the repair or replacement may be covered by your insurance policy or the [other party’s insurance](/blogs/is-california-a-no-fault-state-or-an-at-fault-state), depending on the circumstances. It’s recommended to check with your insurer to confirm coverage for digital license plates, as policies may vary.

### Are digital license plates available for motorcycles?

The manufacturer’s current consumer-product FAQ says RPLATE is not available for [motorcycles](/blogs/california-motorcycle-accident-guide). It also identifies exclusions for commercial semi-trucks and trailers. Check the exact product and vehicle category rather than treating consumer and fleet offerings as identical. [Manufacturer eligibility FAQ](https://www.reviver.com/faq)

## The Bottom Line

Digital license plates are a glimpse into the future of connected driving, offering convenience, customization, and advanced tracking. However, the high upfront cost and ongoing security concerns mean they may not yet be worth it for every driver.

## Compare the full ownership cost

Put the offer into a simple budget before ordering. Separate the hardware, any installation work, ongoing service, and possible replacement expenses. Ask which items are mandatory and which are optional. A monthly figure can look inexpensive while obscuring the amount paid over several years.

| Cost question | What to obtain in writing |
|---|---|
| What is included in the initial purchase? | Hardware, mounting components, any included service period, and shipping terms |
| Which service plan will apply? | Billing frequency, renewal price, required features, cancellation terms, and taxes |
| What happens when service ends? | Which functions stop and the steps needed to keep the vehicle legally registered and displayed |
| Who pays for a damaged display? | Warranty exclusions, insurance response, replacement terms, and any installation charge |
| What happens when the car is sold? | Account-transfer or removal instructions and any remaining financial obligation |

**Hypothetical comparison.** One driver buys mainly for appearance and rarely changes registration information. Another manages several eligible vehicles and values a centralized record. The same hardware can have different practical value to those owners. Compare the tasks it actually replaces rather than treating every advertised feature as a benefit you will use.

Keep a dated copy of the offer and the plan terms. If a salesperson makes a promise that does not appear in the contract, ask for clarification before paying. Check return deadlines and any conditions on installation or activation. A return policy for hardware may not answer what happens to a separately purchased service.

## Ask specific privacy questions

Start by identifying which product is being offered and whether the feature you want uses location data. Do not assume every connected plate has the same tracking capabilities, account settings, or fleet controls. Read the applicable privacy notice and ask what data is collected, why it is needed, how long it is retained, and who can access it.

If the vehicle is shared, consider the people whose travel could be revealed. A family member, employee, or later buyer may have different expectations from the account holder. Review notification, permission, and account-transfer requirements rather than relying on a broad claim that the plate is secure. Our [personal injury trends guide](/blogs/state-of-personal-injury-2026-ca-trends) explains related evidence questions involving connected and automated vehicles.

Protect the account as you would another connected vehicle service. Use the available authentication safeguards, keep recovery information current, and know how to contact support if a phone or account is compromised. These steps are practical precautions; they do not guarantee that a device or service cannot fail.

### Before selling or returning the car

Make a checklist covering the physical plate, registration, manufacturer account, and any subscription. Confirm what transfers to the buyer and what must be removed or reassigned. Save the confirmation. Simply deleting the mobile app may not cancel billing or complete a registration change.

For a fleet or employer-provided vehicle, ask who owns the device and which party controls the account. A personal payment toward the hardware does not necessarily determine ownership of the registration or collected data. Resolve that question before a driver changes roles or leaves the business.

## What to preserve if the plate is damaged in a crash

A registration or display problem is different from the driver’s licensing status. The [California unlicensed-driving guide](/blogs/what-happens-if-you-drive-without-a-license-california) explains that distinction. If the vehicle was involved in a collision, the [car accident practice page](/practice-areas/car-accidents) explains the separate injury-claim review.

Photograph the vehicle and plate when safe, and retain the purchase receipt, product identifier, installation record, and relevant service correspondence. Record whether the display remained readable and what changed after the incident. Do not assume a damaged display proves a particular malfunction caused the crash.

Notify the appropriate insurer and ask how the device is treated under the actual policy. A customization, accessory, or electronic component may raise questions different from an ordinary registration fee. Keep the written coverage explanation. Do not discard the damaged item before its relevance to an injury or property claim has been considered. For the separate reporting process, see our [California DMV accident reporting guide](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident); an app notification does not replace a required accident report.

### Does a digital plate eliminate ordinary registration duties?

No. A connected display should not be treated as a substitute for keeping registration, insurance, and required vehicle records in order. Confirm the applicable agency instructions if the display is damaged, the service fails, or the account changes.

### Should I buy based on promised approval in my state?

Wait for confirmation that the specific product and registration are eligible. Marketing about planned expansion does not establish that an agency will accept an application now.
