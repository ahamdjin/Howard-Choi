## What Are the Most Common Causes of Motorcycle Accidents?

Motorcycle accidents occur for a variety of reasons, including human error and environmental hazards. These crashes may result from rider mistakes, negligent drivers, poor road or weather conditions, or mechanical failures. Below, we’ll explore some of the most common causes behind motorcycle collisions.

### Speeding

Riding or driving at excessive speeds reduces reaction time and increases the severity of crashes. Speeding is a leading factor in both single-vehicle and multi-vehicle motorcycle accidents.

### Alcohol and Drug Use

Impaired riding affects balance, coordination, and judgment. Intoxicated riders are significantly more likely to lose control or misjudge traffic situations.

### Left Turns

Many accidents happen when a vehicle turns left in front of an oncoming motorcycle. Drivers often misjudge the rider’s speed or fail to notice them entirely.

### Poor Road Conditions

Potholes, loose gravel, uneven surfaces, or slick paint lines can cause a motorcyclist to lose control more easily than other drivers.

### Distracted Driving

Drivers texting, eating, or using in-car systems may overlook motorcycles, leading to rear-end crashes or lane-change collisions.

### Weather Conditions

Rain, fog, or wind reduces traction and visibility, increasing the risk of crashes. Motorcycles are especially vulnerable due to their reduced tire contact and lack of balance.

### Mechanical Problems

Worn brakes, bald tires, or engine issues can lead to sudden failures. Regular maintenance is crucial for rider safety. In some cases, a motorcycle defect can lead to an accident.

## What to Do After a Motorcycle Accident (Step-by-Step)

If you’ve been involved in a motorcycle accident, the steps you take immediately after can significantly impact your recovery and legal claim. Here’s what to do:

### 1. Ensure Safety First

Make sure everyone is safe and move out of traffic if possible. Call 911 right away to request police and emergency medical responders. A police report is a crucial document that can support your case later.

### 2. Collect Insurance and Vehicle Information

Obtain the other driver’s insurance, license plate number, and contact details. If there are passengers or witnesses, gather their information as well. Even if you feel only mildly hurt, keep in mind that injuries like whiplash or internal trauma can worsen in the hours or days that follow.

### 3. Gather Evidence

Preserve as much evidence as possible.

- Take photos of the accident scene, your damaged motorcycle, any skid marks, weather conditions, and all other vehicles involved.
- Document your visible injuries immediately.
- Do not repair or dispose of your motorcycle—its damaged condition can help accident reconstruction experts determine fault.
- If possible, get the names and badge numbers of the responding officers and EMS personnel.

### 4. Always Seek Medical Treatment

Even if you feel fine, it’s vital to get checked by a medical professional. Follow your doctor’s treatment plan and attend all follow-up appointments. These records are critical proof of your injuries and are often challenged by insurance companies trying to downplay your claim.

Working with an attorney gives you the best chance of recovering the full compensation you deserve. An attorney can protect your rights, deal with insurers, and help build a strong case based on evidence and legal strategy.

## Overview

### 4. Common Motorcycle Accident Injuries

Despite significant advancements in motorcycle technology and safety features, injuries and fatalities from motorcycle crashes remain alarmingly high. These incidents often result in severe, life-altering trauma for riders.

Some of the most [common motorcycle injuries](/practice-areas/motorcycle-accidents) include:

- **Road Rash:** A highly common injury, road rash occurs when the rider’s skin scrapes against the pavement, often causing deep abrasions that may lead to infection or require skin grafts.
- **Fractures and Broken Bones:** Impact from a crash can easily [break bones](/injury-types/fractures)—especially in the arms, legs, wrists, and ribs—due to the lack of external protection.
- **Head and Brain Injuries (TBIs):** Head trauma is a leading cause of fatal motorcycle crashes. Riders without helmets are at especially high risk of skull fractures and [traumatic brain injuries](/blogs/traumatic-brain-injury-evidence-after-california-accident).
- **Spinal Cord Injuries:** These injuries can result in partial or complete paralysis, affecting motor function and quality of life.
- **Internal Injuries:** Damage to internal organs or internal bleeding may not be immediately visible but can be life-threatening if untreated.
- **Face and Dental Injuries:** Helmets protect most riders from facial injuries, but not wearing a helmet or wearing the wrong one can lead to painful injuries affecting a rider’s teeth, chin, and jaw.
- ** Emotional Trauma (PTSD, Anxiety):**Psychological effects such as post-traumatic stress disorder and anxiety are common, particularly in severe or near-fatal accidents.

## Who Is Liable in a Motorcycle Accident? Understanding Fault & Negligence

Following a motorcycle accident, one of the most pressing questions is: Who is at fault? Was it the motorcyclist, another driver, a third party, or a combination of factors?

In most cases, liability is based on negligence—the failure to exercise reasonable care. The party whose negligence contributed most directly to the crash is typically held liable for damages. Determining liability involves investigations by law enforcement, insurance adjusters, and attorneys.

Common factors considered during a liability investigation include:

- ** The rider’s behavior** (e.g., speeding, improper lane changes, lane splitting)
- ** The other driver’s actions** (e.g., failure to yield, distracted or impaired driving)
- ** Vehicle defects** linked to manufacturers or repair shops
- ** Hazardous road design or poor maintenance** by government entities

** California’s comparative fault laws** allow blame to be assigned to multiple parties, potentially reducing compensation based on the rider’s share of fault.

Understanding these elements is essential for protecting your legal rights after a crash.

## California Motorcycle Laws: Helmets, Lane Splitting & Licensing

Depending on where you ride, laws and requirements can differ. Let’s break down some of the key motorcycle laws.

### Helmet Laws

Most U.S. states have some type of law requiring the use of a motorcycle helmet. For example, California has a universal helmet law, which requires all motorcycle riders and passengers to wear a helmet at all times, regardless of age or experience. Additionally, all helmets must meet the standards of the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT).

Other states, like Texas, have partial motorcycle helmet laws, where helmet use is only mandatory for riders under a certain age (typically under 21) or for those who do not meet specific training or insurance requirements.

### Lane Splitting

Lane splitting, which is the practice of riding between moving lanes of traffic, is only legal in California under Vehicle Code §21658.1. Other states, such as Utah, Montana, Arizona, Colorado, and Minnesota, permit lane filtering (moving between stopped or very slow traffic), but not full lane splitting.

Although lane splitting is supported by the American Motorcyclist Association (AMA) and other organizations, many still view it as a risky maneuver, making it a frequent topic of debate, even among motorcyclists themselves.

### Motorcycle Licensing & Insurance Requirements

All states require a motorcycle-specific endorsement (commonly a Class M license). Riders must pass written and skills tests, though many states waive the skills test for those who complete a state-approved rider safety course.

If you file a motorcycle accident claim, here is what to expect:

## How to Prevent Motorcycle Accidents: Safety Tips for California Riders

Dealing with the insurance company after any type of motor vehicle accident can be a complex process. Fortunately, your attorney will handle all communications with the adjuster so you don’t have to. Still, taking the right steps after a motorcycle accident can protect your legal rights and strengthen your claim:

- **Don’t Admit Fault at the Scene:** Avoid making statements that could be interpreted as accepting blame. Fault is a legal determination that should be made after a full investigation.
- ** Keep Records and Receipts:**Save all documentation related to the accident—this includes medical bills, repair estimates, prescription costs, and proof of lost income. These records are critical for calculating compensation.
- ** Follow All Medical Treatment Plans:**Attend all follow-up appointments and adhere to your doctor’s instructions. Gaps in treatment can be used by insurers to argue that your injuries weren’t serious.
- ** Stay Off Social Media:**Avoid posting about the accident, your injuries, or recovery. Insurance companies may monitor your online activity and use it to challenge your credibility or claim.

## Preventing Future Accidents

Most motorcycle accidents are preventable, given the right precautions. Whether you are a seasoned rider or just starting out, it’s always important to stay vigilant, wear proper protective gear, and practice safe, defensive riding techniques.

Following traffic laws, avoiding distractions, and regularly maintaining your motorcycle can significantly reduce your risk on the road

### Tip #1: All Gear All the Time (ATGATT)

Never ride without full protective gear. This includes a DOT-approved helmet, abrasion-resistant jacket and pants, gloves, knee protection, and over-the-ankle boots. Proper gear can reduce the severity of injuries in the event of a crash.

### Tip #2: Enroll in a Safety Course

Whether you’re new or experienced, taking a motorcycle safety course sharpens your skills and reinforces safe riding habits. Many states also offer insurance discounts for completing certified training programs.

### Tip #3: Maintain Your Motorcycle Regularly

Check tire pressure, brake pads, lights, and fluid levels before every ride. A well-maintained bike is less likely to malfunction and more responsive in emergencies.

### Tip #4: Ride Defensively and Stay Visible

Assume other drivers don’t see you. Use reflective gear, headlights (even during the day), and position yourself where you’re most visible in traffic. Leave plenty of space between you and other vehicles, and always plan for sudden moves from drivers.
