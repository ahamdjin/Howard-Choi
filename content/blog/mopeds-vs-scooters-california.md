## Identify the vehicle before choosing a rule

Start with the manufacturer’s label, manual, model number, and any registration paperwork. Record the number of wheels, whether it has a floorboard for standing, how it is powered, and whether it has working pedals. A photograph of the whole device alongside the label is more useful than a receipt that says “electric scooter.” If the device has been modified, keep documentation of the changes. A modification may make an original manufacturer description incomplete.

A typical stand-up motorized scooter has handlebars, a motor and a floorboard. A moped is a separate vehicle classification. A seated vehicle marketed as a scooter can be a motor-driven cycle or motorcycle depending on its design and specifications. An electric bicycle also has its own definition and rules. The California DMV and CHP explain these distinctions and list the relevant Vehicle Code provisions. [California DMV vehicle classifications](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/new-registration/motorcycles-mopeds-and-scooters/) · [CHP vehicle rules](https://www.chp.ca.gov/programs-services/programs/california-motorcyclist-safety/motorcycles-and-similar-vehicles)

| Type | What to verify | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motorized scooter | Stand-up floorboard, actual design, and local rules for the route | Different licensing, operating, and registration rules apply |
| Moped or motorized bicycle | DMV classification, M1 or M2 licensing, moped plate and identification card | Moped registration differs from ordinary motorcycle registration |
| Motor-driven cycle or motorcycle | Vehicle design, engine information, registration and license class | Motorcycle insurance and equipment requirements may apply |
| Electric bicycle | Pedals, class label, and how the motor provides assistance | Separate state and local requirements can govern where and how it may be ridden |

## What changes when the device is a stand-up scooter?

California’s rules for a motorized scooter include a minimum age of 16, a valid driver’s license or instruction permit, and an operating speed limit of 15 mph. Riders under 18 must wear an appropriate helmet. State rules also address riding on sidewalks and where scooters may operate on roads. A rental company or local ordinance may impose additional restrictions. Check the posted rules and the route before riding. [California Office of Traffic Safety guidance](https://gosafelyca.org/e-bikes-e-scooters-and-e-motorcycles/)

If you are comparing two routes, look at the conditions on the route itself. A path shown as a bike lane in an app may end before a busy intersection. Local rules can affect the street segment, trail, or property where the incident occurred. After a collision, save the route and the location where you entered or left a bikeway. Photograph the signs and lane markings as they existed on the day, when safe.

A rented scooter also creates a record with a vendor. Save the ride receipt, account history, device ID, and any message reporting a malfunction. Request that the company preserve trip and maintenance records if they may matter. Record what the scooter was doing in plain terms: for example, the brake lever moved but the device did not slow as expected. Keep that observation distinct from a conclusion about why the brake did not work.

## What changes when it is a moped or a motorcycle?

California DMV lists an M1 or M2 license for a moped and describes a special plate and identification card. The agency distinguishes the moped registration process from the process for a motorcycle. It states that insurance is not required to register a moped. That does not establish what a particular rider’s policy will pay after a collision. Ask the insurer about the exact vehicle, its use and who is insured. [California DMV moped and scooter guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/new-registration/motorcycles-mopeds-and-scooters/)

A motor-driven cycle or motorcycle can have a different license class and registration process. If a dealer describes the vehicle as road legal, ask for the documents that support that statement. Compare the VIN and classification with the title and registration. The [California motorcycle accident guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-accident-guide) explains the evidence and insurance questions that can arise when a driver injures a rider.

### Why a crash investigator needs more than a receipt

Imagine two devices that look similar in a photograph. One is a stand-up rental scooter. The other is a seated motor-driven cycle with a state registration. Both are struck by cars making right turns. The vehicle classification can affect which rules and policies are examined, but the evidence about the turn still matters for both. Ask where the driver was looking, what the signal showed, whether there was a sight obstruction, and where the rider entered the intersection.

In the rental case, a trip record might show the time and approximate route. In the motor-driven cycle case, the rider’s declarations and registration may make it easier to identify applicable coverage. Neither record alone establishes who had the right of way. The [California comparative fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) explains how evidence about each person’s conduct can affect a claim.

## What to preserve after a moped or scooter crash

- Get care and contact emergency services when needed. Photograph the location only when safe.
- Record the other vehicle, its plate, driver and insurance information. Get witness contact details.
- Save the device’s manufacturer label, serial number, model and any receipt or registration.
- Preserve the device, charger, helmet and damaged parts if their condition may matter. Document where they are kept.
- Keep trip receipts, app screenshots, and any maintenance or incident reports. Ask the provider how to request the underlying records.
- Check the applicable insurance and reporting requirements. Keep coverage communications separate from evidence about the crash.

If someone altered the device or repaired it after the crash, keep records of the work and when it occurred. Tell an insurer or investigator if a later photo shows the device in a different condition. When the rider was using a route shared with pedestrians and bicyclists, the [pedestrian accident guide](/blogs/california-pedestrian-accident-guide) can help identify useful witness and location details. ## Check insurance before assuming the device is covered

A car policy, motorcycle policy, renters policy and shared-scooter agreement can use different definitions for the same device. Pull the documents that applied when the crash happened and ask each relevant insurer which definition it is using. The [California motorcycle insurance guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-insurance) explains the separate coverage questions when the vehicle is a motorcycle or motor-driven cycle. Give the carrier the model and specifications. If the device was rented, provide the trip record and the agreement rather than relying on a promotional page about the service.

For a crash caused by someone driving a car, identify the car, its driver, and its insurer. The rider’s classification can matter to some questions, but the investigation also needs the evidence of where the car and rider were and what each person did. If the dispute involves poor maintenance or a damaged device, the rental operator’s records may matter even if the rider has no separate policy for it. Preserve the condition of the controls and any error message if safe to do so.

A delivery rider should also save records of the job and app status at the time. A personal trip, travel to a pickup, and an active delivery may present different policy questions. Get a copy of any insurance explanation and identify the coverage and exclusion cited. If your insurance card covers a car rather than the device, ask whether any personal benefits could still apply in your circumstances. The answer can differ between medical expenses, damage to the device and a claim against the rider.

For an incident on private property, record who controls the property and any signs about access or riding. A route through a shopping center can include a driveway, a pedestrian path and a public street within a short distance. Save enough location detail to tell which part of the route the insurer or investigator is reviewing.

When you are shopping for a device, ask the seller for the classification and specifications in writing. Keep those documents with the receipt so you can resolve a question later without relying on a sales listing that may have changed.

## When a scooter trip crosses a driveway or city boundary

For a trip through Buena Park or a neighboring Orange County or Los Angeles County community, save the exact location rather than only the city name. A collision outside a shopping center might happen on a public roadway, inside a private driveway, or where a driveway crosses a sidewalk. Record where the device was traveling immediately before contact and where the other vehicle began its movement. Photos of the whole entrance can explain something a close-up of the damaged scooter cannot.

Keep the route in its original form if an app recorded it. A screenshot with a pin marks a place, but it may omit the approach, a turn, or the time spent stopped. Save the trip identifier and ask whether a fuller record exists. If a camera belongs to a nearby business, identify the approximate time window and the entrance it overlooks. The camera owner may have a short retention period; a preservation request does not guarantee that footage exists or will be released.

Write down the agency that actually handled the incident. Do not assume that the nearest city police department created the report. Keep the incident number, the location, and the responding agency together. If no report was prepared, the [guide to claims without a police report](/blogs/car-insurance-claim-without-police-report-california) explains how to organize other evidence. Avoid changing an account of the route merely to match a rental map or a later recollection.

An injury can also raise a medical-history question separate from vehicle classification. Tell the treating clinician about an earlier problem in the same area and explain what changed after this incident. The [guide to a crash aggravating a pre-existing injury](/blogs/car-accident-pre-existing-injury-california) describes records that can help make that comparison. If an insurer rejects a loss, keep its explanation with the specifications and trip records; the [denied-claim guide](/blogs/car-insurance-claim-denied-california) explains how to identify the issue before responding.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a regular driver’s license for a stand-up motorized scooter?

California permits operation of a qualifying motorized scooter with a valid driver’s license or instruction permit, subject to operating rules. A vehicle sold as a scooter can fall into another category. Check its specifications and DMV classification before riding.

### Can I register a moped like a motorcycle?

California DMV describes a separate plate and identification process for qualifying mopeds. Confirm what applies to your specific model. A seated vehicle or one described as a “moped” in a sales listing may qualify under a different category.

### Does a rental app tell me where riding is legal?

The app can provide information about the rental, but you still need to check state and local rules and the posted conditions. Save a screenshot of any route instructions if they are relevant to an incident.

### Does a licensing issue automatically decide who caused the crash?

No. Licensing, insurance coverage, and crash responsibility are separate issues. Preserve the records for each. A dispute about whether a device could lawfully be used on a road does not answer what a driver or rider did immediately before impact.

### Does a stand-up motorized scooter need DMV registration?

DMV states that qualifying motorized scooters do not require registration. That differs from the special plate process for a moped and the registration requirements for a motor-driven cycle. Verify the device category first; the name on a seller’s page does not establish which process applies.
