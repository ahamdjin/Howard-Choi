## Three records serve three different purposes

| Record | What it does | What to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Police or CHP collision report | Records a law enforcement response or investigation when one occurs | Agency, incident number, and how to request a copy |
| DMV SR-1 | Reports a collision that meets California’s filing criteria | The submitted information and confirmation |
| Insurance claim | Gives the carrier notice and starts a review of coverage and losses | Claim number, date reported, and each adjuster’s assignment |

A missing police report can mean different things. Officers may have responded and the report is still pending. You may have a call or incident number but no written collision report. Or you may have exchanged information without requesting an officer at all. Tell the insurer which situation applies. If a report is pending, give the carrier the agency and incident number rather than describing the report as unavailable.

A DMV filing has its own requirements. California DMV says each driver must report within 10 days if anyone is injured or killed, or if property damage exceeds $1,000. The DMV filing is separate from a police report or insurer notice. For collisions involving injury or death, the DMV handbook describes a separate report to law enforcement within 24 hours. [California DMV collision reporting guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/financial-responsibility-insurance-requirements-and-collisions/)

## What to send when there is no report

Write down the date, time, exact location, direction of travel and the sequence you recall. Identify your location using a nearby address, cross street, or stall number. If you took photographs, preserve the original files, including wider photos that show traffic controls, lane markings, and vehicle positions. Close-ups of damage become more useful when the adjuster can see how the vehicles were positioned.

Add the other driver’s name, vehicle information, plate number and insurance details if you were able to exchange them. Keep the names and contact details of witnesses. If they saw only part of the event, describe what they said they saw. A person who heard the impact and turned around is useful for a different purpose from someone who watched the approach.

Ask nearby property owners about cameras as soon as you can. Identify the exact time window and where the camera was pointed. Save any receipt, parking record, or photograph that helps narrow the timing. A business may have a short retention period and may require a formal request before releasing footage. Record who controls it and what they told you about preservation.

If your car was towed or inspected, obtain a copy of the tow record and estimate. These can show when it was moved and what damage was documented. If you sought medical care, keep the treatment dates and bills. A property damage estimate and a medical record answer different questions. Keep them in separate folders under the same crash date.

If the crash happened during an Uber or Lyft trip, keep the trip receipt and the platform incident number alongside whatever crash records you have. The [California Uber and Lyft insurance guide](/blogs/uber-lyft-accident-insurance-california) explains how to report the crash and ask whether an insurer has opened a claim.

## How to handle a late report or a hit-and-run

If you discover the damage after the other driver has gone, document what you found and ask the responsible agency how to report it. Write down when you last saw the car undamaged and when you found the damage. That interval is more accurate than an invented time of impact. If you suspect another car struck it, say what supports that conclusion. A scratch alone may not identify the vehicle that caused it.

Contact your carrier promptly to ask which coverage might apply and whether it requires a report or other specific evidence. Uninsured motorist coverage can have rules that differ from collision coverage, especially when the other driver is unknown. The [California hit-and-run and uninsured driver guide](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims) explains the separate injury and vehicle questions. A notice to one insurer does not establish that every available benefit has been properly requested.

If the agency will accept only an incident report or an online submission in your situation, keep the confirmation and ask how to obtain a copy. Give the insurer the confirmation number and ask what it still needs. Avoid sending the carrier a statement that law enforcement declined to investigate unless that is what the agency actually told you.

## A parking-lot example

A driver returns to a parked car and finds the rear door scraped. No one is present. A store receipt shows when the driver arrived, and a photo taken earlier in the day shows the door undamaged. The shopping center has cameras, but the manager needs the stall location and a narrower time range to search.

The driver can photograph the whole parking area and damage, report the discovery to the appropriate agency, and notify the insurer. The receipt and earlier photo can help narrow the window. The carrier can start reviewing collision coverage and inspecting the car even while the manager checks the camera records. If a plate number turns up, give it to the agency and carrier with the source of the information. The plate may identify a vehicle without establishing who was driving.

Now suppose the repair estimate comes back over $1,000 after an initial visual estimate below that amount. Check the DMV requirements promptly and retain the dates of both estimates. The dollar threshold concerns the reportable loss and can be hard to judge from appearance alone. Our [DMV accident reporting guide](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident) explains the separate SR-1 process.

## Questions that move the insurance claim forward

- Can you open the claim with the incident number while I request the written report?
- Do you need a law enforcement report for this specific coverage or would other records address the same issue?
- What fact is still in dispute: how the crash happened, who drove, whether the policy applies, or what damage it caused?
- Who can inspect the vehicle, and do you need it left in its current condition before repairs?
- What will you accept to document witnesses, camera footage and the date the damage was discovered?

Put the answers in your claim log. If one adjuster says a report is required, ask them to confirm the applicable policy term and what alternatives are available if the agency did not prepare one. Supply the available documents promptly and ask which ones are still needed. The [California post-crash guide](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california) has a broader checklist for preserving records and getting the claim started.

## Give the claim a clear next step

When you first call, explain which record is missing and what you already have. You could say, “I have the other driver's insurance information, photos and a witness's number. I have asked the investigating agency whether it prepared a written report. Can you open the claim now and tell me what you need to review it?” Write down the claim number and where to submit files securely. If the other driver's carrier is handling a separate file, use the right claim number for each submission. Our guide to [filing a claim with the other driver's insurer](/blogs/third-party-car-insurance-claim-california) explains where that second file fits.

If the adjuster says the file cannot move forward without a report, ask them to identify the coverage under review, the specific document they require and any date for supplying it. Then confirm whether a call number, a written agency response or other records will address their request. Save their answer with the claim number so you can follow up on the exact issue instead of starting over with the next person.

If the crash caused injuries and responsibility is disputed, bring the evidence you have to a [car accident claim review](/practice-areas/car-accidents). A camera clip, damage photos, or a witness can matter even without a detailed report.

## Give the insurer a record it can actually use

Build the evidence around the question being investigated. If the carrier wants to know when the damage occurred, a photo taken before the incident and a parking receipt can narrow the window. If the question is which vehicle caused the damage, a camera recording, witness statement or plate number may help. If the dispute is whether the crash caused a particular repair, send the shop’s inspection and any photos from before work started. A pile of screenshots can be harder to use than a brief explanation with each document attached.

Keep a clear difference between the original record and your notes about it. For instance, save a store camera clip as provided and keep your description of the time and vehicles in a separate message. If a camera’s clock may be wrong, mention how you checked it. Give the insurer the full clip when it is available so that the events leading up to impact can be considered.

When two people provide different descriptions, write down who supplied each account and when. Avoid editing the source to make the stories agree. If you discover an error in your first notice, correct it with the date and supporting record. The change is easier to understand when it is tied to information that became available later.

If the agency later provides a written report and an entry appears incorrect, see [how to request a collision-report correction](/blogs/correct-inaccurate-car-accident-police-report-california).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file a claim if police did not come to the scene?

You can generally notify the insurer and ask it to investigate. Provide the available records and ask whether a particular benefit requires a report. Check and meet any separate legal reporting duties.

### Is an SR-1 the same as a police report?

No. An SR-1 is a DMV report, while a police report documents a law enforcement response or investigation. Keep the confirmation or report number for each. Neither opens an insurance claim on its own.

### Can an insurer decide fault without a report?

An insurer can evaluate other evidence, such as witness statements, photographs, video, and vehicle damage. Ask what evidence supports a disputed decision and what information would help it reconsider.

### What if the report becomes available after I filed the claim?

Send the report or its identifying details with your claim number. Read it for errors and flag any disputed information in writing. Keep copies of what you sent and ask whether the insurer needs anything else to continue the review.
