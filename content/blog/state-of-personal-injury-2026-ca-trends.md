Changes in firm ownership, connected vehicles, funding arrangements, and proposals affecting legal fees can influence how a claim is investigated or financed. Their effects are not identical. A proposed rule is not an operative law, and a large verdict is not a reliable estimate of an individual case.

Most of this has unfolded in legislative committees and insurance trade reports rather than on the news. Here’s what’s shaping [personal injury](/blogs/california-personal-injury-guide) right now, and why each one matters if you’re a Californian who might get hurt and need to make a claim.

## Private equity is buying personal injury firms

For most of American history, you couldn’t own a piece of a law firm unless you were a lawyer. That rule still exists in almost every state. Investors found a way around it.

The structure is called a management services organization, or MSO. The firm stays lawyer-owned on paper. A separate company, backed by private equity, buys everything that isn’t technically the practice of law: the marketing, the call center, the intake staff, the billing. Then it charges the firm management fees. The lawyers keep practicing. The investors collect.

For a prospective client, the important question is who controls the work and the decisions. Ask which lawyer is responsible for the case, whether another firm will receive it, who approves outside expenses, and how you will be consulted before a settlement. Investment in a firm’s operations can support resources, but it does not by itself establish quality or independence.

“Private equity buying PI firms (MSOs): If investors own your firm’s business side, there’s a risk that profit targets start shaping which cases get taken and how fast they’re pushed to settle, so the firm’s incentives may not line up perfectly with getting you the most money. The flip side is that well-funded firms can sometimes afford to fight longer and take cases a smaller shop would turn down.”

## States are racing to ban the fee-sharing that makes those deals work

Regulation of lawyer independence and nonlawyer financial interests differs by jurisdiction. A firm’s management structure, referral relationship, and fee arrangement should be evaluated under the rules that actually apply. Do not assume a business model allowed elsewhere is automatically permitted in California.

When reading about a new bill, distinguish introduction, committee approval, passage, signature, and the operative date. Those stages answer different questions. An announcement about a proposal cannot establish the rule governing a client’s agreement today. Ask counsel to identify the current authority if a proposed change is said to affect your engagement.

## The fight over who’s secretly funding lawsuits

There’s a parallel money story one layer down, in who funds the lawsuits themselves. Third-party litigation funding is when an outside investor pays for a lawsuit in exchange for a cut of the recovery. For years, it operated almost entirely in the dark.

Funding agreements can involve repayment terms, charges over time, control provisions, and consequences if a case settles for less than expected. A person considering an advance should obtain the full contract and a written explanation of what would be owed at several possible repayment dates. The advertised advance alone does not describe the transaction.

Distinguish money advanced to a claimant from financing supplied to a law firm. Ask who owes the obligation, whether repayment depends on a recovery, and whether it affects authority over settlement. State regulation and the actual contract matter; do not infer that a funding agreement is favorable merely because it is described as nonrecourse.

## Trust should be evaluated, not assumed

Trust is better assessed through observable conduct than an industry-wide claim. Ask whether the fee agreement is understandable, who returns calls, how often updates are provided, and how disputed expenses are explained. Keep written answers so that expectations can be compared with what actually happens.

Neither hiring a lawyer nor speaking with an adjuster eliminates the need to check records. Read authorizations, confirm which insurer a person represents, and ask for explanations of decisions that affect treatment or payment. A respectful interaction and a legally sufficient claim are separate matters.

## Robotaxis are changing what a car accident claim even looks like

A collision involving an automated vehicle can require records beyond a driver’s account: operating status, sensor or video data, vehicle maintenance, and the identity of entities controlling relevant systems. Record the vehicle’s identifiers, incident reference, location, and available witness information promptly.

An automated-vehicle crash does not automatically become a product-liability case against one manufacturer. The facts may raise questions about another driver, a vehicle operator, software, maintenance, or multiple entities. A sound investigation identifies the alleged failure and the applicable legal theory before assuming who should be sued.

Aggregate safety claims do not decide responsibility for a particular collision. Preserve the specific event record and ask whether relevant data could be overwritten or changed. Avoid sending an unqualified conclusion that the vehicle malfunctioned when the available information shows only that a crash occurred.

## Insurance fraud is following the money into no-fault states

Fraud allegations can involve staged collisions, invented treatment, inflated records, or misuse of claims processes. An allegation in a complaint or indictment is not a finding that every named person acted unlawfully. For an injured claimant, accurate documentation is the most useful response to this risk.

Different insurance systems create different payment procedures. [California’s fault-based system](/blogs/is-california-a-no-fault-state-or-an-at-fault-state) should not be treated as interchangeable with another state’s no-fault benefits. That distinction does not establish that fraud cannot occur in California; coverage and the legitimacy of a particular bill still require review.

Read bills, attend only appointments actually scheduled for you, and correct material errors in dates or treatment descriptions. Do not sign a statement describing care you did not receive. Keep copies of instructions and invoices, and raise questions about unexplained charges with the appropriate professional.

## Uber is spending big to rewrite California’s injury rules

A California initiative proposed restrictions on attorney compensation and medical-expense recovery in motor-vehicle injury matters. The proposal is useful context for the debate, but its text should not be presented as existing law merely because a campaign describes it that way. [Legislative Analyst’s Office analysis of the proposal](https://lao.ca.gov/BallotAnalysis/Initiative/2025-022)

Read the official analysis alongside campaign arguments. Ask which cases the text covers, what expenses count, how an attorney’s compensation would be calculated, and whether transitional provisions affect existing agreements. Those details can matter more than the campaign’s short description.

The earlier claim that the measure still needed to qualify for the November 2026 ballot is outdated. The Secretary of State announced eligibility in 2026. Qualification, the election result, certification, and an operative date are distinct stages: confirm the current official status before treating a proposal as enforceable or changing a fee agreement. [Secretary of State ballot-measure records](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/ballot-measures/qualified-ballot-measures)

## Courts are sanctioning lawyers for AI that invents cases

Generative AI can produce plausible-sounding citations, quotations, or legal rules that do not match the authority. A polished answer is not evidence that a case exists or that a statute says what the answer claims. Legal work requires checking the underlying sources and their current relevance.

When evaluating legal research, ask whether the person checked the actual decision or statute rather than relying on a summary. A citation should support the proposition for which it is used. The responsibility to verify authority remains with the person using it; an automated tool does not take over that responsibility.

For a client organizing a claim, an AI tool may help draft a list of questions or sort a chronology, but avoid entering sensitive records without understanding data handling. Do not let an automated estimate determine a filing deadline, a diagnosis, or an acceptable settlement without an appropriate professional review.

## Nuclear verdicts are climbing, and so is the backlash

“Nuclear verdict” is an industry label often used for very large jury awards. Headlines about those awards do not show the net amount a claimant receives, whether post-trial proceedings changed the result, or how closely the facts resemble your claim. Read the procedural context before using a verdict as a comparison.

Advocacy organizations can use the same event to support competing reform arguments. Identify who produced a report, how it defines the costs or benefits, and which assumptions drive the result. A claim about the cost of the legal system is not a substitute for evidence about a particular person’s damages.

Medical-expense recovery can involve the relationship between amounts billed, amounts paid, future care, liens, and reimbursement rights. The applicable legal rules and evidence determine what can be claimed. Ask for an explanation of those categories rather than treating the total on a provider invoice as a guaranteed recovery.

## Social inflation is quietly raising everyone’s premiums

The last trend ties several others together. Insurers call it social inflation: claim costs rising faster than ordinary inflation, pushed up by larger verdicts, litigation funding, advertising, and shifting juror attitudes. i.e., If normal inflation is 3% a year but the [average injury claim](/blogs/how-much-is-my-personal-injury-case-worth-california) costs insurers 8-10% more each year, that extra gap is “social inflation.”

Separate an insurer’s explanation of rising claims costs from the evidence needed to evaluate your own claim. Changes in repair costs, treatment expenses, litigation, and policy pricing can have different causes. A broad industry estimate does not establish that a particular bill is inflated or that a claimant should accept a lower offer.

Advertising is another reason to examine the actual engagement. A prominent campaign does not tell you who handles the file, whether a case will be referred elsewhere, or how expenses affect the net recovery. Ask those questions directly before signing and preserve the written agreement.

The thread running through all ten is that the rules around personal injury are being rewritten right now, by investors, insurers, tech companies, and legislators, often faster than the average person hears about it. The consequences depend on the actual rule, agreement, and facts. Assess a claimed benefit or disadvantage from the underlying documents rather than assuming that every reform has the same effect.

## A practical review before signing or settling

The [California settlement timeline guide](/blogs/how-long-car-accident-settlement-takes-california) explains how records, negotiations, and reimbursement questions affect a claim. Our [personal injury practice page](/practice-areas/personal-injury) describes the firm’s civil injury work.

Start with the agreement that directly affects you. If you are selecting a lawyer, identify the named firm, the responsible lawyer, the scope of work, and the proposed fee. Ask how costs are handled if the case does not recover money. A percentage is only one part of the financial arrangement; expert fees, advances, medical obligations, and other deductions may require separate explanation.

If someone offers funding, ask for repayment illustrations at different dates and recovery amounts. Keep the funding agreement separate from the legal engagement and medical paperwork. Do not assume approval by one participant means every other participant has reviewed the arrangement or that it is suitable for your circumstances.

Before accepting a settlement, request a written breakdown of the proposed gross recovery, fees, expenses, known reimbursement claims, and estimated net payment. Identify unresolved amounts. An estimate should be labeled as an estimate, especially while a provider or benefit plan is still asserting a claim. Compare the release’s scope with the injuries and parties you intend to resolve.

### Use the headline to form a question

A headline about a new technology might lead to an evidence question: which records should be preserved? A headline about fees might lead to a contract question: what will this agreement require? A headline about a huge verdict might lead to a damages question: which facts made that case different? This method keeps industry news useful without turning it into a prediction about your recovery.

Write the answer next to the source. Distinguish what your records establish, what a professional has explained, and what remains uncertain. If a deadline is involved, ask for the specific date and the event from which it is calculated. A national trend should never replace a case-specific response to a notice or filing requirement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a large verdict mean my claim has a similar value?

No. The injuries, evidence, defendants, coverage, and procedural history may be very different. A reported award also may not describe the final payment.

### Should I change a fee agreement because I read about a ballot measure?

Do not act on a campaign summary alone. Check the official status, actual text, operative date, and any transition provisions, then obtain advice about the existing agreement.

### Does an automated vehicle crash automatically establish a product defect?

No. The investigation must identify what happened and which conduct or condition contributed. Vehicle status, another road user, maintenance, and available data may all matter.

### Can an AI tool calculate a safe settlement or filing deadline?

A tool cannot replace review of the actual records, legal rules, and circumstances. Use it cautiously for organization, and verify consequential legal conclusions with an appropriate professional.

### What is the most useful question to ask about a proposed settlement?

Ask what you will receive after the identified deductions and which claims the release closes. Obtain the written breakdown and identify any unresolved medical or reimbursement issue before deciding.
