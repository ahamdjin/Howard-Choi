## How to check exotic pet laws in any state

Start with the animal’s identity and the thing you intend to do. A seller may describe a reptile as a small monitor, a cat as a Savannah, or a bird as a parakeet. Ask for the scientific name, any known synonym, and records showing its origin. For a hybrid, get the documented parent species and generation. An agency can compare those details with its rules. A photo or trade name alone may leave the classification unresolved. Massachusetts, for example, gives a specific generation requirement for when a Savannah cat is treated as domestic and calls for documentation. [MassWildlife guide to wildlife as pets](https://www.mass.gov/guides/wildlife-as-pets).

Then check the following in order.

1. **Private possession.** Ask the destination wildlife agency whether someone at your address can keep this species as a personal pet. Ask for the exact list or rule and whether the agency treats an unlisted animal as prohibited, restricted, or exempt.
2. **Entry.** Ask the destination state animal health or agriculture office whether an animal coming from your origin needs a veterinary certificate, advance entry permit, inspection, or other paperwork. Use the USDA’s destination selector to locate state or territory requirements. For exotic animals, confirm that the page covers your species.
3. **City and county rules.** Search the municipal or county code for prohibited animals, animal keeping, or zoning. If the municipal boundary is uncertain, give animal control or zoning the full street address. Check your lease separately if you rent.
4. **Federal coverage.** For wildlife with special federal protections, international travel, or unusual wildlife transactions, ask the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service which wildlife rules apply. Confirm transport and possession separately with the state agencies.
5. **Records and timing.** Save the rule, the agency’s written response, any issued permit, and the health and provenance records. Ask who must apply and whether approval must be issued before the animal ships or arrives. [USDA APHIS state and territory pet travel lookup](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/state-to-state).

A single phone call can give you a lead. A written response that names the species and activity gives you something specific to verify if a seller, carrier, or second agency tells you something different. Keep the date you checked. You may need to verify again near the move if regulations or entry orders change.

The Association of Fish & Wildlife Agencies maintains a directory of state wildlife agency websites. Use it to find the agency for your destination and look for its captive wildlife or licensing office. Some states divide responsibility by animal type or activity. If the wildlife office refers you elsewhere, ask for the name of the office and which part of your question it handles. [State wildlife agency directory](https://www.fishwildlife.org/landing/press-releases/state-agency-websites).

## How rules differ across states and cities

These examples show how five states and one city approach different animals. Use the links to check the actual species and activity before you make a decision. For a state outside the table, start with the USDA destination selector for entry and that state’s wildlife agency for private possession. Search the official state site using the scientific name and terms such as captive wildlife, restricted species, or wildlife possession.

| Jurisdiction | What the official rules show |
| --- | --- |
| California | The Department of Fish and Wildlife lists ferrets, hedgehogs, and sugar gliders among animals prohibited as pets. Restricted-species permits cover specified purposes. [California exotic pet rules](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Living-with-Wildlife/Exotic-Pets) |
| Florida | The Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission lists domestic ferrets, hedgehogs, and sugar gliders as species that do not require a personal pet permit. Confirm any separate import and local requirements before moving an animal. [Florida personal pet permits and exemptions](https://myfwc.com/license/captive-wildlife/personal-pet/) |
| Hawaiʻi | The Plant Quarantine Branch lists ferrets and bearded dragons among animals prohibited from private entry or possession. There are separate classifications and permit rules for some other nondomestic animals. [Hawaiʻi animal guidelines](https://dab.hawaii.gov/pi/pq/import-program/animal-guidelines/) · [Nondomestic animal lists](https://dab.hawaii.gov/pi/pq/import-program/pq-non-domestic-animal-and-microorganism-lists/) |
| Massachusetts | The exemption list names the four-toed African pygmy hedgehog and the sugar glider. It also addresses lawful sources of acquisition and separate restrictions on release. [Massachusetts 321 CMR 9.00](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/321-CMR-900-exemption-list) |
| Arizona | The Game and Fish Department announced amended Article 4 rules in 2026, including changes to the restricted live wildlife list. | New York City | The city has its own prohibited animal rules. Check the city’s list for a home in the five boroughs, even if a breeder elsewhere in New York quotes a state rule. [NYC311 illegal animal guidance](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02255) |

For example, a Florida ferret owner arranging a move to Buena Park should check California possession rules before asking a vet to prepare travel paperwork. Florida’s permit exemption concerns ownership there. California’s rules govern the proposed home in Buena Park. If the move can still be planned, ask the appropriate agency and a qualified rescue about lawful care options while you have time to organize them. [California restricted species licensing](https://wildlife.ca.gov/licensing/restricted-species).

## How to read permit and exemption language

When you find a permit page, look for who may apply and what the approval actually authorizes. An exhibit license can cover an organization’s work at a specific facility. A permit for scientific research may contain a different set of conditions. If the only available permits cover defined institutional or commercial uses, ask the agency whether private possession is allowed at all. Rules can vary even for owners who kept the animal legally somewhere else.

Read exemptions with equal care. Check whether the exemption names the exact species or a broader group, what records it requires, and which activities it covers. A rule that permits possession may handle sale, breeding, public contact, import, or release elsewhere in the same code. If you are adopting an animal, ask what records must transfer with it and who is responsible for any filing. If you want to breed or rehome it later, verify those permissions separately.

Ask about transition rules when an animal was acquired legally before a rule change. A provision protecting current possession may set an acquisition date and may restrict transfer or replacement. Give the agency the original acquisition date, documentation, and what you plan to do. Request the rule in writing before assuming a prior owner’s authorization follows the animal.

## Moving, shipping, or buying an exotic pet online

Before booking a move, write down the origin, destination, travel dates, and planned stops. Tell the destination agency if you are staying temporarily, moving permanently, or having an animal delivered. If the route includes an overnight stop or transfer in another jurisdiction, ask that jurisdiction whether its transit or temporary possession requirements apply. An airline or carrier can separately decline the species or require a specific container. Ask about both government requirements and carrier acceptance early enough to change plans.

Before paying for an animal, ask the seller for dated identity and source records, any applicable permits, and the proposed shipping and transfer paperwork. Check that the name and address on the records match the parties to the transaction. Ask which documents you will receive before the animal leaves and what happens if the destination rejects entry. Give the state agency the proposed transfer details directly. A seller’s willingness to ship tells you what they will take an order for. Your destination agencies can tell you whether the transaction qualifies.

### Federal rules for big cats and international wildlife travel

Federal law can limit activities even when a local or state rule looks permissive. The federal Big Cat Public Safety Act places restrictions on private possession and breeding of covered big cats, such as lions and tigers, with defined exceptions. The deadline for qualifying preexisting owners to register their animals under the Act has passed. A person planning to acquire one today should start with federal wildlife authorities and ask whether any exception actually applies to the proposed activity. [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service guide to the Big Cat Public Safety Act](https://www.fws.gov/what-you-need-to-know-about-big-cat-public-safety-act).

For international movement, ask U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service about wildlife import and export requirements and USDA APHIS about animal health rules. Check the foreign country’s requirements, too. Some species may require documentation under the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species. Tell the agencies whether you intend to return with the animal, because a round trip can involve requirements in both directions. [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service permits](https://www.fws.gov/international-affairs/permits) · [USDA APHIS pet travel](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel).

### A sample inquiry to send before you move

Include the animal’s scientific name, its recorded origin, the new address, and the date you expect to arrive. State whether you intend to keep it as a household pet or transfer it to another owner. Ask the official to identify the applicable rule and list any documents required before departure or entry. If wildlife possession and entry are handled by separate offices, ask for the contact for the second office so you can get answers to both questions.

## Frequently asked questions about exotic animal laws

### Can I own a ferret in California if it came from a state where ferrets are legal?

California prohibits keeping ferrets as pets. A lawful purchase or prior residence in another state does not change California’s private pet rule. If you are planning a move, ask California Fish and Wildlife about the applicable rules before you bring the animal across the state line. [California exotic pet rules](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Living-with-Wildlife/Exotic-Pets).

### Does a health certificate let me bring an exotic animal into another state?

A health certificate documents the animal’s health or testing for travel. Confirm possession and wildlife import permission separately. A receiving state can have a wildlife restriction even if a veterinarian can issue a certificate. Ask which agency has authority to approve entry for your species. [USDA interstate pet travel guidance](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/state-to-state).

### Can a city ban an exotic pet that the state allows?

A local animal ordinance can impose additional limits. Check the code and animal control office for the actual destination address, including any rules about the type of animal, number kept, or property use. New York City’s prohibited animal guidance is an example of a city-level rule. [NYC311 illegal animal guidance](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02255).

### What if a seller calls the animal a hybrid and says it is exempt?

Ask for pedigree or other records identifying the parent species and generation. Send those to the destination agency and ask which rule applies to that documented animal. A marketplace listing may use one breed name for animals from several generations. [MassWildlife guide to wildlife as pets](https://www.mass.gov/guides/wildlife-as-pets).

### Can I use the previous owner’s permit or move an animal with a permit from my old state?

Check with the issuing agency and the destination agency. License terms can tie an authorization to a permit holder, a facility, an animal, or a purpose. Before a move or transfer, confirm who needs to apply, what paperwork follows the animal, and when the new approval must be issued.

### What should I do if I already have an animal that may be prohibited?

Contact your state wildlife office or local animal control for direction on lawful surrender, placement, or approved transfer. Keep the animal securely contained while you get guidance. If it is sick, tell the veterinarian the species before arriving so they can advise on care and handling. Keep any purchase or adoption records, and avoid releasing the animal into the wild. [NYC311 surrender guidance](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02255) · [MassWildlife guidance on release](https://www.mass.gov/guides/wildlife-as-pets).
