## Which license covers the motorcycle you want to ride?

Start with the bike itself. California’s M1 classification covers a two-wheel motorcycle or motor-driven cycle and includes the privileges of M2. M2 is narrower. It covers vehicles such as qualifying mopeds and motorized bicycles. An ordinary automobile license does not, by itself, authorize riding a conventional two-wheel street motorcycle. Three-wheel motorcycles, motorcycles with a sidecar, electric bicycles and stand-up motorized scooters raise different classification questions.

A listing that says “scooter” is a poor place to stop your research. A seated scooter with a motor can fall into a different category from a stand-up scooter with a floorboard. For a used or modified vehicle, write down the manufacturer, model, year and the specifications on the frame label. Check whether the bike has pedals and how its motor operates. Those details help DMV answer a licensing question that would otherwise sound like “Can I drive a scooter?” [DMV’s vehicle classification guide](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/new-registration/motorcycles-mopeds-and-scooters/) and our [moped and scooter guide](/blogs/mopeds-vs-scooters-california) explain the differences.

## Plan the under-21 route before booking a test

For an applicant under 21, the California Highway Patrol–approved motorcycle rider course is part of the licensing process. DMV says to bring the course completion certificate, called DL 389, to obtain the motorcycle permit and to hold the permit for six months before obtaining the license. A birthday, the date you bought the motorcycle and the date you rode at the training site are different milestones. Use the issue date on the permit when planning the holding period.

A family arranging a summer course can avoid a costly misunderstanding here. Suppose the student completes the course in June but waits until August to obtain the permit. The family should plan around the August permit date. Finishing the riding exercises in June does not start the six-month period by itself. Before committing to a test appointment, compare the permit date, the certificate date and the date of the proposed appointment.

Applicants under 18 also need to check the minor-specific application, driver education or training, and parent or guardian requirements that apply to their age and license status. DMV lists 15½ as the minimum age for a motorcycle instruction permit. Ask which documents your particular applicant must present, especially if the student has never held a California driver license. A motorcycle school can explain its enrollment rules. DMV determines whether the licensing application is complete.

## What changes for a rider who is 21 or older?

For an adult applicant age 21 or older, DMV offers a route through a skills test and a route using an approved course completion certificate. The course can be useful even for someone who has ridden before. A rider accustomed to one machine may be less familiar with the low-speed control, braking and observation required on another. Look at what the course includes and what you must provide before paying a deposit.

A course waiver and a license are different documents. DMV may waive the motorcycle skills test upon presentation of a valid DL 389. Other application requirements still apply, and an observation test may be required for a motorcycle-only license. DMV says the DL 389 must be submitted within 12 months of issuance. If you took training some time ago, locate the dated certificate before relying on the waiver. [California DMV motorcyclists guide](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/special-interest-driver-guides/motorcyclists-guide/)

Someone who already drives a car should ask to add the appropriate motorcycle class. Someone seeking a motorcycle-only license should say so at the start. The distinction matters because two people taking the same course on the same weekend can arrive at DMV with different outstanding requirements. Keep the application receipt and make a note of what DMV says remains open.

## What a motorcycle permit lets you do

The motorcycle instruction permit has restrictions on carrying passengers, freeway riding and riding at night. Treat those as constraints on the entire trip, including the route home. A weekday ride that starts comfortably in daylight may end after dark if a shift runs late. A navigation app may direct you onto a freeway for one exit even when the destination looks close on the map.

Consider a Buena Park rider planning to practice after work. Before leaving, identify a surface-street route in both directions, check when daylight ends and decide how the motorcycle will get home if the practice session runs late. A licensed friend following in a car does not remove the restrictions. Nor does being close to home. If the only practical route involves a freeway, use another way to reach the practice location.

The same issue comes up around a test appointment. Do not assume that booking an appointment gives you extra privileges to ride there. Ask the test office what it requires for the vehicle and how to arrange arrival if you cannot lawfully ride it to the office yourself. That is easier to resolve several days in advance than in the DMV parking lot.

## Separate the application file from the vehicle file

In the application file, keep your identification, application confirmation, training certificate if applicable, instruction permit and information about any required knowledge or skills test. Confirm the current identity, residency and fee requirements directly with DMV before the appointment. A checklist from a friend who renewed an existing license may omit steps for a first-time applicant.

In the vehicle file, keep the registration, identifying information and applicable evidence of insurance. Verify which documents the office wants if you borrow a motorcycle for the skills test. Permission from the owner answers only one question. It does not establish that the vehicle meets test requirements or that the insurance contract covers every use. Our [California motorcycle insurance guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-insurance) explains why the vehicle, rider, coverage dates and type of loss should be checked separately.

Before leaving the office, read the temporary document carefully. Look for the class, restrictions, issue date and expiration. If a privilege you expected is missing, ask what step remains. Riding based on a training receipt or what a seller told you can create a problem that checking the actual document would have prevented.

## If a crash happens while licensing is disputed

After a collision, a licensing question can quickly take over the conversation. Keep the question precise. What vehicle was involved? What permit or license was in effect on that day? Was the rider carrying a passenger or using a freeway? Preserve the actual document and any restrictions. A license issued after the crash should be clearly identified as later paperwork.

Also record how the collision happened. A driver turning across the rider’s path, a door opening into traffic or [a disputed lane change](/blogs/unsafe-lane-change-accident-fault-california) can require witness accounts, video, photos and a diagram of the approach. A licensing issue alone does not reconstruct who moved first or who could see whom. Avoid making assumptions about fault or coverage before those facts and the policy have been reviewed. The [motorcycle accident guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-accident-guide) addresses scene evidence, while the [motorcycle practice page](/practice-areas/motorcycle-accidents) explains the records useful for a consultation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does having a California car license mean I can ride a motorcycle home from the seller?

For a conventional two-wheel motorcycle, you need the appropriate motorcycle privilege. Before pickup, check the vehicle classification and the license or permit you actually hold. If the ride would exceed your privileges, arrange transport or a properly licensed rider who has the owner’s permission and appropriate coverage.

### Is every motorcycle training certificate accepted by DMV?

No. Check that the course is CHP-approved and that the document issued is the DL 389 used for licensing. An attendance receipt, an online safety lesson or a course completion email may be useful for your records, but ask the provider exactly what DMV document you will receive and when.

### Can I carry a passenger if that person already has an M1 license?

A passenger’s license does not change the restriction on your motorcycle instruction permit. This matters even on a short practice trip. If an experienced rider is helping you learn, plan a safe way for that person to coach without riding as your passenger.

### Do I need an M1 for something sold as an electric scooter?

The label alone is not enough. A stand-up motorized scooter, an electric bicycle, a qualifying moped and an electric motorcycle can be regulated differently. Check the design and specifications against DMV’s definitions, including any changes made by the previous owner. If the classification remains unclear, resolve it before riding on public streets.

### What should I save if DMV asks me to return with another document?

Save the application receipt, the name of the missing document, any deadline and the next appointment confirmation. If DMV retains an original, keep a copy or a record of when you submitted it. Before your next visit, compare the documents with the actual request. This prevents a repeat trip with the same paperwork that was insufficient the first time.
