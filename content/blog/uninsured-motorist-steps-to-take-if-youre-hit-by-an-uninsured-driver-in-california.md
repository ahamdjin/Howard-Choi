Your options depend on who caused the crash, what coverage applies, and whether a responsible party can pay. Start with medical care, evidence, and [notice to your insurer](/blogs/what-is-an-insurance-adjuster-car-accident). Treat [DMV reporting](/blogs/notify-the-california-dmv-about-an-accident), insurance claims, and lawsuits as separate tasks. Completing one does not necessarily satisfy the others.

## Open separate records for injuries and vehicle damage

Ask the insurer to identify which policy provisions it is reviewing for each loss. A bodily injury claim may require medical records and proof about the other driver. Vehicle damage may involve a different limit or deductible. Save each claim number and deadline so a decision on the repair bill does not get mistaken for a decision on the injury claim. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

## Put safety and medical care first

Check for injuries and call 911 if anyone needs emergency help or the collision creates an immediate danger. Move your vehicle out of traffic if you can do so safely. Never chase a driver who leaves.

Contact law enforcement when appropriate and ask how to obtain the incident number or report. Officers may not attend every minor collision, so gather your own evidence when possible.

Seek medical evaluation if you have symptoms, suspect an injury, or are unsure whether you need care. Explain how the crash happened and when symptoms started. Follow treatment recommendations and save [medical records, bills, and receipts](/blogs/medical-bills-while-car-accident-claim-pending-california). These documents help connect your injuries and expenses to the collision. [California Courts personal-injury guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury)

## Gather evidence while it is available

If it is safe, photograph vehicle positions, damage, license plates, road markings, signals, and visible injuries. Exchange names, contact information, driver’s license details, and vehicle information. Request insurance information even if the driver says the policy expired. Coverage may need further investigation.

Get witnesses’ contact details, preserve dashcam recordings, and note nearby cameras that may have captured the crash. Write down the time, location, weather, and what you remember. Keep original files and backup copies.

Stick to what you observed rather than guessing about speed, injuries, or responsibility. A driver’s lack of insurance does not establish that they caused the accident. Evidence still matters when seeking insurance benefits or pursuing a lawsuit. [California Courts personal-injury guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury)

## Understand California’s insurance requirements

Standard California automobile liability minimums increased on January 1, 2025. They are $30,000 for injury or death to one person, $60,000 for injury or death to multiple people in one accident, and $15,000 for property damage. Different limits apply under California’s Low Cost Automobile Insurance Program. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

Liability coverage generally pays covered claims against an insured driver who causes harm. Your own liability insurance ordinarily does not cover your injuries or vehicle repairs. California also accepts other approved forms of financial responsibility, so the absence of a conventional policy deserves investigation rather than an immediate assumption that no payment source exists. [California DMV financial-responsibility guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/dmv-safety-guidelines-actions/financial-responsibility-insurance/)

## Notify your insurer and review your coverage

Report the crash promptly, even if you believe the other driver should pay. Provide accurate information, obtain a claim number, and request your declarations page and relevant policy provisions. Keep dated notes of conversations and copies of documents.

California insurers must offer uninsured motorist coverage, but policyholders may reject it in writing. Do not assume it is included. Ask about uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, collision, [medical payments coverage](/blogs/medical-payments-coverage-medpay-california), and deductibles. Medical payments coverage may help with covered medical expenses regardless of fault, subject to policy terms and limits. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

### Uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage

Uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage may compensate eligible insured people for covered injuries caused by an uninsured driver. Payment depends on fault, documented damages, policy limits, and applicable requirements.

An unknown hit-and-run driver does not automatically trigger coverage. Physical-contact, police-reporting, and insurer-notice conditions may apply. Report the incident promptly and ask the insurer to identify the requirements for your claim. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

### Underinsured motorist coverage

Underinsured motorist coverage addresses qualifying crashes where the responsible driver has insurance but insufficient bodily injury limits. It does not automatically add your full limit to the other driver’s payment. Offsets and other conditions may apply.

For a hypothetical example, suppose the responsible driver has $30,000 in applicable liability coverage and you have a $100,000 underinsured motorist limit. You should not assume that $130,000 is available. Any benefit depends on established damages, applicable offsets, and coverage requirements. Seek advice before settling with or releasing the other driver. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

## File the required DMV accident report

California generally requires an SR-1 report within 10 days if an accident causes injury, death, or more than $1,000 in property damage. The requirement applies regardless of fault. Reporting the collision to police or your insurer does not replace this filing. [California DMV accident-reporting guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/dmv-virtual-office/accident-reporting/)

The SR-1 is not a request for compensation. The DMV does not use this process to pay medical bills or repair costs. Financial-responsibility violations can affect driving privileges, but those proceedings do not replace an insurance claim or lawsuit. [California DMV financial-responsibility guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/dmv-safety-guidelines-actions/financial-responsibility-insurance/)

## Handle vehicle damage separately

Uninsured motorist bodily injury coverage is different from uninsured motorist property damage coverage. California uninsured motorist property damage coverage is commonly limited to $3,500 and requires identification of the uninsured driver. Do not assume it covers damage from an unknown hit-and-run driver.

Collision coverage may pay for covered vehicle damage regardless of the other driver’s insurance status, subject to [your collision deductible](/blogs/car-insurance-deductible-after-accident-california) and policy terms. A collision deductible waiver is separate protection. Comprehensive coverage generally applies to noncollision losses, such as theft, rather than an ordinary crash. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

Photograph the damage before repairs. Ask whether the insurer needs to inspect the vehicle and whether your policy covers [rental expenses after an accident](/blogs/rental-car-reimbursement-after-accident-california). Save towing, storage, and rental receipts, along with repair estimates and invoices.

## Consider legal options and collectability

A lawsuit may be appropriate if an uninsured driver caused your injuries. But obtaining a judgment and collecting payment are different matters. A driver may have little reachable income or few assets. Even a successful lawsuit does not guarantee recovery.

An investigation may identify another responsible party, such as an employer when the facts support employer liability. That requires evidence, not an assumption based on where the driver worked. A lawyer can assess potential defendants, available coverage, and collection concerns. [California Courts personal-injury guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury)

Economic damages may include documented medical expenses and lost earnings. Noneconomic damages may include pain and suffering where recoverable. California comparative fault can reduce damages if you share responsibility, but it does not guarantee payment. Punitive damages require qualifying evidence and are not routine merely because a driver was uninsured. Discuss the available damages and practical costs before pursuing a personal injury lawsuit.

## Track each deadline separately

California personal-injury lawsuits generally have a two-year filing period. Property-damage lawsuits generally have a three-year period. Neither rule applies universally. Public-entity claims can require much earlier action, while cases involving minors, delayed discovery, medical malpractice, or other circumstances need individual review. Fatal crashes also require careful assessment of the applicable claims and deadlines. [California Courts filing-deadline guidance](https://www.sucorte.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations)

Insurance notice requirements and uninsured motorist procedures raise separate timing issues. Arbitration and particular contractual or statutory steps may be necessary. Opening an insurance claim does not necessarily preserve every right. Ask counsel which actions are required, when they are due, and who will complete them.

## Prepare for negotiations before signing a release

Organize treatment records, income documentation, vehicle expenses, and insurer correspondence. If an adjuster disputes an expense or part of your claim, request a written explanation. Respond with records that address the specific issue.

For example, in a hypothetical lost-income dispute, an employer’s confirmation of missed shifts and pay records may help explain the amount requested. A clear calculation is more useful than an unsupported total.

Before signing a release, understand which claims it settles and whether it affects other insurance benefits. Consider ongoing care, reasonably supported future losses, and any reimbursement obligations. A lawyer can review disputed coverage, organize evidence, and help with insurance negotiations.

## Seek support and choose legal help carefully

Tell a healthcare professional if anxiety, sleep problems, or fear of driving persist after the crash. Keep an accurate record of symptoms and treatment without exaggeration.

A [car accident consultation](/practice-areas/car-accidents) may be especially useful when injuries are substantial, responsibility is disputed, coverage is denied, or deadlines are near. Ask about relevant experience, fees, costs, and who will handle communications. No attorney can guarantee an outcome.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I recover compensation without uninsured motorist coverage?

Possibly. Collision or medical payments coverage may address particular losses. You may also have a claim against the responsible driver or another legally responsible party. Recovery depends on evidence, applicable coverage, and the ability to collect payment.

### Will the DMV make the uninsured driver pay me?

No. DMV reporting and financial-responsibility proceedings are not compensation programs. Payment generally comes through applicable insurance, a settlement, or collection on a judgment. [California DMV accident-reporting guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/dmv-virtual-office/accident-reporting/)

### Does uninsured motorist coverage pay for car repairs?

Not necessarily. Bodily injury coverage does not serve the same purpose as property damage coverage. Review uninsured motorist property damage, collision, and deductible-waiver provisions separately. Limits and identification requirements matter. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

### What if the driver leaves before I get their information?

Do not follow them. Record the vehicle description, direction of travel, and any plate details you safely observed. Preserve witness information and video. Notify police and your insurer promptly because hit-and-run coverage can have specific conditions. [California Department of Insurance auto guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm)

### Should I accept cash at the scene?

Avoid a rushed final settlement before you understand your injuries and repair costs. A private payment arrangement does not remove applicable reporting duties. Before signing anything, check whether it releases injury claims, property claims, or both.

### How long do I have to act?

Several deadlines may apply. An SR-1 generally is due within 10 days for qualifying crashes. Lawsuit deadlines, government-claim requirements, insurance notices, and arbitration procedures need separate review. Reporting the accident does not necessarily preserve every claim. [California DMV accident-reporting guidance](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/dmv-virtual-office/accident-reporting/) [California Courts filing-deadline guidance](https://www.sucorte.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations)
