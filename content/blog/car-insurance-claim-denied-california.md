## Start with the decision the insurer actually made

Keep the entire letter, its attachments, the envelope if relevant, and the date it reached you. Save a downloaded copy of a portal message rather than relying on continued access to the account. Note the claim number, policy number, adjuster, and person or vehicle identified. A wrong accident date or policy period is worth clarifying before arguing about the conclusion.

The words used in a phone call can be misleading. An adjuster saying that it cannot approve treatment yet may mean it needs a missing record. A letter denying collision coverage might leave a separate injury claim unresolved. A reservation-of-rights letter raises coverage concerns while an investigation continues; it is not necessarily a final denial. Ask the company to confirm the status of each part in writing.

The [insurance adjuster guide](/blogs/what-is-an-insurance-adjuster-car-accident) explains how to identify the person handling each loss. Use that information to direct the response. Sending medical records to the vehicle estimator may leave the injury adjuster unaware that you supplied them.

## Is this your insurer or the other driver’s insurer?

A first-party claim seeks benefits under a policy that covers you. Examples can include collision, [medical payments coverage](/blogs/medical-payments-coverage-medpay-california), or [uninsured motorist benefits](/blogs/california-uninsured-driver-hit-and-run-injury-claims), depending on the contract and circumstances. A third-party claim seeks payment from another person’s liability carrier because that person allegedly caused your loss. The policy relationship, available procedures, and legal options differ.

California’s claims regulation requires a written first-party denial to explain its factual and legal grounds and identify applicable policy provisions when relied on. Request the complete explanation if you received only a brief message. The [Department of Insurance’s fair claims regulations](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/130-laws-regs-hearings/05-CCR/fair-claims-regs.cfm) link to section 2695.7, which addresses these requirements.

If the other driver’s insurer disputes responsibility, identify what its insured allegedly did and the evidence for your account. A carrier’s position is not a court judgment. Conversely, proof that the other driver caused the crash does not establish that a particular policy covers that driver. The [third-party claim guide](/blogs/third-party-car-insurance-claim-california) discusses that separate relationship.

If a rideshare insurer disputes whether the driver was waiting for a request, driving to pickup, or carrying a passenger, ask which timestamp and app record the carrier used. The [Uber and Lyft accident insurance guide](/blogs/uber-lyft-accident-insurance-california) shows which records can help check that decision.

## Match the response to the reason given

| Issue in the letter | Records to locate | Question to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy was inactive or the wrong vehicle was listed | Declarations, endorsements, payment records and cancellation correspondence | Which policy dates and vehicle identifiers were reviewed? |
| Another account of the crash was accepted | Original photos, full video, witness details and the collision report | Which fact is disputed and what evidence supports that position? |
| An expense was not linked to the collision | Itemized bill, treatment record, estimate or explanation of the loss | Which part is considered unrelated, and why? |
| A policy exclusion or limit applies | The complete policy and relevant endorsements | Which provision applies to this person, vehicle, use or expense? |
| A document or response was missing | Submission receipt, attachment list and earlier requests | Was the supplied record received, and what remains missing? |

This table is a way to organize questions, not a prediction that every denial can be reversed. Preserve unfavorable records too. A review needs the actual policy and full evidence, including material that may support the insurer’s position.

## Build a response that a new reviewer can follow

Begin with the claim number and date of the decision. State which conclusion you dispute, identify the attached evidence, and explain how it addresses the stated reason. Ask for reconsideration and a written response. Avoid sending the same large packet repeatedly without identifying the missing or misunderstood point.

For example, suppose the carrier says a repair charge relates to older damage. A useful response could identify the dated photograph showing that panel before the collision, the shop’s inspection findings, and the specific estimate line in dispute. A statement that the entire car was perfect supplies less information. If you do not have a dated image, say so; do not turn an assumption into evidence.

Number attachments and keep the exact version sent. If a portal limits uploads, record which files were accepted and use the company’s stated process for the remainder. Save delivery confirmations and follow up on a missing acknowledgement. A clear record helps distinguish a disagreement about the evidence from a failure to receive it.

## What if the report or medical history is part of the dispute?

A collision report can contain an inaccurate vehicle position, an incomplete witness account, or information recorded before all evidence was available. Identify the specific entry and supporting record. The [collision report correction guide](/blogs/correct-inaccurate-car-accident-police-report-california) explains how to request review by the agency. Keep the original report and any supplement. An insurer does not have to change its assessment simply because a correction was requested.

An injury denial may instead say the symptoms came from an earlier condition. Compare the symptoms, treatment and daily functioning before and after the crash. The [pre-existing injury guide](/blogs/car-accident-pre-existing-injury-california) explains why that comparison matters. Do not omit earlier care or ask a provider to change a medical opinion to fit the claim.

For a scooter or moped incident, the dispute may concern the type of device or its use. Save the model, specifications and trip records. The [moped and scooter comparison](/blogs/mopeds-vs-scooters-california) helps identify the classification question without assuming that an ordinary car policy applies.

## Keep other payment questions moving

A denied liability claim does not automatically stop every other source of assistance. Check benefits that may apply under your own policy or health coverage. Each has its own terms, limits and obligations. Ask about a deductible, reimbursement rights, and whether a proposed settlement could affect a different claim before making a final decision.

If treatment bills are arriving, the [medical bills guide](/blogs/medical-bills-while-car-accident-claim-pending-california) explains how to track provider accounts while responsibility remains disputed. Keep a bill’s due date separate from an insurer’s anticipated response date. Do not assume the provider knows that an auto claim is pending or has agreed to wait for payment.

For vehicle storage or rental costs, request the dates and amounts still authorized. A reconsideration request does not automatically extend those benefits. Ask where the vehicle is, whether an inspection is outstanding, and what charges continue each day. Preserve evidence before arranging a move or repair when its condition is disputed.

## Reconsideration, a regulator complaint and legal action are different steps

You can ask the carrier or a supervisor to review a decision with additional evidence. Request the company’s process and the department responsible. Avoid assuming that a process called an appeal has the same rules as a health insurance appeal or a court appeal.

The [California Department of Insurance consumer help page](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/101-help/index.cfm) provides an insurance complaint route. An organized complaint identifies the insurer, dates, disputed conduct, decision letter, and attempts to resolve the issue. State what explanation or review you are requesting. Include relevant correspondence rather than a series of unsupported conclusions about the company’s motives.

A regulatory complaint does not itself establish that another driver was negligent or replace the steps needed to pursue a lawsuit. A disagreement also does not automatically establish insurance bad faith. That legal analysis depends on the policy relationship, facts and applicable duties. Bring the complete file to counsel if the denial affects substantial benefits or injuries.

## Protect deadlines while the company reviews the file

Do not treat an open claim, reconsideration request or complaint as an automatic extension of a lawsuit deadline. Policy conditions and uninsured motorist procedures can raise separate timing questions. A letter mentioning one date may not list every action necessary to preserve your rights.

The [California personal injury deadlines guide](/blogs/california-personal-injury-deadlines) explains why the parties and claim type matter. Identify any government involvement promptly. If a deadline may be close, get advice about preserving the claim while the coverage disagreement continues, rather than waiting for one more reply from an adjuster.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a denial mean I cannot sue the driver?

Not necessarily. A coverage decision and a claim against a person who caused harm raise different questions. Whether a lawsuit is appropriate depends on liability, damages, deadlines, available defendants and the practical ability to recover payment. Review the denial and evidence before choosing a route.

### Can the insurer deny only part of the claim?

Yes. A company may dispute one expense or type of coverage while accepting another. Ask which amounts or benefits remain unresolved and whether a payment requires a release. Keep vehicle damage and injury decisions separately identified even if they share a claim number.

### Is there a universal 40-day deadline to pay every claim?

No. California generally requires a decision within 40 days after proof of claim, subject to applicable exceptions. When more time is needed, the regulation provides for written explanations and continuing status notices. That is different from a promise that every claim must be paid 40 days after the crash. Ask what has been received, what remains under investigation and which rule applies.

### Will filing a complaint extend my lawsuit deadline?

Do not assume so. Check the applicable deadline independently and obtain advice about any action required to preserve the claim. Keep the complaint confirmation, but do not use its pending status as a substitute for that review.

### What should I bring when asking a lawyer to review a denial?

Bring the full decision, policy and endorsements, claim correspondence, original evidence and a short chronology. Include what you sent, when it was received, any release or payment, and the date of the next requested response. Identify missing documents honestly so the review can address them.
