## Start with the difference between the claim and the renewal

After a crash, the claims team may decide what to pay under collision coverage. At renewal, the insurer calculates a premium using its applicable rating rules and your policy information. The fact that a claim was opened or paid does not, by itself, tell you whether an accident surcharge was applied.

This distinction matters when two events happen close together. You might make a claim in July and receive a more expensive renewal in September. During the same period, the insurer may also have changed its rates, you may have moved, or a different vehicle may have been added. The timing can suggest a question to ask. The declarations and the insurer’s explanation can tell you what actually changed.

Ask whether the premium changed because of an accident, a revised driver or vehicle classification, a different discount, or a broader rate change. Check the dollar amount and the period it covers. A six-month policy and a monthly payment plan can show different figures even when the underlying premium is the same. Compare the total premium for equivalent terms before calculating a percentage increase.

## How California treats fault for rating

California has standards for deciding whether an accident is principally at fault for auto insurance rating. The Department of Insurance explains that a renewal surcharge can apply when you are at least 51 percent at fault. The regulation also has conditions and presumptions, including rules about the type or amount of loss and circumstances such as a lawfully parked car. Ask what evidence and rule support any fault determination that affects your price.

An insurer’s rating decision is distinct from a civil claim for damages. Two people can share responsibility for a collision, and the parties to an injury claim can disagree about the allocation. Our [California comparative fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) explains how those percentages can affect an injury recovery. When you question a rate change, identify whether you are asking about rating, the claim’s liability decision, or both.

The Department of Insurance tells consumers that an accident they did not cause should not raise their premium on account of that accident. A bill can still be higher for an unrelated reason. If the insurer says the accident was not used, request the specific factors that explain the new amount. If it says the accident was used, ask for the date, responsible driver, and basis for its decision in writing. ## How to compare two declarations pages

Start with the old and new policy terms. Check the driver list, addresses, annual mileage, vehicles, use classification, coverage limits and deductibles. Then compare the discounts and premiums by vehicle. A change on one car may account for most of the increase even if the notice displays only the household total. Save both documents rather than relying on a screenshot of the amount due.

If the carrier applied a surcharge, ask when it took effect, how long the insurer will consider the event under the applicable rules, and when it will be reviewed. The exact impact can vary by driver, policy and insurer. A nationwide percentage estimate cannot tell you how much your California carrier changed this premium.

Ask how any advertised forgiveness feature works on your actual policy. A marketing phrase is not evidence that a particular policy includes the feature or that it applies to an accident. Get the policy or rating explanation that addresses your facts. Keep this inquiry separate from decisions about accident reporting and from whether an insurer will pay a claim.

### A renewal example with more than one change

Suppose your six-month premium goes from $920 to $1,120. You also changed cars and moved to a different ZIP code after reporting a collision. Start by asking whether the insurer included a rating surcharge for the crash and the dollar amount assigned to it. Then confirm the effect of the vehicle and address changes. Check whether the limits and deductible remained the same.

If the company incorrectly lists you as the driver when your car was parked, send the records that show what happened. Include dates, photos, a claim letter or police report if available, and the exact entry you want reviewed. Ask who is responsible for updating it and when you can expect a revised explanation. If the claims department says your car was parked but underwriting has a different record, reference both communications in the request.

## What to do while the accident claim is still open

Report the crash as required by your policy and meet any separate legal reporting duties. Do not assume that avoiding a claim will resolve an insurance record question. Another driver may report the same crash and the information can surface later. Keep records of the facts and any insurer determination. The [California post-crash guide](/blogs/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident-in-california) explains what to preserve after the collision.

If you compare quotes after a price change, match the coverage and limits first. A lower price can reflect a higher deductible, a removed driver, or reduced uninsured motorist coverage. Ask for the quote in writing. Compare it with the declarations rather than the renewal notice alone. A quote is a useful benchmark even if you decide to keep the existing policy.

If the collision caused an injury, keep your damages and coverage review separate from the renewal issue. The [California at-fault insurance guide](/blogs/is-california-a-no-fault-state-or-an-at-fault-state) explains the difference between an available benefit and responsibility for causing the crash. A [car accident claim review](/practice-areas/car-accidents) can focus on the injury and evidence while you ask the insurer about your price.

## How to dispute a rating entry without reopening every issue

Start with the specific entry. Ask for the accident date, vehicle, driver and fault designation. If a renewal reflects an entry for a former vehicle or another driver, send the relevant policy dates or vehicle records. If the date is correct and the fault designation is disputed, state which facts the insurer overlooked and attach the documents that support your position. A photo showing a lawfully parked car, for example, answers a different question from an estimate showing what the repair cost.

Keep a copy of the notice and your written response. Ask when the company will review the entry, who owns that review, and whether it will send a revised explanation. If you must make a payment to maintain coverage while the issue is open, ask how a corrected premium would be handled. Keep the policy in force and avoid relying on an oral promise of a credit.

If the carrier gives a general response about statewide rates, ask it to separate any change for the policy as a whole from an accident-specific adjustment. If it changed an unrelated factor, such as mileage, you can check that figure against your records. A disagreement about one entry should not obscure other changes that you can verify. The Department of Insurance can help consumers understand the process when an explanation remains unclear.

### Why the renewal and the injury file need different evidence

A claims adjuster may need treatment records to evaluate an injury. A rating reviewer may need the accident date, driver information, fault evidence and the policy declarations. Keep both sets organized and share the records appropriate to the request. If a claim later resolves without an admission of fault, ask how the insurer reached its separate rating determination. Do not assume a settlement automatically corrects every insurance record.

## Compare replacement coverage before ending the current policy

If you shop for a different insurer while questioning a renewal, obtain the complete quote and confirm what still needs to happen for coverage to begin. An application, a payment request and an issued policy can represent different stages. Ask for the effective date and time, listed drivers and vehicles, limits, deductibles and any exclusions. If the vehicle is financed or leased, check the coverage requirements in that agreement too.

Keep the new coverage confirmation with the instructions for ending the old policy. Ask the existing insurer how it calculates any refund or remaining balance and retain the cancellation confirmation. Check that the dates match what you intended. A lower advertised installment is difficult to compare when it excludes fees, uses a different policy term or removes coverage that you planned to keep.

If your household also insures a motorcycle, compare its declarations separately. Confirm whether a quoted household price includes the bike and which coverages appear on the motorcycle policy. The [California motorcycle insurance guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-insurance) explains the distinction between liability coverage, damage to the bike and benefits for an injured rider. Do not treat two quotes as equivalent until the vehicles and benefits match.

When the disputed event involved an unattended vehicle, the [parked-car accident guide](/blogs/what-to-do-if-someone-hits-your-parked-car-california) can help you assemble the time window, parking location and evidence supporting that account. Put the carrier's rating explanation beside those records. That creates a focused request for review while you separately decide which policy best meets your needs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my insurance go up if another driver rear-ends me?

California’s Department of Insurance says an accident you did not cause should not produce an accident surcharge. If your renewal rose, ask whether the insurer assigned you fault or changed the premium for another reason. A rear-end impact is a fact in the investigation, and the available evidence still matters.

### Does making a collision claim automatically increase my rate?

A payment under collision coverage and a rating decision serve different purposes. Ask how the carrier classified the accident for rating and which facts it used. Check your actual renewal for any change.

### How long will an accident affect my premium?

Ask your insurer which rule and lookback period apply to the renewal and when the event will no longer be used. The answer depends on the rating issue and policy. An online average from another state cannot establish your carrier’s calculation.

### What if my insurer got the accident wrong?

Request the written basis for its decision and identify the specific error. Send supporting records, keep confirmation of delivery, and ask when the company will review or correct the record. The California Department of Insurance can provide consumer assistance if the disagreement remains unresolved.

### Will recovering my deductible remove an accident surcharge?

Ask the insurer how it classified the accident for rating and whether new information changes that decision. The [subrogation process](/blogs/what-is-subrogation-car-insurance-california) concerns recovery of payments and may have a different timeline. A returned deductible is a record worth keeping, but obtain the rating reviewer's explanation of any effect on the renewal rather than assuming the two files were updated together.
