But Disneyland has also experienced serious accidents. Some involved rides, while others happened in areas outside the attractions.

So, how many people have died at Disneyland?

Historical reports confirm at least 10 fatal incidents associated with Disneyland Park between 1955 and 2003. More deaths have been reported in the years since, including incidents involving employees and medical emergencies. The exact total depends on whether the count includes only attraction accidents or every reported death across the Disneyland Resort.

## How many people have died at Disneyland?

To understand these numbers, it helps to look at the incidents, what investigators discovered, and how safety rules have changed.

There is no single, consistently defined public total covering every type of death at Disneyland since its opening.

Contemporary reporting in September 2003 described Marcelo Torres as the tenth death in Disneyland’s historical count. That figure was a snapshot from 2003, rather than a verified total for every later guest, employee, hotel, or medical incident. [Contemporary 2003 reporting](https://www.deseret.com/2003/9/6/19782721/disneyland-ride-kills-one-and-injures-10/)

That does not mean only 10 people have ever died at the resort.

Later reports include incidents involving attraction passengers, employees, and people experiencing medical emergencies. Some deaths occurred inside Disneyland Park. Others happened elsewhere on resort property.

These distinctions matter because a medical emergency near a ride is not necessarily an accident caused by that ride.

## Matterhorn Bobsleds: 1964

The first widely documented fatal accident at Disneyland occurred in May 1964 on Matterhorn Bobsleds.

Mark Maples, a 15 year old visitor, fell from a bobsled and died several days later.

Early accounts offered different explanations for how he left the ride vehicle. Some reports suggested unsafe behavior, but later historical reporting found that the available evidence did not establish one definite explanation.

Anaheim police ultimately classified the death as accidental. Historical reporting distinguishes the police finding from early speculation about the guest’s actions. [Matterhorn historical reporting](https://www.sfgate.com/disneyland/article/matterhorn-ride-deaths-disneyland-fatal-accidents-16285220.php)

The case remains part of Disneyland's history because it shows how important reliable evidence is when reviewing an amusement park accident. Initial witness reports do not always provide a complete picture of what happened.

## America Sings: 1974

Disneyland employees can face risks that visitors may never see.

In July 1974, 18 year old employee Deborah Gail Stone died while working at the America Sings attraction.

The attraction used a rotating theater to move audiences between different performance scenes. Stone was working near the moving theater structure when the accident occurred.

Disneyland later introduced safety changes to the attraction, including modifications designed to prevent workers from entering dangerous spaces.

This incident brought attention to an important part of amusement park safety: protecting employees who work around moving equipment. Our [California workers’ compensation filing guide](/blogs/workers-comp-step-by-step-process-of-filing-a-workers-comp-claim-in-california) explains the separate process for qualifying work injuries.

Routine inspections, safe operating procedures, and proper training matter just as much behind the scenes as they do for guests.

## Matterhorn Bobsleds: 1984

The Matterhorn Bobsleds experienced another fatal incident on January 3, 1984.

Regina Young, 48, fell from a bobsled and was struck by another ride vehicle.

At the time, Disneyland representatives said they did not know exactly how she had left the vehicle. Reports did not identify a confirmed mechanical breakdown. [Contemporary report on the 1984 accident](https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1984/01/04/Tourist-killed-in-fall-from-Matterhorn-bobsled/2012442040400/)

The 1964 and 1984 deaths are often discussed together because they occurred on the same attraction, roughly 20 years apart.

They also demonstrate why it is important to separate confirmed facts from assumptions about a guest's actions or an attraction's condition.

## Sailing Ship Columbia: 1998

On December 24, 1998, a serious accident occurred while the Sailing Ship Columbia was approaching its dock in Disneyland's Frontierland area.

A metal fixture used during docking came loose and struck people nearby.

Luan Phi Dawson, 33, later died from his injuries. His wife and a Disneyland employee were also seriously injured. [Contemporary Columbia accident report](https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1999/02/19/Damage-found-on-second-Disneyland-boat/6475919400400/)

The accident led to investigations involving the attraction's equipment, maintenance practices, and employee training.

Unlike some earlier accidents involving guests entering restricted areas, this incident raised serious questions about equipment safety during normal park operations.

It became one of Disneyland's most widely reported fatal accidents.

## Big Thunder Mountain Railroad: 2003

One of the most significant Disneyland ride accidents occurred on September 5, 2003.

A train derailed on Big Thunder Mountain Railroad, killing 22 year old Marcelo Torres and injuring 10 other passengers.

Investigators found problems with maintenance procedures and mechanical components.

A California safety investigation identified failures involving ride maintenance, inspections, and operating practices. [State accident-investigation report, archived copy](https://ridesdatabase.org/accident_reports/CA_911333.pdf)

Disney later accepted responsibility for the accident and settled a lawsuit with Torres' family in 2005. The settlement amount was not publicly disclosed.

Following the incident, Disneyland introduced additional maintenance and employee training measures.

The case is an important example of how an amusement park accident can result in both legal action and changes to safety procedures.

## Backstage golf-cart accident: 2024

On June 5, 2024, Disneyland employee Bonnye Mavis Lear was involved in an accident in a backstage area of the resort.

According to Anaheim police, Lear fell from a moving golf cart and was taken to a hospital. She died on June 7.

Lear was 60 years old and had worked at Disneyland for approximately 24 years. [Associated Press reporting on the employee accident](https://apnews.com/article/bcb3e4295a86bddab7a9dc4fb370533c)

The incident did not involve a guest attraction. However, it highlighted the importance of workplace safety across the wider Disneyland Resort.

When discussing Disneyland deaths, employee accidents should be identified separately from guest ride incidents so readers understand the circumstances.

## Medical emergency: 2025

On October 6, 2025, a woman in her 60s experienced a medical emergency after riding Disneyland's Haunted Mansion attraction.

Emergency services responded, and the woman was taken to a hospital, where she was pronounced dead.

Anaheim police reported that there was no indication of an operating problem with the attraction. [Reporting on the police statement](https://www.foxla.com/news/haunted-mansion-disneyland-death-medical-episode.amp)

This distinction is especially important.

A death occurring after a theme park ride does not automatically mean the ride caused it. Establishing a cause requires reliable medical or investigative evidence.

Reports about the event should therefore describe it as a medical emergency rather than a confirmed ride malfunction.

## Understanding attraction safety and risk

Disneyland operates many attractions, from gentle family rides to roller coasters.

The historical record shows that serious accidents have occurred, but it does not establish that every attraction has the same risk.

Some reported incidents resulted from equipment or maintenance failures. Others involved unsafe behavior, workplace hazards, or medical conditions.

It is also important to remember that millions of people have visited Disneyland over the decades.

Historical fatality counts alone cannot provide an accurate current risk percentage. That would require reliable figures for ride usage, types of incidents, and the time periods being compared.

Visitors should follow posted attraction warnings, use the required restraints, and speak with an employee if they have concerns about boarding or accessibility.

## California amusement ride inspections

California has a dedicated inspection program for permanent amusement rides.

The California Division of Occupational Safety and Health oversees this program through its Amusement Ride and Tramway Unit. [Cal/OSHA amusement ride inspection program](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/AROffices.htm)

Under California regulations, permanent amusement rides are subject to several safety requirements.

Qualified safety inspectors conduct inspections covering ride structures and operations.

Operators must maintain records related to inspections, maintenance, and other safety procedures.

Ride operators must follow applicable maintenance requirements and ensure authorized personnel perform inspection and maintenance tasks.

Training is particularly important because employees need to recognize when an attraction may not be operating normally.

Certain serious accidents must be reported to state authorities.

Regulators can also conduct additional inspections after receiving reports of accidents or credible safety concerns.

These requirements are intended to identify problems before they place visitors or employees at risk.

## How fatal accidents are investigated

When a fatal accident occurs at a theme park, the circumstances may be reviewed by emergency services, law enforcement, workplace safety officials, or other investigators.

The agencies involved depend on where the incident happened and whether it involved an attraction, an employee, or another type of emergency.

An investigation may examine:

1. Whether the attraction or equipment was operating properly.
2. Whether required inspections and maintenance were completed.

3. Whether employees followed established safety procedures.
4. Whether the park responded appropriately to the emergency.

5. Whether unsafe conditions or another party's negligence contributed to the accident.

Not every death at a theme park results in legal liability.

For example, a medical emergency unrelated to park operations may involve very different legal questions from a death caused by a documented equipment failure. ## California wrongful death claims

Under California law, certain surviving family members may be eligible to file a wrongful death claim when a death results from another party's wrongful act or negligence.

California Code of Civil Procedure Section 377.60 identifies who may bring such a claim.

Depending on the circumstances, eligible individuals may include a surviving spouse, domestic partner, children, or certain other qualifying relatives or dependents.

A successful claim generally requires evidence supporting legal responsibility. ## Frequently asked questions

### Has anyone died at Disneyland?

Yes. Fatal incidents have been documented at Disneyland since the 1960s. They include attraction accidents, employee incidents, and other emergencies.

### How many Disneyland deaths have been reported?

Historical reporting identified 10 fatal incidents associated with Disneyland Park between 1955 and September 2003. Additional deaths have been reported since then. There is no single verified public total that consistently includes every category of death across the entire resort.

### What was the first widely documented fatality?

The first widely documented fatality was Mark Maples, a 15 year old guest who died after a Matterhorn Bobsleds accident in May 1964.

### Have there been fatal Matterhorn accidents?

Yes. Two widely documented fatal accidents occurred on Matterhorn Bobsleds, in 1964 and 1984.

### Was there a fatal Big Thunder Mountain Railroad accident?

Yes. Marcelo Torres, 22, died in a train derailment on September 5, 2003. Ten other passengers were injured.

### Was a medical emergency reported in October 2025?

### Are permanent amusement rides inspected in California?

Yes. Permanent amusement rides in California are subject to safety requirements, including inspections, maintenance records, and specified incident reporting procedures.

A family may have legal options if there is evidence that negligence or another legally wrongful act caused the death. Whether a claim is possible depends on the circumstances and California law.
