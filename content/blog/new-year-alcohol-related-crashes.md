## What national crash data show

NHTSA reports that 11,904 people died in alcohol-impaired driving crashes in the United States in 2024. That figure covers the full year. It gives context for the harm impaired driving causes across the country. [NHTSA drunk driving statistics](https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drunk-driving)

## Make a plan for getting home

If you expect to drink, arrange a ride with a sober friend, book a ride, or check when the last train or bus leaves. Agree on a well-lit pickup point and decide on a backup if the first option falls through. Hosts can share the plan before guests arrive.

## What to document after a collision

Get to safety and call for emergency help if needed. If it is safe to collect information, save the time and location, witness contact details, photos, and original dashcam footage. Record what you observed directly. The movement of a vehicle and the condition of its driver are separate questions for investigators.

## What holiday crash numbers can tell you

A national statistic is a count of what happened across a defined area and period. The 2024 figure above includes the full year. For a closer look at seasonal travel, NHTSA separately reported 1,038 deaths in drunk-driving traffic crashes during December 2023. That count covers the entire month, including days well before New Year’s Eve. Read each number with its date range and definition so it stays useful. [NHTSA December impaired-driving data](https://www.nhtsa.gov/drive-sober-december-and-every-month)

The agency also runs a winter holiday impaired-driving campaign. Its 2024 campaign ran into the first day of the new year and encouraged people to make a sober transportation plan. A campaign window tells you when the agency focused its outreach. It does not give an estimate of the chance that a person leaving a particular party will be in a crash. [NHTSA winter holiday campaign](https://www.nhtsa.gov/speeches-presentations/drive-sober-winter-holiday-campaign-2024)

That distinction matters when comparing a headline about holiday crashes with an annual report. Ask whether the figure counts crashes, people injured, or people killed. Then check whether it applies to one state, a group of states, or the entire country. A higher crash count can reflect a number of factors, including the amount of driving. An individual travel plan can still address the risks you can control, such as how you will get home and whether your driver will stay sober.

## Why the trip home deserves its own plan

You may know where you are going hours before you know how you will leave. A venue can be easy to reach in the afternoon and hard to leave once service slows or streets close. Check the last transit departure, the walking route to the stop, and a place where you can wait safely if a ride is delayed. Put those details in the group chat before people arrive.

If a friend is driving, agree on who that is and confirm that the person will stay sober. Make sure everybody has a seat and a working belt; our [California seat belt guide](/blogs/california-seat-belt-laws) explains restraint requirements and fit. If plans change and the designated driver starts drinking or decides to leave early, move to the backup plan. A sober friend, another available ride, or a safe place to stay can keep the group from making the decision in a hurry.

At a large event, pick a landmark that people can recognize when it is crowded. Save the pickup location before your phone battery runs low. If you are using a rideshare, confirm the vehicle and plate against the app before getting in. A little extra time at the pickup point is easier to plan for than a last-minute walk through a congested traffic lane.

## For hosts, settle transportation before the party

Ask guests how they plan to leave when they arrive. If several people are sharing a driver, make sure their expected departure times work together. If you know the event will end after transit service, share alternatives early. Keep a safe indoor waiting area available if practical, especially when people are waiting on a late pickup.

A guest who decides to drive after drinking can put everyone in a difficult position. Keep the conversation about the next step. Offer to call a sober friend or help book a ride. If there is an immediate danger, contact law enforcement from a safe place and give the location and vehicle description.

## Alcohol can affect a drive before the legal limit

A drive home can demand quick decisions even on familiar streets. A pedestrian may step out from behind a parked car, or the driver ahead may brake suddenly while looking for a turn. Alcohol can affect coordination, judgment, and the ability to divide attention. The usual adult BAC threshold in California is a legal standard, and impairment can occur below it. The DMV also describes different thresholds for some drivers. [California DMV alcohol and drugs handbook](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/california-driver-handbook/alcohol-and-drugs/)

Holiday plans can also change when someone is tired or taking medication. Check warnings about drowsiness and interactions if you take a medicine, and speak with a pharmacist or prescriber if you have questions about driving. If you expect to be too tired to drive safely at the end of the evening, plan another way home while you still have options.

## If a collision happens during the holiday

Once you are safe, write down the time, direction of travel, and the nearest intersection. Late at night, it can be hard to remember whether an incident happened before or after midnight. Use a timestamped message, a ride receipt, or an emergency call record to help confirm the date. Record what you saw directly, and keep other people’s statements separate from your own notes.

Save dashcam footage and photographs in their original form. If a witness says a nearby business has a camera, record where it is and what direction it faces. If you are hurt, seek appropriate medical care and keep treatment records. A collision report can help establish who responded and what was reported. Medical records and repair estimates address different parts of the loss. [California Courts personal injury guidance](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury)

Tell your insurer which records you have and ask what it needs next. Save the claim number and any written deadlines. If someone was injured or killed, or property damage exceeds the DMV reporting threshold, California may also require an SR-1 report within ten days. A police report and an insurance claim are separate from that DMV filing. [California DMV SR-1 instructions](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/dmv-virtual-office/accident-reporting/)

If you are speaking with a lawyer, bring the records that document the collision, treatment, and insurance contacts. Identify gaps, such as a missing report number or a witness whose name you did not get. The person evaluating the claim can help decide which records matter and which deadlines apply. A holiday date on the calendar can explain the setting, while the evidence about the vehicles, people, and injuries determines how the claim is evaluated.

## Three trips to plan when the group is splitting up

Think about the ride to the event, the ride home, and the trip to collect a parked car the next day. A plan that covers only the middle trip can leave someone feeling pressured to take their car home at the end of the night. Check the venue’s parking rules before leaving the vehicle. Make sure the driver has a way to return later and has enough time to do so safely.

If friends live in different directions, decide where each person will be dropped off. Someone who lives farther away may need a separate ride rather than an extra stop nobody agreed to. Share a contact number and check that everybody knows how to reach their ride if they leave the venue at different times. The goal is a plan each person can follow even if the group is no longer together.

A sober ride still involves walking to the pickup point. Choose a route with sidewalks and marked crossings when available. If the location is crowded, find a place to wait that keeps you clear of turning cars and gives the driver a safe place to stop. Confirm the ride in the app or with the person picking you up before stepping into the street.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can alcohol affect driving below the legal limit?

Yes. Alcohol can affect judgment and coordination before a driver reaches the usual adult alcohol threshold. If you are drinking, arrange a sober ride.

### What if I cannot get a rideshare after midnight?

Use your backup plan. Call a sober friend, use available transit, or wait in a safe place until a ride is available.

### What if I suspect another driver was impaired?

If the situation is dangerous, contact law enforcement from a safe place. Describe observable conduct and the vehicle’s location.

### What should a host plan ahead of time?

Share transport options, make a clear pickup plan, and help guests arrange a ride with someone sober.

### What if I have footage of the collision?

Save the original file and note when and where it was recorded. Keep a separate copy if you need to share it.
