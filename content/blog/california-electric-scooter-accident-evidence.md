## Start with the scooter and the trip

After addressing immediate safety and medical needs, capture the ride receipt, trip ID, time and map from your account. Save the scooter’s QR label or serial number if you can do so safely. Keep messages to and from the rental company, including any case number. A trip ending on the map does not necessarily establish where the impact occurred, so write down the nearest address, cross street and direction of travel separately.

For a privately owned scooter, keep the make, model, photographs, purchase information and any maintenance history. If you noticed a brake or steering problem before the crash, write down what you experienced. Preserve the scooter’s condition and avoid attempting to reproduce the failure.

## Identify the road feature that matters

A turning-car collision and a fall after hitting a pavement defect need different evidence. For the first, document the driveway, turning path, signals, paint and sight lines. For the second, capture the defect, its location relative to the curb or lane, and wider photographs that help show visibility. Take photos only from a safe place. Nearby witnesses or businesses may have video. Note the camera location and the time range so an owner can find the right recording.

The [California right-of-way guide](/blogs/right-of-way-accident-claims-california) explains how road layout and movements matter when accounts conflict. If a roadway condition might involve a government entity, check the [California Courts guide to government claims](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/government-claim) promptly because a different claims process and shorter deadlines may apply.

## California scooter rules can help frame the questions

California’s rules for stand-up motorized scooters differ from those for mopeds and other devices marketed as scooters. A motorized scooter operator needs a valid driver’s license or instruction permit. Operators under 18 must wear a properly fitted bicycle helmet. California also has a 15 mph operating limit and restrictions on sidewalks and higher-speed roads.

The [California Highway Patrol’s vehicle comparison](https://www.chp.ca.gov/programs-services/programs/california-motorcyclist-safety/motorcycles-and-similar-vehicles) and [CHP’s e-bike and e-scooter guide](https://sr06.senate.ca.gov/sites/sr06.senate.ca.gov/files/E-BIKE-Laws-and-Safety-CHP-flyer.pdf) describe the rules. Start with the actual vehicle and location, then check whether a bikeway or local rule affects the analysis. The [moped and scooter comparison](/blogs/mopeds-vs-scooters-california) explains the different vehicle classes in more detail.

## Find out which insurance policies may apply

If a car was involved, keep the driver’s insurance information and any police report number. If it was a rental scooter, save the rental agreement in effect for your trip and ask the operator about available coverage in writing. If a delivery or other work activity was involved, preserve the assignment and timing records. Coverage depends on the parties, facts and policy language. The [third-party auto insurance claim guide](/blogs/third-party-car-insurance-claim-california) explains what to document when making a claim with another driver’s insurer.

Keep equipment losses and medical records in separate folders. Photograph the scooter and other damaged property, and retain treatment bills and missed-work records. If someone offers to reimburse a specific item, read what the payment would settle before signing a release.
