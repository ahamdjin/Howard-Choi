## Do passengers have to wear a motorcycle helmet?

Yes. The [California DMV Motorcycle Handbook](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/motorcycle-driver-handbook-pdf/) explains that Vehicle Code section 27803 applies to both operators and passengers on motorcycles, motor-driven cycles and motorized bicycles. A helmet must fit and be securely fastened. A passenger’s obligation is separate from any restrictions on carrying passengers under a motorcycle permit. See the [California motorcycle license and permit guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-license-permit-requirements) for that distinction.

## What does the DOT label show?

On a modern helmet sold in the United States, look for the label on the rear showing the manufacturer or brand, model, DOT, FMVSS No. 218 and certification wording. [NHTSA’s helmet guide](https://www.nhtsa.gov/motorcycle-safety/choose-right-motorcycle-helmet) explains what the marking means, why labels on older helmets differ and how to check fit. The label reflects manufacturer certification. NHTSA does not individually approve each helmet model before it is sold, and counterfeit labels exist.

If you still have the packaging, receipt or product listing, keep it with your records. Those documents can help identify the model when the label is scratched or partly missing.

## Keep the helmet and evidence of how the crash happened

Once immediate medical and safety needs are addressed, photograph the helmet from several angles. Include the strap, buckle, label, visible marks and any areas damaged or cut during rescue. Write down who collected it and when. Preserve its condition and seek advice before discarding it or surrendering it for an inspection.

The helmet is one item in a larger record. Save the report number, witness names, video and photos showing lane positions or impact points. The [California motorcycle accident guide](/blogs/california-motorcycle-accident-guide) explains how these facts fit together. If a driver turned across the motorcycle’s path, evidence of the turn and the traffic controls can still matter even when the parties disagree about helmet use.

## Can helmet use affect a California injury claim?

An insurer may argue that a helmet issue contributed to particular injuries. The questions are what the evidence shows about the helmet and whether there is a connection to the injury being claimed. An injury mechanism may need medical or other expert evaluation. Preserve the original account and records before trying to infer what happened from damage to the helmet alone.

See the [California comparative fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) for how disputed responsibility is evaluated. The [brain injury evidence guide](/blogs/traumatic-brain-injury-evidence-after-california-accident) covers early symptoms, treatment records and observations from people who knew the rider before the collision.
