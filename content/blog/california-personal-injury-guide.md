## What is a personal injury lawsuit in California?

A claim can begin with a report to an insurer and a request for payment. A civil lawsuit begins when a plaintiff files a complaint with the court. The complaint identifies the parties and explains the legal basis for relief. After filing, the plaintiff generally needs to serve the other party with the required papers. The [California Courts guide to suing someone](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/plaintiff) distinguishes filing, service, and the later steps in a court case.

For example, a driver with a broken wrist may start by sending medical and wage records to the other driver’s insurer. An open claim number shows that the carrier has a file. It gives no answer about whether the carrier accepts fault, whether the policy will cover the loss, or when a court complaint is due. Keep insurance correspondence and the court deadline in separate entries. If a crash also totaled the car, use the [California total-loss guide](/blogs/what-happens-when-car-is-totaled-california) to track the valuation, rental, and loan payoff separately.

Once a lawsuit is filed, the parties may exchange information through discovery, take depositions, or ask a judge to resolve procedural or legal issues. Some disputes settle through negotiations or mediation. Others go to trial. You can still discuss settlement after filing. [California Courts guide to preparing a case](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/prepare-case)

## How to file a personal injury claim in California

Start by identifying which process you need. Giving an insurer notice of an injury, requesting compensation with supporting records, and filing a civil lawsuit are different actions. An insurer's claim number is not a court case number. The [California Courts personal injury overview](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/personal-injury) explains the civil case context; the steps below help you prepare the information for an individual review.

### Identify the event and the people involved

Write a short account with the date, exact location, people or businesses involved, and the injury you are reporting. Distinguish what you saw from what someone later told you. Include a report reference if you have one, but do not invent a complete account to fill a gap. Keep the original photographs and correspondence so an account can be checked against its source.

### Find the right claim contact and keep proof of delivery

Identify which insurer or representative is handling which part of the event. Record the claim number, adjuster's contact and the date the claim was reported. If coverage or the correct recipient is uncertain, say that during the consultation. Keep the acknowledgement and a copy of what you actually sent, including any attachments. A message saved as a draft does not show that a recipient received it.

### Organize the records by the question they answer

Scene photographs, witness details and reports help explain the event. Treatment records, work restrictions, bills and income records help explain its consequences. Keep paid bills separate from open balances, estimates and costs paid by another source. Mark records that have been requested but have not arrived. The [case-value guide](/blogs/how-much-is-my-personal-injury-case-worth-california) explains why those distinctions matter to an evaluation.

### Check the court deadline separately from the insurance discussion

Ask which filing deadline applies to the actual people and facts. Do not treat an open claim file or ongoing negotiations as a substitute for checking the time to sue. A lawsuit begins through the court process, including a complaint and the required service steps. [California Courts explains the questions to consider before suing](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/before-sue). See the [California injury-deadlines guide](/blogs/california-personal-injury-deadlines) for the issues to raise promptly.

### Use the correct guide for a workplace injury

A workers' compensation claim uses a different process from an ordinary personal injury claim. If the event happened while working, identify the employer and the work activity as well as any other person or business involved. The [workers' compensation filing guide](/blogs/workers-comp-step-by-step-process-of-filing-a-workers-comp-claim-in-california) addresses that system. A work injury can raise questions about a separate third-party claim, but the existence of one process does not complete the other.

### Make the first consultation specific

Tell the office the decision you need help with: reporting the event, responding to a request, reviewing an offer, or checking a deadline. Bring the available records and a list of what is missing. ## What Is Personal Injury Law?

Personal injury law gives injured individuals the right to seek compensation when someone else’s negligence or wrongful actions cause them harm. The goal is to help victims recover physically, emotionally, and financially, restoring them as much as possible to their pre-accident condition.

Most personal injury claims arise from events such as:

- Motor vehicle accidents
- Slip and fall incidents
- Dog bites
- Unsafe property conditions

### What Is Negligence?

Negligence is at the heart of most personal injury cases. It means someone failed to act with reasonable care, leading to another person’s injury.

**Example:** A driver runs a red light and crashes into another car. Because they ignored traffic laws and endangered others, they can be held legally responsible for the resulting injuries.

Personal injury law allows you to seek financial compensation when another person or entity’s negligence causes you harm.

## Common Types of Personal Injury Cases

Personal injury law covers a wide range of incidents where someone’s negligence causes bodily injury and financial loss. These are some of the most common and often most life-altering cases we see at our firm.

### Motorcycle Accidents

[Motorcyclists](/practice-areas/motorcycle-accidents) face a higher risk of severe injuries due to their lack of physical protection. Even careful riders are vulnerable when other drivers fail to check blind spots, speed, or drive distracted.

### Truck Accidents

[Crashes involving commercial trucks](/practice-areas/truck-accidents), such as 18-wheelers or delivery vehicles, often lead to serious consequences due to their size and weight. Determining liability can be complex, involving multiple parties such as the truck driver, the trucking company, or even a third-party maintenance provider.

### Pedestrian & Bicycle Accidents

[Pedestrians](/practice-areas/pedestrian-accidents) and [bicyclists](/practice-areas/bicycle-accidents) must share the road with vehicles, but this can lead to catastrophic injuries if drivers fail to yield, become distracted, or drive recklessly. If an accident occurs involving a negligent vehicle driver, the injured pedestrian or bicyclist can file a claim to pursue damages for their medical expenses, lost wages, and more.

### Slip and Fall

When a property owner [fails to maintain](/practice-areas/slip-and-fall) a safe environment, they may be held liable for injuries caused by hazards like wet floors, broken stairs, or poor lighting. These are known as premises liability cases and can happen in both public and private settings.

### Dog Bites

In many states, dog owners are strictly liable if their [animal attacks](/practice-areas/dog-bites) someone in a public place or on private property where the victim is lawfully present. Even if the dog has never shown aggression before, the owner may still be responsible.

### Wrongful Death

A [wrongful death claim](/practice-areas/wrongful-death) arises when someone is killed due to another person’s negligence or misconduct. These lawsuits are filed by surviving family members to recover damages for funeral expenses, lost financial support, and emotional suffering.

### Catastrophic Injury

[Catastrophic injuries](/practice-areas/serious-injuries), such as traumatic brain injuries, spinal cord damage, or paralysis, can change a person’s life forever. These cases typically involve significant medical costs, long-term care needs, and a profound impact on the victim’s ability to work or live independently.

### Workers’ Compensation

Employees injured on the job are often covered by workers’ compensation insurance. However, if a third party (such as a contractor or equipment manufacturer) played a role in the injury, an additional personal injury claim may be possible.

From car accidents to dog bites, personal injury cases stem from negligent actions that result in physical, emotional, or financial damage.

## Overview

### Proving Negligence: Duty of Care, Breach, Causation, and Damages

To win a personal injury claim, you must prove that the other party was legally at fault. This involves establishing [four essential elements of negligence](/blogs/elements-of-negligence-california):

- **Duty of Care** The defendant (at-fault party) had a legal obligation to act with reasonable care to prevent harm. For example, all drivers have a duty to follow traffic laws and drive safely.
- **Breach of Duty** The defendant failed to meet that obligation. This could involve reckless driving, ignoring safety rules, or failing to maintain property.
- **Causation** The breach directly caused your injury. There must be a clear link between the defendant’s actions (or inaction) and your harm.
- **Damages** You suffered measurable losses as a result of the injury, such as medical expenses, lost wages, or emotional distress.

**Example of negligence:**

A distracted driver runs a stop sign and hits a cyclist. The driver had a duty to obey traffic laws, breached that duty by failing to stop, caused the collision, and the cyclist suffered injuries and financial losses.

### Comparative Negligence: What Happens If You’re Partially at Fault?

In California, a claimant’s share of fault can reduce the amount recovered. See the [California comparative fault guide](/blogs/california-comparative-fault-personal-injury) for examples and ways to check a disputed fault percentage.

### Strict Liability: What It Means

Not all personal injury cases require proof of negligence. In strict liability cases, the defendant can be held responsible regardless of intent or care.

Common examples include:

- ** Dog bites** (in states with strict dog bite laws)
- ** Defective products**
- ** Ultrahazardous activities**, such as using explosives or handling toxic chemicals

In these cases, if someone is harmed, the responsible party is liable, even if they did everything reasonably possible to prevent harm.

To win a personal injury case, you must prove the other party had a duty of care, breached it, and caused you measurable harm.

## What Compensation Can You Receive?

Personal injury law allows you to seek financial compensation, also known as damages, to help you recover from your injuries. These damages typically fall into three categories: economic, non-economic, and, in rare cases, punitive.

### Economic Damages (Out-of-Pocket Costs)

- ** Medical expenses:**ER visits, surgeries, physical therapy, future care.
- ** Lost wages:**Past and future income if you’re unable to work.
- ** Loss of earning capacity:**Reduced ability to earn income due to the injury.
- ** Property damage:**Repair or replacement of damaged items.

### Non-Economic Damages (Intangible Losses)

- ** Pain and suffering:** Physical discomfort and emotional distress from the injury.
- ** Emotional distress:** Anxiety, depression, PTSD, or other mental health impacts.
- ** Loss of enjoyment of life:** Inability to participate in hobbies, exercise, travel, or other activities you once enjoyed.
- ** Loss of consortium:** Compensation for the negative impact on your relationship with a spouse or partner.

### Punitive Damages (Punishment for Wrongdoing)

California allows punitive damages in some cases upon clear and convincing proof of oppression, fraud, or malice under Civil Code section 3294. These damages serve to punish and deter qualifying conduct. [California civil jury instructions on punitive damages, CACI No. 3940](https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/judicial_council_of_california_civil_jury_instructions_2026.pdf).

Damages in personal injury cases include medical expenses, lost wages, emotional distress, and, in rare cases, punitive damages.

## Common Mistakes After a Personal Injury Claim (and How to Avoid Them)

Making the wrong move after an injury can jeopardize your health and your ability to recover damages from the other party. Here are some common mistakes to avoid:

- ** Delaying medical treatment:** Waiting too long to seek care can hurt your recovery and make it harder to prove your injuries.
- ** Failing to follow medical advice:** Ignoring your treatment can be used against you by insurers or defense attorneys.
- ** Not documenting everything:** Failing to keep records of symptoms, expenses, and conversations weakens your case.
- ** Talking to insurance adjusters alone:** They may try to get you to settle quickly or admit fault. Always consult an attorney first.
- ** Posting on social media:** Photos, check-ins, or even comments can be used to dispute your injury claims.
- ** Missing legal deadlines:** Statutes of limitations can bar your case if you wait too long to file.
- ** Accepting a low settlement:** Quick settlements often don’t cover long-term costs. A lawyer can help you assess the true value of your claim.

Common missteps, such as delaying treatment or posting on social media, can harm your claim. Protect yourself by acting strategically.

## The Personal Injury Claim Process

If you are filing a [personal injury case](/faqs/personal-injury) for the first time, here is a simple breakdown of the case process so you understand what happens at every stage:

- ** Free Consultation:** A [free meeting](/contact) with a personal injury expert to review the facts of your case and determine if you have a valid claim.
- ** Hire an Attorney:** If you decide to move forward, you’ll sign a retainer and contingency fee agreement, meaning your attorney only gets paid if you win or settle your case.
- ** Investigation & Evidence Gathering:** Your attorney will gather key evidence, including accident reports, medical records, photos, surveillance footage, and witness statements.
- ** File a Demand Letter:** Your attorney sends a demand letter to the insurance company, detailing your injuries, medical treatment, lost wages, and other damages. This starts the negotiation process.
- ** Negotiation Phase:** Most claims settle during this phase. Your attorney negotiates with the insurance company to reach a fair settlement.
- ** Settlement or Trial:** Many cases are resolved through settlement or mediation before trial. However, if necessary, your case may go to court, where a judge or jury will decide the outcome. Your attorney will represent you throughout the process.

Personal injury cases can take anywhere from a few months to a few years, depending on their complexity, the severity of the injuries, and the willingness to settle.

Most claims settle out of court, but the legal process involves consultation, evidence gathering, negotiation, and potentially trial.

## 4 Common Challenges in Personal Injury Cases

Personal injury cases can range from relatively straightforward to highly complex. Even when the facts seem clear, certain issues can complicate the process of securing fair compensation. Here are four common challenges victims often face:

### 1. Disputed Liability

Sometimes, it’s not immediately clear who caused the accident, or the at-fault party may argue you share responsibility. When liability is in question, gathering strong evidence like witness statements, photos, and expert testimony becomes essential.

### 2. Pre-Existing Conditions

If you had a medical condition or past injury before the accident, insurance companies may try to argue that your new injuries aren’t related. An attorney can help distinguish between old and new injuries with proper documentation.

### 3. Insurance Company Tactics

Insurers are trained to minimize payouts. They might delay your claim, offer far less than your case is worth, or try to shift blame onto you. Without legal guidance, it’s easy to accept an unfair settlement out of frustration or pressure.

### 4. Delayed Symptoms

Not all injuries are immediately obvious. Conditions like traumatic brain injuries, soft tissue damage, or internal bleeding can take weeks to manifest. If you delay medical treatment or legal action, it can weaken your case.

These challenges highlight why speaking with a personal injury lawyer early in the process is so important. A legal team can protect your rights, counter bad-faith insurance tactics, and help build a strong case for full compensation.

Factors like disputed liability, insurance tactics, and pre-existing conditions can complicate your case; legal help is essential.

## Personal Injury Glossary

** Breach of Duty**

The failure to meet the standard of care that a reasonable person would exercise in a similar situation. This is one of the four elements required to prove negligence in a personal injury case.

** Burden of Proof**

** Contingency Fee**

A fee arrangement where an attorney’s compensation is based on a percentage of the amount recovered in a lawsuit. If the lawsuit is unsuccessful, the attorney does not collect a fee. Most personal injury attorneys work on a contingent fee instead of an hourly or fixed fee.

** Damages**

The legal term for monetary compensation or losses. In a personal injury case, a plaintiff seeks damages due to harm caused by the defendant’s negligence. Damages are typically divided into two categories: economic and non-economic.

** Discovery**

The pre-trial legal process involves both parties in a lawsuit gathering evidence from each other through various methods, including interrogatories, document requests, and depositions.

** Duty of care**

The legal obligation of a person or organization to take reasonable care in their actions or omissions to prevent injury to others. If a person fails to meet the standard of care, their actions are considered negligent.

For example, a driver has a responsibility to follow all traffic laws and operate their vehicle safely to prevent harm to other drivers, pedestrians, cyclists, etc.

** Liability**

The legal responsibility that a person or organization has for their actions or omissions that cause harm or injury to another person or their property. If a defendant is liable in a personal injury case, then they are responsible for paying damages. Liability, negligence, and fault are often used interchangeably.

** Loss of Consortium**

Compensation awarded to the spouse or close family member of a personal injury victim for the loss of companionship, affection, sexual relations, or support resulting from the injury.

** Mediation**

A form of alternative dispute resolution where a neutral third party (mediator) helps both sides negotiate and reach a mutually acceptable settlement, often avoiding the need for a trial.

** Negligence**

The failure to take reasonable care, resulting in injury or wrongful death to another person. Another word for negligence is carelessness.

**Settlement**

An agreement resolving the claims described in its terms. Ask which parties and losses it covers and when payment is due.

**Statute of Limitations**

The legal deadline to file a lawsuit. California generally allows two years for an ordinary personal injury lawsuit, with different rules for some defendants and types of cases. [California Courts filing deadlines](https://www.selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations).

** Tort**

Tort is a civil wrong that causes injury or harm to another person. Separate from criminal acts, torts include negligence, wrongful death, and strict liability. Tort law covers most civil litigation, and the aim is to provide monetary damages as compensation.

** Tortfeasor**

A person or entity who commits a tort. In personal injury cases, the tortfeasor is the party whose negligent or intentional actions caused harm to another person.

## Frequently asked questions about a California injury lawsuit

### Does opening an insurance claim protect the deadline to sue?

An insurer’s claim number and a court case number represent different events. Check when a civil complaint must be filed even if the insurer is reviewing records or discussing settlement. The [California Courts deadline guide](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations) lists the usual injury deadline and explains that different rules apply in cases involving government agencies.

### What if a city vehicle or public property was involved?

Identify the agency, the date of injury, and what the agency owned or operated. A public-entity claim can require an administrative submission before a lawsuit. A police report, maintenance complaint, or phone call to the agency can leave that separate step outstanding. Verify the required recipient, form, deadline, and proof of delivery. [California Courts government claims guide](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/government-claim)

### Do I need all the records before filing?

California Courts explains that you can start a case before you have every piece of proof. Identify the parties, the facts that support your claim, and which records may be withheld or overwritten. Save what you already have and get help with time-sensitive evidence and filing decisions. [California Courts before-filing guide](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/before-sue)

### Can we still settle after a lawsuit is filed?

Yes. Settlement discussions can continue after filing and may occur during discovery or mediation. If an agreement is reached, review who is released, which losses it resolves, and which documents or payments have to be exchanged before the case can be closed.

### What if I already had pain in the area that was injured?

Show the baseline and the change. Earlier records may document your level of pain, treatments, work restrictions, and daily activities before the event. Later notes and records help show what the new event changed. See the [guide to pre-existing injuries after a California crash](/blogs/car-accident-pre-existing-injury-california) for the records to compare.

### What if I was working when the injury occurred?

Tell the reviewer who employed you, what work you were doing, and whether another person or business was involved. Workers’ compensation and a potential third-party claim can involve different procedures and notices. Save incident reports and claim letters for both.
